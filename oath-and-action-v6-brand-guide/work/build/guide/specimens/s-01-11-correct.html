Loop R4a, the map-drawing loop: majorities draw maps that help them keep control.

Texas

In August 2025, Texas’s Republican-led legislature passed a congressional map aimed at five more Republican seats, and Governor Greg Abbott signed it.

California

In August 2025, California’s Democratic-led legislature passed a congressional map aimed at five more Democratic seats, and voters approved it in November.

Our systems atlas records eight new congressional maps passed between censuses in 2025 and 2026. Republican-led legislatures or commissions passed seven, and California’s Democratic-led legislature passed one, which voters approved. Missouri’s voters rejected their state’s map, one of the seven, on November 3, 2026, according to unofficial returns. Virginia’s Democratic-led legislature sought a ninth through a constitutional amendment, and the state’s supreme court voided the statewide vote of April 21, 2026, in which voters approved it.

How we counted

We counted each map by the party that led the legislature or commission when it passed, from each body’s own member lists and minutes. Ohio’s redistricting commission, five Republicans and two Democrats, adopted its map 7 to 0 on October 31, 2025, the deadline the state constitution set, and the constitution required at least two votes from each party’s members. Missouri’s vote came after the snapshot. Its returns, 1,273,815 no and 785,969 yes, stay unofficial until the state certifies the count, after local election authorities file theirs by November 17, 2026. A law sent to Missouri’s voters takes effect only if they approve it, so the state keeps its 2022 districts. A court adopted Utah’s map, so the count leaves it out.
