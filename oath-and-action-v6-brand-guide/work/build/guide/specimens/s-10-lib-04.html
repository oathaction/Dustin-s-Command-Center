Meanings and smallest sizes of the 25 drawings
R‑01 All-party primaries with ranked general elections
Paths from three voters meet at one checked ballot, for a first round open to every voter, whatever their party, followed by a ranked general election.
Smallest size: 48 px
R‑02 Ranked-choice voting for single-winner races
A ballot that ranks three candidates in order stands for voters ranking candidates by preference in a race with one winner.
Smallest size: 48 px
R‑03 Proportional elections in multi-seat House districts
Seat dots fill a half ring, for larger House districts that each elect several members, with seats shared in proportion to votes.
Smallest size: 48 px
R‑04 Independent citizen commissions draw election maps
Three residents before a map split into districts stand for independent citizen commissions that draw election maps.
Smallest size: 48 px
R‑05 Automatic voter registration
A check badge marks an ID card, for registering eligible people to vote when they deal with a state agency, unless they opt out.
Smallest size: 24 px, one color
R‑06 Hold local elections alongside November races
Two calendars joined by an arrow and marked on the same date stand for holding city and county elections on the November ballot with state and national races.
Smallest size: 24 px, one color
R‑07 Public matching for small donations and vouchers
An arrow runs from a short coin stack to a tall one, for public money that multiplies small donations, or vouchers that voters give to candidates.
Smallest size: 48 px
R‑08 Disclose the original funders of political spending
A magnifier sits over a page marked with a dollar sign, for requiring groups that spend on elections to report who paid.
Smallest size: 24 px, one color
R‑09 Ban stock trading by members of Congress
A slash across a stock certificate stands for barring members of Congress from trading individual stocks.
Smallest size: 24 px, one color
R‑10 Limits on lobbying and the revolving door
A figure walks out a door toward an office building past a no-entry sign, for waiting periods before former officials can lobby the offices they left.
Smallest size: 48 px
R‑11 A Federal Election Commission able to act
A gavel lies beside a ballot record with a checked box, for a Federal Election Commission with enough commissioners to enforce campaign-finance law.
Smallest size: 48 px
R‑12 Senate filibuster reform

Smallest size: 48 px
R‑13 Congress’s own experts
Two staff members and an analyst’s report with a rising chart line sit below a chamber in session, for Congress hiring and keeping more of its own analysts and experts.
Smallest size: 48 px
R‑14 Automatic funding to prevent government shutdowns
A funding arrow runs through a budget record and out the other side, for funding that continues at the prior year’s level when Congress misses a budget deadline.

R‑15 Inspectors general with remedies that work
A magnifier, its handle to the lower left, holds a check over a page, for inspectors general, the watchdogs inside federal agencies, with remedies that work when officials obstruct them.

R‑16 Supreme Court 18-year terms and enforceable ethics
A clock hangs under a balance scale beside a check badge, for 18-year terms for Supreme Court justices and an ethics code with a way to enforce it.

R‑17 Protect the career civil service

R‑18 Expiring emergency powers and enforced spending law
A clock sits over the corner of an order, for emergency powers that end unless Congress renews them, and spending law enforced on time.

R‑19 Rules for drug-pricing middlemen (PBMs)

R‑20 Merger enforcement for consumers and workers
