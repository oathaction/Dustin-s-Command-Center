Our systems atlas, snapshot of October 15, 2026: act of Congress, including appropriations and reconciliation, by the house, Senate, President.

Documentedon the public record: the law or rule behind each stage, with the date it was checked

In Congress

  1. 1

    Introduced and sent to committee

    A member sponsors the bill in the House or the Senate, and it goes to a committee for study.

    U.S. House of Representatives, “The Legislative Process”, checked November 11, 2026

  2. 2

    Passed by the first chamber

    A simple majority: 218 of 435 in the House, or 51 of 100 in the Senate.

    U.S. House of Representatives, “The Legislative Process”, checked November 11, 2026

  3. 3

    Senate debate closed

    Most bills need cloture first: three-fifths of the Senate, 60 of 100, vote to end debate.

    U.S. Senate, “About Filibusters and Cloture”, checked November 11, 2026

  4. 4

    In place of step 3

    Budget reconciliation

    A simple majority passes a reconciliation bill, which can carry taxes and spending and leaves out most rule changes.

    Then step 5.

  5. 5

    Passed by the second chamber

    A simple majority, after step 3 in the Senate.

    U.S. House of Representatives, “The Legislative Process”, checked November 11, 2026

  6. 6

    One text for both chambers

    A conference committee of House and Senate members works out the differences, and the bill returns to both chambers for final approval.

    U.S. House of Representatives, “The Legislative Process”, checked November 11, 2026

  7. At the President

  8. 7

    Presented to the President

    The enrolled bill goes to the President, who has 10 days, Sundays excepted, to sign or veto it.

    U.S. House of Representatives, “The Legislative Process”, checked November 11, 2026

  9. 8

    If the President vetoes it

    Veto, and a vote to override

    The President returns it to Congress with objections. A two-thirds vote in each chamber makes it law, at step 10.

    Short of two-thirds in either chamber, the bill dies.

    U.S. Constitution, Article I, Section 7, checked November 11, 2026

  10. 9

    If Congress adjourns first

    Pocket veto

    Congress adjourns before the 10 days end, and the unsigned bill does not become law.

    The bill dies.

    U.S. Constitution, Article I, Section 7, checked November 11, 2026

  11. 10

    Law

    Signed, or left unsigned for 10 days while Congress sits. The Office of the Federal Register gives it a public law number.

    U.S. Constitution, Article I, Section 7, checked November 11, 2026; National Archives, “Public Laws”, checked November 11, 2026

  12. Once it is law

  13. 11

    Funded

    No money leaves the Treasury without an appropriation made by law, so the money comes in a spending act, which takes steps 1 to 10 of its own.

    U.S. Constitution, Article I, Section 9, checked November 11, 2026

  14. 12

    Put to work by agency rules

    The agency publishes a proposed rule, takes comments and publishes the final rule at least 30 days before it takes effect, the steps Rule 11.9 tracks.

    5 U.S.C. 553, checked November 11, 2026

  15. 13

    Before a rule takes effect

    Review by Congress

    The agency reports each rule to Congress, and a major rule waits at least 60 days. A joint resolution of disapproval, passed and signed as at steps 2 to 10, ends it.

    The rule ends, and the agency may not issue it again in substantially the same form.

    5 U.S.C. 801, checked November 11, 2026; 5 U.S.C. 802, checked November 12, 2026

  16. 14

    Once a case is brought

    Review by a court

    A person harmed can sue, and a court can hold an agency’s rule unlawful and set it aside, as arbitrary, capricious or against the law, among other grounds.

    The rule, or the part the court names, is set aside.

    5 U.S.C. 706, checked November 12, 2026

  17. 15

    Repealed, changed or ended by its sunset

    A later act can repeal or change the law, and a sunset date in its own text ends a part of it on that date. Public Law 119-21 struck the end date Public Law 115-97 set for the child tax credit’s rules.

    A repeal, or a new end date, is a new act: back to step 1.

    Public Laws 115-97 and 119-21, govinfo.gov, checked November 11, 2026
