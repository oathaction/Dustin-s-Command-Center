What the six steps mean

  1. 1 Filed. A legislator files the bill, and it goes to a committee.
  2. 2 Hearing. The committee holds a hearing and takes testimony on the bill. Many state legislatures take testimony from the public there. In Congress, the committee hears the witnesses it invites. A bill’s own page says how to take part.
  3. 3 Committee vote. The committee votes on whether to send the bill to the full House or Senate.
  4. 4 First chamber. The full House or Senate, wherever the bill was filed, votes on it.
  5. 5 Second chamber. The other chamber takes the bill up. Its own committee can hold a hearing first, and then the full chamber votes and both chambers agree on one text. Nebraska has one chamber, so a Nebraska bill keeps this step under a slash.
  6. 6 Governor. The governor signs or vetoes the bill, and lawmakers can vote to override a veto.
