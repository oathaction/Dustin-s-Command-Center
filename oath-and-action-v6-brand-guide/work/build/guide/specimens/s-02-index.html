How Oath & Action writes

These 21 rules teach writing that sounds like a person talking, and each one shows a sentence before and after the fix. Twelve house rules for every page come with them.

Our founder and editor, Dustin Cathcart, set these rules for everything Oath & Action publishes. Sixteen of the 21 rules quote our own work and rewrite the line to pass every rule on the page. Where we found none, we wrote one, and anything we invented carries the Illustrative label.

Every rule in this chapter appears below by group, and each name leads to its worked example. Headings, labels, captions and chart keys may be phrases; every other line on the page is a full sentence.

01Sound like a person talking

  1. W01Write it the way you would say it
  2. W02Name who acts
  3. W03Change the length of your sentences
  4. W04Join thoughts that belong together

02Put the point first and stop

  1. W05Open with something the reader can use
  2. W06Give the point without a reveal
  3. W07Stop when the facts stop
  4. W08Close without a slogan

03Say what is so

  1. W09Say what a thing is
  2. W10Drop the matched pair
  3. W11Skip the run of negatives
  4. W12List what the facts hold
  5. W13Give each sentence its own shape

04Cut the padding

  1. W14Pull stacked nouns apart
  2. W15Use the verb inside the noun
  3. W16Drop the hedges and keep the grade
  4. W17Cut the intensifiers
  5. W18Use everyday verbs
  6. W19Let the facts carry the feeling
  7. W20Size each word to its evidence

05Marks and numbers

  1. W21Leave out the em dash
  2. H05Write dates in words
  3. H06Spell out numbers under ten
  4. H07Use the true minus sign

HHouse rules for every page

  1. H01Quote the locked lines exactly
  2. H02Explain a term the first time you use it
  3. H03Label what is invented beside it
  4. H04Source every public policy statistic
  5. H08Let a title claim only what its evidence holds
  6. H09Keep a period whole
  7. H10Name parties in words
  8. H11Say what an ID refers to
  9. H12Use the neutral name for a contested policy
  10. 10Before you publish: ten questions
