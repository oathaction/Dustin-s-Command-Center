1. iPhone with VoiceOver

    1. Open Settings, then Accessibility, then Accessibility Shortcut, and choose VoiceOver. From now on, pressing the side button three times turns VoiceOver on and off.
    2. Open the page in Safari, then press the side button three times. VoiceOver says “VoiceOver on”.
    3. Swipe right with one finger to move to the next item, and left to go back. Each item is read aloud.
    4. Double-tap anywhere to press the item VoiceOver is on. To type, double-tap a field, type, then swipe on.
    5. Go through the ten things to listen for below, and write Pass or Fail beside each with what you heard.
    6. Press the side button three times to turn VoiceOver off.

  2. Android phone with TalkBack

    1. Open Settings, then Accessibility, then TalkBack, and turn on the TalkBack shortcut. Makers name these menus differently; the shortcut is usually holding both volume keys for three seconds.
    2. Open the page in Chrome, then hold both volume keys for three seconds. TalkBack says it is on.
    3. Swipe right with one finger to move to the next item, and left to go back. Scroll with two fingers.
    4. Double-tap anywhere to press the item TalkBack is on. To type, double-tap a field, type, then swipe on.
    5. Go through the ten things to listen for below, and write Pass or Fail beside each with what you heard. Gestures differ between TalkBack versions; Google’s TalkBack help lists the ones on your phone.
    6. Hold both volume keys for three seconds to turn TalkBack off.

  3. Windows PC with NVDA and a contrast theme

    1. Download NVDA, a free screen reader, from nvaccess.org and install it. Start it from the Start menu; it says “NVDA started”.
    2. Open the page in Chrome, Edge or Firefox. Press Tab to move from control to control, and Shift and Tab to go back. Press Enter or Space to press a control. Press Control to stop the speech.
    3. Press H to jump from heading to heading, and D from landmark to landmark.
    4. Go through the ten things to listen for below, and write Pass or Fail beside each with what you heard. Press Insert and Q to quit NVDA.
    5. Now turn on a contrast theme: on Windows 11, open Settings, then Accessibility, then Contrast themes, choose Night sky and press Apply. On Windows 10, open Settings, then Ease of Access, then High contrast.
    6. Reload the page and look: every button keeps an edge, the focus ring shows on each Tab stop, a pressed chip keeps its check, an error keeps its bar, and the warning and success marks stay visible. Write down any that vanish, then set the theme back to None.

  4. Mac with VoiceOver

    1. In Safari, open Settings, then Advanced, and turn on “Press Tab to highlight each item on a webpage”.
    2. Press Command and F5 to turn VoiceOver on. It says “VoiceOver on Safari”.
    3. Press Tab to move from control to control, and Shift and Tab to go back. Press Control, Option and the Right Arrow to read the next item, and Control, Option and Space to press it.
    4. Press Control, Option and U to open the rotor, a list of the page’s headings, links and landmarks. The Left and Right Arrows switch lists, the Up and Down Arrows choose, and Return goes there.
    5. Go through the ten things to listen for below, and write Pass or Fail beside each with what you heard.
    6. Press Command and F5 to turn VoiceOver off.

Ten things to listen for: a pass sounds like this

  1. The page’s title is read as the page opens.
  2. The first stop is “Skip to main content”.
  3. Headings come in order: the chapter, then each rule.
  4. Each control says its words and what kind of control it is, and a pressed, checked or current one says so.
  5. A button that waits says it is unavailable and why.
  6. The phone menu takes you inside when it opens, and Close the menu, or Escape on a keyboard, brings you back to Menu.
  7. Typing in a search field, you hear how many results there are, and the cursor stays in the field.
  8. Pressing Copy, you hear what was copied.
  9. Sending a form with a mistake takes you to the field to fix, and you hear the error.
  10. Nothing you cannot see is read, and nothing you cannot see takes focus.

Write down the device, its system version, the browser and its version, the screen reader’s version, the date, and each step’s result with what you heard. A fail with its words is as useful as a pass.
