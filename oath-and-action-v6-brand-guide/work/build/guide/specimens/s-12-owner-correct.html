Who owns the next step?

A federal records request, from your letter to an appeal

  1. 1

    You

    Send a written request that describes the records you want to the agency’s records office.

    ↓ Next step: The agency’s records office. Twenty working days to decide whether to release the records, from the day the request arrives.

  2. 2

    The agency’s records office

    Confirms the request with a tracking number, searches for the records, reviews them, blacking out what an exemption protects, and sends a written answer with the records it can release.

    ↓ Next step: You. At least 90 days to appeal a refusal or a partial answer.

  3. 3

    You

    If you appeal, write to the agency’s appeal office.

    ↓ Next step: The agency’s appeal office. Twenty working days to decide the appeal.

  4. 4

    The agency’s appeal office

    Reviews the answer and decides whether to release more.

    ↓ Next step: You, again. Ask the National Archives’ records ombudsman to mediate, or sue in federal court.

Source: 5 U.S.C. 552, the Freedom of Information Act, sections (a)(6)(A), (h)(3) and (a)(4)(B), and FOIA.gov, “Frequently Asked Questions”, Office of Information Policy, U.S. Department of Justice, both retrieved November 9, 2026. Federal agencies only; states set their own records laws.
