Documented

After the 2020 election, Donald J. Trump’s presidential campaign asked a federal appeals court to block Pennsylvania’s certified vote totals from taking effect. The court refused on November 27, 2020, noting that the campaign “never alleges that any ballot was fraudulent or cast by an illegal voter.” A July 2022 review titled Lost, Not Stolen, written by eight former officials and lawyers, among them two former U.S. senators and three former federal appeals judges, counted 64 cases it says Donald Trump and his supporters brought in six battleground states. It found that they “failed to produce substantive evidence to make their case,” and it records one case they won, in Pennsylvania, on far too few votes to change the result.

Sources: U.S. Court of Appeals for the Third Circuit, Donald J. Trump for President, Inc. v. Secretary Commonwealth of Pennsylvania, No. 20-3371, opinion filed November 27, 2020, pages 9 and 21, via govinfo.gov; John Danforth and seven co-authors, Lost, Not Stolen: The Conservative Case that Trump Lost and Biden Won the 2020 Presidential Election, July 2022, printed pages 1 and 3, lostnotstolen.org; the 2020 presidential election in Arizona, Georgia, Michigan, Nevada, Pennsylvania and Wisconsin. Retrieved November 7, 2026. The court issued the opinion as not precedential. The count of 64 cases is the review’s own, and its authors say each of them “has worked in Republican politics, been appointed to office by Republicans, or is otherwise associated with the Party.”
