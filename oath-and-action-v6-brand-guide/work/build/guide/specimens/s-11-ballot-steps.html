StepWhat happensWhere the law sets it
1 FiledThe people behind the measure file its full text with the official the state’s law names, in some states with the first signatures.Massachusetts Constitution, Amendment Article XLVIII, The Initiative, II, Section 3; California Elections Code 9001
2 Title and summaryThe attorney general checks the measure’s form and subject and writes the title and summary that petitions and ballots carry. No petition goes out before then.Massachusetts, Article XLVIII, The Initiative, II, Section 3; California Elections Code 9004 and 9014
3 SignaturesThe measure’s backers gather signatures and file them by the deadline, and election officials count and check them. With too few valid signatures, the measure does not qualify.Massachusetts, Article XLVIII, The Initiative, II, Section 3; California Elections Code 9030 and 9031
4 LegislatureWhere the initiative is indirect, the Legislature votes on the measure as filed, and if it does not pass it, a second round of signatures sends it on. A direct initiative goes to the ballot with no vote here, so it keeps this step under a slash.Massachusetts, Amendment Article LXXXI, Section 2; Ohio Constitution, Article II, Section 1b; Michigan Constitution, Article II, Section 9
5 On the ballotThe state’s election officer certifies that the measure will appear on the ballot.California Elections Code 9033; Massachusetts, Amendment Article LXXXI, Section 2
6 VoteThe voters decide. The count stays unofficial until the state certifies it, and a measure that passed takes effect on the date its state’s law sets, such as 30 days after the election.California Constitution, Article II, Section 10(a); Massachusetts, Amendment Article LXXXI, Section 2; Ohio, Article II, Section 1b; Michigan, Article II, Section 9
