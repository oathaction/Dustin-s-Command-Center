StepWhat happens, and the law that sets it
1 Filed

The people behind the measure file its full text with the official the state’s law names, in some states with the first signatures.

Law: Massachusetts Constitution, Amendment Article XLVIII, The Initiative, II, Section 3; California Elections Code 9001

2 Title and summary

The attorney general checks the measure’s form and subject and writes the title and summary that petitions and ballots carry. No petition goes out before then.

Law: Massachusetts, Article XLVIII, The Initiative, II, Section 3; California Elections Code 9004 and 9014

3 Signatures

The measure’s backers gather signatures and file them by the deadline, and election officials count and check them. With too few valid signatures, the measure does not qualify.

Law: Massachusetts, Article XLVIII, The Initiative, II, Section 3; California Elections Code 9030 and 9031

4 Legislature

Where the initiative is indirect, the Legislature votes on the measure as filed, and if it does not pass it, a second round of signatures sends it on. A direct initiative goes to the ballot with no vote here, so it keeps this step under a slash.

Law: Massachusetts, Amendment Article LXXXI, Section 2; Ohio Constitution, Article II, Section 1b; Michigan Constitution, Article II, Section 9

5 On the ballot

The state’s election officer certifies that the measure will appear on the ballot.

Law: California Elections Code 9033; Massachusetts, Amendment Article LXXXI, Section 2

6 Vote

The voters decide. The count stays unofficial until the state certifies it, and a measure that passed takes effect on the date its state’s law sets, such as 30 days after the election.

Law: California Constitution, Article II, Section 10(a); Massachusetts, Amendment Article LXXXI, Section 2; Ohio, Article II, Section 1b; Michigan, Article II, Section 9
