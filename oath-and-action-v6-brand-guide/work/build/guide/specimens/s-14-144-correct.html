    The atlas’s evidence and tradeoffs for Reform R-06

    The evidence for the remedy, well supported: “Using California voter files, Hajnal, Kogan and Markarian (2022) found that on-cycle timing roughly doubles turnout and makes the local electorate more representative. A school-district study by Kogan, Lavertu and Peskowitz (2018) found that off-cycle timing shifted the electorate and raised teacher pay relative to on-cycle districts, which links timing to policy. Main limitation: effects on housing and other policy outcomes are plausible but less established than the turnout effect.”

    Its tradeoffs: “Ballots get longer. Local races can be lost among national ones, and some voters skip them.”

    The atlas’s evidence and tradeoffs for Reform R-30

    The evidence for the remedy, plausible: “Where zoning limits bind, allowing more homes raises building and lowers rents over time. Auckland, New Zealand’s 2016 upzoning roughly doubled permits in upzoned areas, and six years later three-bedroom rents were 22% to 35% below a modeled comparison. Minneapolis is also cited, though a 2025 Federal Reserve Bank of Minneapolis analysis attributes much of that city’s slower rent growth to a drop in demand in 2020. Main limitation: results depend on whether zoning was the binding limit, and infrastructure and construction costs can still block delivery.”

    Its tradeoffs: “Owners in high-demand places give up some price growth and control, and neighborhoods change.”
