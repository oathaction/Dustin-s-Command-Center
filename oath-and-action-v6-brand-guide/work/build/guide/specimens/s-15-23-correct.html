Five trackers, each beside its workflow

Each row puts a tracker’s step beside the stages of the law behind it. A step can hold several stages, several steps can share one, and some stages sit before step 1 or after step 6, where the tracker does not follow them.

The Freedom of Information Act beside the records request tracker of Rule 11.8

A federal request’s steps in the law, behind the tracker’s six steps

  1. Before step 1

    • Look first: check whether the agency already posts the records

  2. 1 Requested

    • Write the request
    • Say what fees you will pay
    • Ask for faster handling, where the requester shows a need

  3. 2 Acknowledged

    • The agency decides, within 20 working days that start here

  4. 3 Searching

    • The agency decides, inside the same 20 working days

  5. 4 Reviewing

    • The agency decides, inside the same 20 working days

  6. 5 Answered

    • The agency decides within 20 working days, or 10 more in unusual circumstances

  7. 6 Appeal

    • Appeal a denial, with at least 90 days to do so

  8. After step 6

    • Ask for mediation
    • Go to court

Stages from 5 U.S.C. 552 and FOIA.gov, read November 12, 2026.

Federal rule making beside the agency rule tracker of Rule 11.9

The law’s steps for a federal rule, behind the tracker’s six steps

  1. Before step 1

    • Anyone petitions for a change

  2. 1 Early notice

    • Agency drafts a rule. The law we read sets no early notice; an agency may give one while it drafts

  3. 2 Proposed

    • White House review of a significant draft
    • Proposed rule published

  4. 3 Comments open

    • Public comment opens

  5. 4 Comments closed

    • Public comment closes

  6. In place of steps 2 to 4

    • Guidance without comment

  7. 5 Final rule

    • White House review of a significant draft, again
    • Final rule published

  8. 6 In effect

    • Report to Congress; a major rule takes effect at least 60 days later

  9. After step 6

    • Congress disapproves
    • A court reviews the rule

Stages from 5 U.S.C. 551, 553, 701 to 706, 801 and 802 and Executive Order 12866, read November 12, 2026.

Route BI beside the ballot measure tracker of Rule 11.10

A citizen initiative’s stages in four states’ constitutions and California’s guide, behind the tracker’s six steps

  1. 1 Filed

    • The full text filed with the attorney general or the secretary of state

  2. 2 Title and summary

    • The attorney general’s title and summary, before anyone signs

  3. 3 Signatures

    • Signatures counted and checked

  4. 4 Legislature

    • In an indirect initiative, the Legislature’s vote, and a second round of signatures if it does not pass the measure
    • In a direct initiative, a hearing that cannot stop it
    • A referral, in which the Legislature sends a measure to the ballot

  5. 5 On the ballot

    • Certified for the ballot
    • A court challenge before the vote, where a state allows one

  6. 6 Vote

    • The vote, the certified result, and the day it takes effect

  7. After step 6

    • Amended or repealed later, as each state allows

Stages from the constitutions of Massachusetts, Ohio and Michigan and the California Secretary of State’s guide, read November 14, 2026.

Route CRT beside the court case tracker of Rule 11.11

A federal civil case’s stages in Title 28, the courts’ rules and the Supreme Court’s rules, behind the tracker’s six steps

  1. 1 Filed

    • A complaint filed in a district court

  2. 2 Trial court ruling

    • Motions, discovery, a settlement or a trial, and the judgment
    • An order on an injunction, which can be appealed at once

  3. 3 Appeal

    • A notice of appeal, within 30 days, or 60 where the United States is a party

  4. 4 Appeals court ruling

    • A panel of three judges decides, and the full court may rehear it

  5. 5 Supreme Court petition

    • A petition within 90 days, which the Court grants or denies
    • An appeal straight from three district judges, in the cases the law names

  6. 6 Supreme Court ruling

    • Briefs, argument and the Court’s decision

  7. After step 6

    • A later ruling that replaces earlier doctrine

Stages from 28 U.S.C., the Federal Rules of Civil and Appellate Procedure and the Rules of the Supreme Court, read November 12 and 14, 2026.
