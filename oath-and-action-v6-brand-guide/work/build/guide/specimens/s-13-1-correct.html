FindingDocumented: a Census Bureau survey

Child poverty stood at 13.4 percent in 2025 under the Supplemental Poverty Measure

Children under 18 below the poverty line, Supplemental Poverty Measure, percent, United States, 2009 to 2025

The Supplemental Poverty Measure counts taxes, tax credits and benefits such as food aid, and subtracts costs such as medical expenses. The survey’s margin of error for 2025 is 0.6 points at 90 percent confidence.

Source: U.S. Census Bureau, Poverty in the United States: 2025 (P60-290), Table 8, Supplemental Poverty Measure, children under 18, 2009 to 2025, United States. Retrieved November 6, 2026. Figures for 2019 to 2024 include the Bureau’s August 2026 correction to the poverty thresholds, and 2024 also takes new population weights; they differ by up to 0.4 points from the earlier release.

Posted wages in a local job market at the 75th percentile of employer concentration against one at the 25th, United States, 2010 to 2013

EstimateWell supported, as our systems atlas grades this delivery case

Vehicle safety technologies saved an estimated 614,000 lives from 1960 to 2012

Lives saved by vehicle safety technologies and the federal safety standards behind them, United States, 1960 to 2012

Method: the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration removes each technology from its crash records, using that technology’s measured effect, and counts the deaths that would have followed. The agency’s figure is 613,501, and it calls the result “a hypothetical estimate.” Its crash records begin in 1975, so earlier years are extrapolated.

Source: National Highway Traffic Safety Administration, Lives Saved by Vehicle Safety Technologies and Associated Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standards, 1960 to 2012 (DOT HS 812 069), United States, as recorded in our systems atlas’s delivery log, delivery case DL06, snapshot of October 15, 2026. The estimate assumes each technology’s effect held steady for five decades.
