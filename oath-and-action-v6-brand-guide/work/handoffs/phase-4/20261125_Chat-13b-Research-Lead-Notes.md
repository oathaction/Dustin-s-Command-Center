So a chart image made 1,456 px wide draws no wider than 728 CSS px on a desktop window and 358 CSS px on a 390 px phone, a scale of 0.492 between the two. Type drawn into the image shrinks by that factor on a phone: a label meant to read at 14 px at 728 reads at 6.9 px at 358.

So a full-width image is read from the stylesheet, not measured on a post (listed under Not tested). The ratio rule above still holds: up to 1:1.

### F7. The law on a commercial email's postal address (DR-343)

- The statute, 15 U.S.C. 7704(a)(5)(A)(iii), read today at govinfo.gov (USCODE-2023 edition, 00:19:07 UTC) and at Cornell's Legal Information Institute (00:18:48 UTC), same words: "It is unlawful for any person to initiate the transmission of any commercial electronic mail message to a protected computer unless the message provides ... (iii) a valid physical postal address of the sender." Source law: CAN-SPAM Act of 2003, Pub. L. 108–187, §5, 117 Stat. 2706.
- What counts, 16 CFR 316.2(p), read today through eCFR's own interface (https://www.ecfr.gov/api/renderer/v1/content/enhanced/current/title-16?part=316, 00:19:07 UTC; the eCFR page itself sent the reader to a bot check at unblock.federalregister.gov) and at Cornell (https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/316.2, 00:18:49 UTC): "'Valid physical postal address' means the sender's current street address, a Post Office box the sender has accurately registered with the United States Postal Service, or a private mailbox the sender has accurately registered with a commercial mail receiving agency that is established pursuant to United States Postal Service regulations." (Added at 73 FR 29677, May 2008.)
- Who the sender is, 15 U.S.C. 7702(16)(A) (govinfo, 00:19:07 UTC): "a person who initiates such a message and whose product, service, or Internet web site is advertised or promoted by the message."
- When it applies, 15 U.S.C. 7702(2)(A): a commercial message is one "the primary purpose of which is the commercial advertisement or promotion of a commercial product or service"; 16 CFR 316.3 sets the primary-purpose tests (subject line, and what a reader of the body "would likely conclude"). The guide does not rule on whether a given Oath & Action email is commercial. DR-343 prints the address slot in every case, and Substack prints a mailing address on all its emails anyway (F1).
- For the slot's words: the footer carries one postal address of the sender: a street address, a registered PO box or a registered private mailbox. A PO box passes only when registered to the sender with the Postal Service. Print it as a labeled Illustrative slot (DR-302, DR-343). This is the text of the law, not legal advice.
