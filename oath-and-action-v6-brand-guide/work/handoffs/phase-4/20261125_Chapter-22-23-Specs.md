## 0. Rules for every application in chapters 22 and 23

- **Tokens only.** Every color is a `--oa-...` token from `work/build/tokens/tokens.css`. The working email is the one exception in form, never in value: mail apps read no custom properties, so the email renderer writes each color as the hex value `lib.token_value()` reads from the token at build, and no hex is typed in a renderer or a content file. Every space is a step of the spacing scale (Rule 07.1) unless a row here names a measured exception and its reason.
- **Faces.** Images (every PNG) set Inter Tight 800 and Inter 400, 600 and 700 from the guide's fonts (Rule 06.10), drawn into the pixels at export. The working email's live text sets the system stack and nothing else (Rule 06.9): `oa.font.family.emailSans` (Helvetica, Arial, sans-serif) for every word, `oa.font.family.emailQuote` (Georgia, serif, italic) for a quotation. No web font loads in the email.
- **Type sizes.** Rule 06.2 for every artboard: up to 600 px wide, each style's phone floor; from 1,200 px, its desktop ceiling; between, either column. A picture a platform shrinks for a phone keeps every word at 13 px or more at the size a phone draws it (Rule 06.3): the floor in the artboard's own pixels is 13 times the artboard's width over the width a 390 px phone draws it, rounded up. Each section gives the figure. Social cards keep Rule 14.141's 36 px floor at 1,080 px.
- **Identity masters.** Every mark from its master file through `lib.mark()`, its paints set by token (Rule 08.2's dark rule on Charcoal: the lockmark's links take the nodes' color). Never typed in a font. Ampersand B in a hinge headline on Newsprint takes DR-330's keyline through `renderers/hinge-keyline.mjs` (Engineer A's item 2): one pixel of the artboard as exported at 1x, two in an export made at twice size, so a reader always meets 1 px. On White and Charcoal it takes none. B in the wordmark, the lockmark, the avatars and the favicon never takes one.
- **The avatar** is ampersand B alone on the Charcoal tile (DR-116, Rule 04.7): B 58 percent of the tile's height, uploaded as a full-bleed square at 1,080 px. It is the Substack logo too (section 2.1). L8 never serves as an avatar (Rule 03.7).
- **Sign-off.** Every social card signs off with the lockmark's 48 px master drawn at exactly 96 px, bottom right inside the margin, on whole pixels (DR-211, Rule 08.2). A cover keeps 48 px. An image inside a Substack post or an email is a figure, not a card, and carries no sign-off.
- **Red.** One working red a view (Rule 05.3): an error, then the step a reader can take now, then the one point the headline names. The wordmark's ampersand and the lockmark's lever are signature red and do not count. Mark every red element `data-red="working"` or `data-red="signature"` with `data-red-job` on the working one. Red never sits on Prussian.
- **Rule 05.1 on every export.** Newsprint 60 to 85 percent of each view, one dark panel a section at most, Pillar Red within 5 percent. A long view (a post, the email, a profile page) is measured on its first screen and on each section's opening screen (DR-310, DR-312). The checks measure each export's own pixels, with the share of each named here as "Shares to expect" where I measured a prototype.
- **Web address.** None on a social card or a post image (DR-55, DR-308): the address is not settled. Where a profile or the email needs one, it prints "oathandaction.org" as plain text with the Illustrative label beside it (DR-308), never as a link the kit makes live.
- **Handles and the Substack address** print as words with the Illustrative label beside them (DR-302): "@oathandaction" on X, Threads and Instagram, and "oathandaction.substack.com" for Substack, until our founder and editor names them.
- **No platform mark.** No logo, glyph or icon of Substack, X, Threads or Instagram is drawn (D4-21). A profile or a post is shown as a plain frame drawn from our tokens, with the platform named in words in the plate's head. A frame that stands for a platform's own screen draws its parts as neutral boxes in `--oa-color-border-hairline` and `--oa-color-text-secondary`, labeled in words ("The platform's buttons"), so no reader takes it for the platform's design.
- **Illustrative content.** Every invented person, place, quote or figure carries the Illustrative label beside it (Rule 13.8). The postal address, the merge fields and the handles are the only invented items DD-1 and DD-2 expect.
- **Overlays.** Every social, Substack and email export carries a safe-area or crop overlay a reader switches on, as A01 and A21 do (`lib.overlay()`, the plate's "Show the safe areas" switch, off at first, by keyboard with scripts off). Each section names its layers.
- **Exports.** Named by the studio's rule, `YYYYMMDD_Title-Case-Words.ext`, with the size in the name; listed in the dated exports manifest with the platform row each one meets; checked by `check-dimensions.py` against `work/research/20261121_Platform-Specifications.json`.
- **Frames in the guide.** Each frame is a picture named by its alt, `inert` and `aria-hidden="true"` inside, as chapter 20's page frames are (contract C4). The export files are the working pieces.

## 6. The working HTML email (DR-343; Rules 03.6, 06.9, 08.2, 02.24)

One complete email for any sender other than Substack, sender-neutral, built for mail apps: one HTML file and its plain-text part. The guide shows it at 600 and 375 px, in the light and the dark treatment and with images off; the export is the file itself.

### 6.1 Build constraints

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| File | One HTML document, `<!DOCTYPE html>`, `<html lang="en" dir="ltr">`, UTF-8, `<title>` the subject line (words: the editor's). Its bytes stay under 40,000, well under the 102,000 at which Gmail clips a message (the research lead's figure, Substack's statement about Gmail), and the build prints the count |
| Layout | Nested tables with `role="presentation"` on every layout table, `cellpadding="0" cellspacing="0" border="0"`. An outer table 100 percent wide holds the container; the container is `width:100%;max-width:600px`, centered, with a fixed 600 px table inside `<!--[if mso]>` comments for the Outlook desktop apps. No `div` carries layout, no flex, no grid, no positioning |
| Styles | Inline on every element. One `<style>` block in the head holds only what cannot be inline: the phone query (`@media (max-width:480px)`), the dark treatment (6.5) and the link reset; the email reads correctly when a mail app strips that block |
| Widths | Content 552 px at 600 (24 px of padding each side); under 480 px wide, 16 px of padding, so 343 px of content at 375. A mail app that ignores the query keeps 24 px (327 px at 375), which still holds |
| Images | PNG, each with `width` and `height` attributes at its CSS size, `style="display:block;width:100%;max-width:Npx;height:auto;border:0"`, alt text on every one (empty only for the two decorations 6.3 names), hosted beside the file in the export and named in a slot (6.6) for the sender's own host. Each is drawn at twice its CSS size. No image carries words a reader needs that the email does not also print as live text |
| Type | The system stack on every text element, inline (Rule 06.9). Sizes from the phone floor: headline 26 px, body 17 px on 27 px lines, body small 15 px, caption 14 px, label 13 px capitals with 0.06 em tracking, quotation 24 px. Nothing under 13 px |
| Links | Words that say where they go (no "click here"); in running text a 1 px underline in the text's ink; each link a slot URL (6.6). A link that stands alone, and each footer link, gets a 44 px tall tap box from its cell's padding |
| Head | `<meta charset="utf-8">`, `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1">`, `<meta name="color-scheme" content="light dark">`, `<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light dark">`, `<meta name="x-apple-disable-message-reformatting">`, the title |
| Plain-text part | A `.txt` file of the same words in the same order, wrapped at 72 characters, links written out after their words in brackets, images given as their alt text in square brackets, the slots as in 6.6. Built from the same word fields as the HTML, never typed twice |

### 6.5 The dark treatment

Written in the head's `<style>` under `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)`, with the same rules repeated under `[data-ogsc]` for Outlook on the web, so mail apps that honor a dark request get our own dark view, not an inverted one: Newsprint grounds become Charcoal; primary text Newsprint; secondary text Dim Newsprint; hairlines stay Stone; the masthead and footer keep Charcoal and take a 1 px Stone line along their inner edge so each still reads as a panel (as chapter 20's dark theme does); the fact panel keeps Charcoal and takes a 1 px Stone edge; the ask keeps Pillar Red with its White label and drops the keyline (4.49 to 1 against Charcoal); images with a light ground swap to their dark twins by a pair of classes (`.oa-l` shown, `.oa-d` hidden, flipped in the query). Every image with a ground of its own is opaque, so an app that inverts colors without asking (some Gmail apps) leaves each picture readable on its own ground. The accessibility lead's rulings govern; where they differ from this paragraph, theirs stand and I revise.
