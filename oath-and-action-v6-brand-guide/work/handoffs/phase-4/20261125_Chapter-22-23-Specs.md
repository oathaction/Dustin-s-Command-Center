## 0. Rules for every application in chapters 22 and 23

- **Tokens only.** Every color is a `--oa-...` token from `work/build/tokens/tokens.css`. The working email is the one exception in form, never in value: mail apps read no custom properties, so the email renderer writes each color as the hex value `lib.token_value()` reads from the token at build, and no hex is typed in a renderer or a content file. Every space is a step of the spacing scale (Rule 07.1) unless a row here names a measured exception and its reason.
- **Faces.** Images (every PNG) set Inter Tight 800 and Inter 400, 600 and 700 from the guide's fonts (Rule 06.10), drawn into the pixels at export. The working email's live text sets the system stack and nothing else (Rule 06.9, AL-33): `oa.font.family.emailSans` (Helvetica, Arial, sans-serif) for every word, `oa.font.family.emailQuote` (Georgia, serif, italic) for a quotation. No web font loads in the email.
- **Type sizes.** Rule 06.2 for every artboard: up to 600 px wide, each style's phone floor; from 1,200 px, its desktop ceiling; between, either column. A picture a platform shrinks for a phone keeps every word at 13 px or more at the size a phone draws it (Rule 06.3, AL-34, AL-42). Social exports keep AL-47's floor: 36 px on a 1,080 px export, scaled by width (40 px at 1,200, 53.3 at 1,600), and set a hinge headline at AL-47's large size or more (56 px bold at 1,080, 62.2 at 1,200, 82.9 at 1,600), so B passes as large text (AL-48).
- **Identity masters.** Every mark from its master file through `lib.mark()`, its paints set by token (Rule 08.2's dark rule on Charcoal: the lockmark's links take the nodes' color). Never typed in a font. Ampersand B in a hinge headline on Newsprint takes DR-330's keyline through `renderers/hinge-keyline.mjs` (Engineer A's item 2), one pixel of the artboard as exported at 1x and two in an export made at twice size, so a reader always meets 1 px. On White and Charcoal it takes none. B in the wordmark, the lockmark, the avatar and the favicon never takes one.
- **The avatar** is ampersand B alone on the Charcoal tile (DR-116, Rule 04.7), drawn as `favicon-set.mjs`'s `app_icon()` draws the Apple touch icon: B from its display master, 58 percent of the tile's height, centered, in signature red on `--oa-component-app-icon-tile`. Uploaded as a full-bleed square at 1,080 px. The same file is the Substack logo (section 2.1). L8 never serves as an avatar (Rule 03.7).
- **Sign-off.** Every social card signs off with the lockmark's 48 px master drawn at exactly 96 px, bottom right inside the margin, on whole pixels (DR-211, Rule 08.2). An image inside a Substack post or an email is a figure, not a card, and carries no sign-off.
- **Red.** One working red a view (Rule 05.3): an error, then the step a reader can take now, then the one point the headline names. The wordmark's ampersand and the lockmark's lever are signature red and do not count. Mark every red element `data-red="working"` or `data-red="signature"`, with `data-red-job` on the working one. Red never sits on Prussian.
- **Rule 05.1 on every export.** Newsprint 60 to 85 percent of each view, one dark panel a section at most, Pillar Red within 5 percent. A long view (a post, the email, a profile) is measured on its first screen and on each section's opening screen (DR-310, DR-312). "Shares to expect" give my estimate by area; the checks measure the pixels.
- **Web address.** None on a social card or a post image (DR-55, DR-308). Where a profile or the email prints one, it is "oathandaction.org" as plain text with the Illustrative label beside it.
- **Handles and the Substack address** print as words with the Illustrative label beside them (DR-302): "@oathandaction" on X, Threads and Instagram (the handle the studio's earlier boards used), and "oathandaction.substack.com" for Substack, until our founder and editor names them.
- **No platform mark.** No logo, glyph or icon of Substack, X, Threads or Instagram is drawn (D4-21). A frame that stands for a platform's screen draws the platform's own parts (its buttons, menus and byline) as plain boxes with a 1 px `--oa-color-border-hairline` edge and a label in words, such as "Substack's buttons", in the caption style and the secondary ink. No reader takes such a frame for the platform's design, and the plate's head names the platform in words.
- **Our name on a platform.** Wherever a platform lets us set a picture in place of typed text, our name is drawn from the masters (Rule 03.1): the wordmark in Substack's header, S1 on the welcome page. Where a platform types the name itself (an email's sender, a footer's copyright line), we cannot stop it, and the rule says so.
- **Illustrative content.** Every invented person, place, quote or figure carries the Illustrative label beside it (Rule 13.8). The postal address, the merge fields and the handles are the only invented items chapters 22 and 23 expect.
- **Overlays.** Every social, Substack and email export carries a safe-area or crop overlay a reader switches on, as A01 and A21 do (`lib.overlay()`, the plate's "Show the safe areas" switch, off at first, by keyboard with scripts off). No export file carries its overlay (AL-51). Each section names its layers.
- **Exports.** Named by the studio's rule, `YYYYMMDD_Title-Case-Words.ext`, with the size in the name; listed in the dated exports manifest with the platform row each one meets; checked by `check-dimensions.py` against `work/research/20261121_Platform-Specifications.json`.
- **Frames in the guide.** Each frame is a picture named by its alt (application, view, width, light or dark, images on or off), `inert` and `aria-hidden="true"` inside, as contract C4 draws chapter 20's (AL-51). A frame that shows a platform's page is the studio's drawing from measures, and its plate line says which parts were measured.

## 6. The working HTML email (A29; DR-343; Rules 03.6, 06.9, 08.2, 02.24; AL-25 to AL-37)

One complete email for any sender other than Substack, sender-neutral, built for mail apps: one HTML file and its plain-text part. The guide shows it at 600 and 375 px, in the light and the dark treatment and with images off; the export is the file itself.

### 6.1 Build constraints

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| File | One HTML document, `<!DOCTYPE html>`, `<html lang="en" dir="ltr">`, with `lang="en" dir="ltr"` again on the outermost wrapper inside `<body>` (AL-26); UTF-8; `<title>` the subject line, word for word (the editor's words). Its bytes stay under 40,000, well under the 102,000 at which Gmail clips a message (F3, Substack's statement about Gmail), and the build prints the count |
| Layout | Nested tables, `role="presentation"` on every layout table, `cellpadding="0" cellspacing="0" border="0"`, no `th`, `caption`, `summary`, `scope` or `headers` in them (AL-27). An outer table 100 percent wide holds the container; the container is `width:100%;max-width:600px`, centered, with a fixed 600 px table inside `<!--[if mso]>` comments for the Outlook desktop apps. No flex, grid or positioning. Source order is reading order; nothing stacks out of it at 375 px |
| Styles | Inline on every element; each text element carries its own color, background and font stack (AL-30 c). | Widths | Content 552 px at 600 (24 px of padding each side); under 480 px wide, 16 px of padding, so 343 px of content at 375. An app that ignores the query keeps 24 px (327 px at 375) |

| Type | The system stack on every text element, inline (Rule 06.9, AL-33). ### 6.5 The dark treatment (AL-30)

Written in the head's `<style>` under `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)`, with the same rules repeated under `[data-ogsc]` for Outlook on the web: Newsprint grounds become Charcoal; primary text Newsprint (13.29 to 1); secondary text Dim Newsprint (7.26 to 1); links Newsprint and underlined; hairlines stay Stone; the masthead and the footer keep Charcoal and take a 1 px Stone line on their inner edge, so each still reads as a panel, as chapter 20's dark theme draws its panels; the fact panel keeps Charcoal and takes a 1 px Stone edge; the ask keeps Pillar Red with its White label and drops the keyline (4.49 to 1 against Charcoal). Images with a light ground swap to their dark twins by a pair of classes (`.oa-l` shown, `.oa-d` hidden at first, flipped in the query; each twin with the same alt). In an app that repaints without asking, every image with a ground of its own stays readable on that ground.
