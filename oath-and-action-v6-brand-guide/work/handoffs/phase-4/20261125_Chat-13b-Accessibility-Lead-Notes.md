### Scope

**AL-25. What each ruling binds.** The working email is a document in WCAG2ICT's sense, so I hold it to every A and AA criterion that applies, reading "web page" as "the email". A mail app is its user agent: what an app strips, repaints or blocks is outside our control, so the pass condition is that the email stays whole and readable when an app does those things, and nothing a reader needs depends on a feature an app may refuse, such as images, web fonts or styles in the head. On Substack, X, Threads and Instagram the platform owns the page's markup, its player, its type and its controls. We control the pictures we upload, the words we post, each image's alt text, Substack's captions, and the theme colors, fonts and images Substack lets a publisher set. The rulings bind those parts only. Where a platform's own part falls short, it goes in "Not fixed" as the platform's, and we never work around it by moving words into a picture.

### The working HTML email

**AL-26. The head, the title and the language (3.1.1; 2.4.2 through WCAG2ICT).** The file opens `<html lang="en" dir="ltr">` and repeats `lang="en" dir="ltr"` on the outermost wrapper inside `<body>`, since a mail app that sets a message inside its own page drops the message's `html` element and its attributes. The head holds `<meta charset="utf-8">`, the viewport tag, `<meta name="color-scheme" content="light dark">`, `<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light dark">` and a `<title>` that repeats the subject line word for word; the subject is the email's title in every inbox, so the editor writes it to name the issue's subject, as AL-01 asks of a page title. Passes: both `lang` attributes present and "en"; the title equals the subject. Checked by script on the file.

**AL-27. Layout tables and reading order (1.3.1, 1.3.2; F46, F49).** Every table used for layout carries `role="presentation"` and holds no `th`, `caption`, `summary`, `scope` or `headers`. The order of the markup is the order a reader meets the email: at 375 px the body is one column, and a row that holds two columns at 600 px stacks at 375 px in source order, the first column first. The structure is real markup: one `h1` (the lead headline), an `h2` for each story or section, `p` for paragraphs, `ul` or `ol` for lists, and every heading's size and margins set inline so each app draws it the same. A chart's table, if the email prints one, is a data table with `th` and `scope`, and it is the only table without `role="presentation"`. Passes: axe-core finds no violation in its table and heading rules; the script finds no layout table with a forbidden element; the text in markup order matches the reading order I see in the 600 and 375 px captures. Checked by axe-core, a script over the tables and the outline, and the captures.

**AL-28. Images off (1.1.1, 1.4.5; F65, H67; Rules 03.6, 19.2, 19.4).** Every `img` has an `alt` attribute and `width` and `height` attributes, so the layout holds when an app blocks images.
- The masthead's L3 image takes `alt="Oath & Action"`, as Rule 03.6's builders' note sets.
- The footer's lockmark takes `alt=""` when the footer's live text names Oath & Action, since a screen reader would hear the name twice; otherwise `alt="Oath & Action"`.
- Dividers, spacers and other decoration take `alt=""` (H67).
- A chart still takes its finding as alt text (Rule 19.2), and its title, measure line and source line print as live text beside it.
- No other word sits in a picture. Mail apps draw live text, so 1.4.5 asks for it; the logotypes are its one exception. The ask is a live-text link on a filled cell, never an image of a button. A background image carries nothing a reader needs.
- Each image's alt text is styled inline (the email's font stack, 13 px or more, a color that passes 4.5 to 1 on the cell behind it), so an app that prints alt text in place of a blocked image prints it readable.

Passes: with every image blocked, the email shows every word and every link it shows with images on, nothing overlaps, and each alt reads as its job. Checked in Chromium with every image request refused, at 600 and 375 px: the visible text compared by script with the images-on render, and the captures read by me.

**AL-29. A hinge headline in the email (DR-330; Rules 04.3, 06.9).** Whether the email runs a hinge headline is the design director's and the editor's call. If it does, B is drawn artwork, so it is a PNG at twice size set inside the live-text headline at the headline's cap height, with `alt="and"`, the word B stands for, as A01's alt text writes it. With images off the headline reads "The records law and your answer." On a Newsprint ground the PNG carries DR-330's 1 px Charcoal keyline inside the mark; on White and Charcoal it carries none. The headline sets at Rule 06.9's 26 px, so B on White (4.17 to 1) and Charcoal (4.49 to 1) passes as large text. Passes: the alt reads "and"; the keyline is present on Newsprint and absent elsewhere, read from the PNG's pixels; the headline's computed size is 24 px or more. Checked by script and on the pixels.

**AL-30. A mail app's dark mode (1.4.3, 1.4.11, 1.4.1).** Mail apps treat dark mode in three ways: some apply the email's own dark styles, some repaint the email's colors themselves, and some leave it as sent. No page the research lead read today says which app does which (F3), so this ruling names no app and the email must hold under all three.
- (a) The email carries its own dark treatment in a `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` block, taken from Rule 05.2's measured pairs: Newsprint surfaces to Charcoal; primary text to Newsprint (13.29 to 1); secondary text to Dim Newsprint (7.26 to 1); links Newsprint and underlined; the ask unchanged, White on Pillar Red (4.17 to 1) at 19 px bold, Rule 05.3's ask label; hairlines Stone, as decoration only.
- (b) For apps that repaint, every image that carries a mark stays visible on a light and on a dark ground. Charcoal measures 1.12 to 1 on black, so a wordmark drawn in Charcoal on a transparent ground vanishes when an app paints the ground dark. Each identity image therefore carries its own opaque ground, the band's color drawn into the PNG with its clear space, or sits on a ground that apps leave alone, such as a Charcoal band; the design director picks. Logotypes fall outside 1.4.3, so this part is the studio's standard (the masthead must stay visible), and I hold it to 3 to 1 between the letters, or their edge, and whatever sits next to them.
- (c) Each text color and background color is set inline on the element that holds the text, since some apps drop styles set only on `body` or in the head.
- (d) Links in running text keep their underline, and the ask is a filled box with words, so neither relies on color (1.4.1).
- (e) No style removes a link's focus outline (`outline: none` or `0`), which a reader who opens the email in a browser needs (2.4.7).

Passes: every text pair 4.5 to 1, or 3 to 1 for large text, measured on the pixels of the light render and the dark render; every identity image at 3 to 1 against Newsprint, White, Charcoal and black. Checked in Chromium, light and with dark emulated; an app that repaints is simulated by an inverting filter, reported as a simulation and never as an app's result; real mail apps go to "Not tested" and to Rule 19.9's device script (Requests).

**AL-31. The plain-text part (the studio's standard).** No criterion asks for it; a reader whose app or setting shows plain text gets the email whole. The `.txt` part is UTF-8 and holds every word of the HTML in the same order: the headline and each section head on a line of their own after a blank line; each paragraph as one line, which the mail app wraps to the screen (a hard break in mid-sentence strands short lines on a phone); each link as its words, a colon and the full address; a chart as its title, its finding (the alt), its source line and the address of its table; each informative image's alt where the image stood. Logos and decoration are left out. No line is a run of symbols, since a screen reader reads each one. The merge fields and the postal address print as the same labeled Illustrative slots the HTML prints (DR-343). Passes: the words match the HTML's, decoration aside, in order; every address the HTML links appears in the `.txt`; no line of symbols. Checked by script.

**AL-32. Link words (2.4.4; Rule 18.5).** Each link's words alone say where it goes or what it does, since a screen reader lists an email's links out of their sentences: never "here", "read more" or "click". Two links with the same words go to the same place. A bare address is a link's words only where the address itself is the point, printed for a reader to copy, as AL-13 prints the docket's. Links in running text keep a 1 px underline. The email carries no another-site mark and no hidden words: every link in an email leads out of it, and an app that drops styles would show hidden words on screen. Where the destination is another site and the reader needs to know, the words name it ("at Regulations.gov"). The unsubscribe link names what it does in words. Passes: the script's list of every link's words and address shows no empty name, no generic words and no same words with different addresses. Checked by script and by axe-core's link rules.

**AL-33. Fonts missing (Rule 06.9; 1.4.12).** The email loads no web font: no `@font-face` and no font link. Every element that holds text sets `font-family: Helvetica, Arial, sans-serif` inline, and a quotation sets Georgia italic (Rule 06.9). No cell that holds words has a fixed height, and no word carries `white-space: nowrap` (Rule 19.6), so a wider face or wider spacing moves words to the next line. Passes: rendered with the system's Arial-metric face and again with a wider face forced in (DejaVu Sans), at 600, 375 and 320 px, no word is cut, overlapped or pushed out of its box; with 1.4.12's four spacing values applied together, nothing is lost. Checked in Chromium with override styles, by script on box overflow and by my reading of the captures.

**AL-34. The smallest type and the phone (1.4.4, 1.4.10; Rules 06.3, 06.9).** The body is fluid: the outer table is `width:100%` with `max-width:600px`, and every image `max-width:100%; height:auto`. A phone then draws the email at true size; a fixed 600 px table would be shrunk to fit a 375 px screen, and 13 px type would land at about 8 px. No text anywhere is under 13 px (Rule 06.3), footer, legal lines and the postal address included; body 17 px, headline 26 px, quotation 24 px (Rule 06.9). No style sets `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none`, which stops a reader's text size from applying. A chart still's smallest label measures 13 px or more as drawn at a 375 px window. Passes: at 375 and 320 px windows the document is no wider than the window; the smallest computed font size is 13 px or more; at 200 percent zoom on a 600 px window nothing is lost (1.4.4); the chart's label height, measured on the image and scaled to the drawn width, is 13 px or more. Checked by script on computed styles and widths, and on the captures.

**AL-35. Tap sizes (2.5.8; Rule 19.5's 44 px).** The ask and every link that stands on its own line are 44 px tall where the app honors padding on a link (`display:inline-block` and padding on the `a`). Each such link's text also sets a line height of 24 px or more, so where an app drops a link's padding the target still clears 2.5.8's 24 by 24 px. Footer links sit one to a line at 24 px or more, or inside a sentence, where 2.5.8's Inline exception covers them. Passes: every link box measured 44 px tall with padding, and 24 px or more with link padding removed, except links inside a sentence. Checked by script on bounding boxes in Chromium, run twice.

**AL-36. Merge fields, the postal address and the Illustrative label (DR-343; 1.4.1, 1.4.3).** Each slot prints as live text at 13 px or more, at 4.5 to 1, with the word "Illustrative" beside it, so the label never rests on a color, a box or an icon alone. A merge field's raw token never reaches a reader without its label. The postal address is footer text, never an image. Passes and checked as AL-28 and AL-34, plus a script that finds every slot and its label.

**AL-37. Preview text (the studio's standard).** If the email carries preview text for the inbox list, it is either the deck, shown as the first line, or hidden from sight and from screen readers alike. The email holds no runs of invisible filler characters to pad the preview, since a screen reader or the plain-text part can meet them. Checked by script.

**AL-42. A chart in a post (1.1.1, 1.4.3, 1.4.11; Rules 13.6, 14.13, 19.2).** The chart is the kit's still.

The column was measured on one publication's newspaper layout, so I measure it again on ours once it exists.

### Social exports for X, Threads and Instagram

**AL-45. Alt text on every export (1.1.1; Rules 14.141, 16.7, 19.2, 19.4).** Each export carries two alts, as A01 does:
- **Full.** Every word on the picture in reading order, then what the drawing shows that the words do not. B in a hinge headline is written "and", as A01 writes it. The lockmark and the wordmark are named once at most, as "Oath & Action". Never "image of".
- **Short.** The headline and the finding in one sentence, for a field with a low limit.

All three platforms take 1,000 characters of alt text on an image (F4, F5, F6: X's help by search and its developer page read; Threads' and Instagram's publishing pages read), so the full alt goes on every image post and carousel slide on X, Threads and Instagram, and must fit 1,000 characters; A01's full alt runs 400. The short alt serves a field with a lower limit, such as a link preview's, and the post's words carry the rest (AL-46). Each carousel slide has its own pair. A photograph follows Rule 16.7. No page found gives a publisher an alt field for the avatar or X's header, so they hold no words a reader needs beyond the logotype and lines the profile's bio repeats (AL-49). Passes: every export's spec holds both alts; each full alt names every word the picture prints and runs 1,000 characters or fewer. Checked by script against each spec.

**AL-45a. Instagram stories and reel covers (1.1.1).** Instagram's publishing page says alt text is "Only supported on a single image or image media in a carousel. Reels and stories are not supported" (F6), and a story carries no caption. So a story or a reel cover holds no words a reader needs that the post or reel it points to does not carry in its own words, alt text or captions. A story built only of words, with nowhere else to read them, fails 1.1.1 and is not built. Checked on the specs, if the design director specifies a story or a reel cover in this chat.

**AL-46. Words in a picture repeated in the post (Rule 19.4; 1.4.5).** A platform's post takes a picture and text, and the design needs type the post's text cannot set, so a card may hold words; Rule 19.4 then has its words carried again. The post's own text carries the finding in a sentence or two and the source's publisher, title and observation period; the alt carries every word on the picture (AL-45); the page the post links carries everything. The limits (F4 to F6): X takes 280 characters, with a link counted as 23, so an X post may carry the source's publisher and period alone and leave the full source line to the alt and the page; Threads takes 500 characters and five links at most; Instagram takes 2,200 characters, 30 hashtags and 20 tags, and a carousel's words sit on the carousel, since its slides take none of their own. In the post's text: hashtags in capitalized words (#PublicRecords), so a screen reader says the words; no emoji standing for a word or used as a bullet, since a screen reader reads its full name; no letters styled through Unicode's mathematical alphabets, which a screen reader reads as symbols or skips; the link last, on its own line. Passes: each post's words hold the finding and the source's three parts, within the platform's character limit from the facts; none of the forbidden characters. Checked by script on the spec's post words.

**AL-47. Contrast and size on each export's pixels (1.4.3, 1.4.11; Rules 05.2, 05.5, 08.2, 14.141).** I measure every text run against the pixels behind it on the export itself.
- **Large text.** A feed shows a single picture at about a third of its size (Rule 14.141), and Rule 08.2's feed test takes 360 px wide as the smallest. A word on an export counts as large only if it would still be 24 px, or 18.66 px in bold, with the export drawn 360 px wide: 72 px, or 56 px bold, on a 1,080 px export; 80 px, or 62.2 px bold, on a 1,200 px card; scaled by width for any other size. Every smaller word needs 4.5 to 1. This is the studio's reading of 1.4.3 for pictures a platform scales.
- **Smallest word.** No word sets under Rule 14.141's 36 px on a 1,080 px export, scaled by width: 40 px on a 1,200 px card, about 53 px on a 1,600 px X post.
- **Marks.** Chart marks, icons and control shapes drawn in the picture take 3 to 1 against their neighbors (1.4.11).

Passes: every pair at its threshold on the pixels; no word under the floor. Checked by script on the PNGs, from each word's box as the renderer records it.

**AL-48. Hinge headlines under DR-330 (1.4.3).** On Newsprint, B in a hinge headline takes Rule 05.3's keyline, a 1 px Charcoal edge drawn inside the mark at the export's true size, which counts as the letter's edge (13.29 to 1). On White (4.17 to 1) and Charcoal (4.49 to 1) it takes none, and passes as large text because every hinge headline on an export sets at the large size of AL-47, 56 px bold or more on a 1,080 px export. B never sits in red on Prussian (Rule 05.3). B in the wordmark, the lockmark, the avatar and the badge does not change.
