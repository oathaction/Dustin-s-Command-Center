# Voice line and headlines

Phase 1, oa-editor, October 21, 2026. Everything here is a proposal.

## 1. Voice line

Original, in force until Dustin approves a revision (DC-04): "We explain the rules. We expose the gaps. We connect people to action. We act."

For Track, whose V5 job is "Follow the decision and keep its record within reach.", the original says "expose the gaps", which assumes a wrong to uncover. All four sentences share one shape. "We act" makes the publication the actor, though V5 hands the step to the reader.

All three keep the pillar verbs in order.

| | Revision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| A | "We explain the rules. Then we track each decision about them and connect you with whoever makes it, so you're ready to act." | The smallest change. It keeps V5's opening sentence and gives Act to the reader. |
| B | "We'll explain the rules and track what happens to them. When a decision is coming, we'll connect you with the people making it, so you can act in time." | It splits at the hinge: commitment (Explain, Track), then action (Connect, Act). "We'll" makes each a promise, and "in time" ties Act to a date. |
| C | "Bring us a question about a rule. We'll explain it and track it with you, then connect you with whoever decides so you can act on what you know." | It opens with the reader, and "with you" carries DC-05's "you and me". It runs longest and repeats V5's "Bring your questions." |

**Recommendation: B.** It says Dustin's idea out loud, a commitment and then the action, in words a newcomer knows. Its opening echoes the reader promise, so 02 Voice should set the two apart. The original remains the fallback.

## 2. Candidate lines

The brand lines table, with each line's role, belongs to 02 Voice (CD review C8). The locked lines, quoted from Master Brief V2 section 3:

- Tagline: "A country that answers to its people."
- Mission: "Help people understand and improve the rules that shape their lives."
- Reader promise: "We explain how a decision is made and give you a practical way to take part."
- Editorial commitment: "Clarity is our duty. Action is our promise."
- Pillars: Explain → Track → Connect → Act

### "From spectators to stewards."

- **Voice.** Its "from X to Y" turn is a contrasting pair and a slogan, naming no mechanism.
- **Nonpartisan.** It favors no party. It does scold the reader as a spectator.
- **Newcomer.** "Steward" is a rare word, and the line never says what one does.
- **Locked lines.** The square mockup puts it in the tagline's slot, and V4.2 lists it as the "Historical tagline", "Retired from current applications." The banner sets "stewards" in red on Prussian, which Rule 05.3 forbids.
- **02 Voice role: none.** Its row records the retired first tagline.

### "Democracy has a structure."

- **Voice.** Four plain present-tense words name a mechanism. It passes.
- **Nonpartisan.** Editorial judgment, unsourced: "democracy" has carried campaign weight lately, and some readers answer it with "a republic." The risk is low.
- **Newcomer.** "Structure" stays abstract until the reader sees the lockmark, the 3 by 3 grid of nodes beside the wordmark.
- **Locked lines.** It explains a symbol, a job no locked line does. V5 already says the lockmark "says that democracy has a locatable structure and that one point moves it."
- **02 Voice role: the lockmark's explanatory line.** Use it in 08 The Signal Grid and on the About page, then name the lever, the larger red node at bottom right. "Every public decision has a structure." works if the word worries Dustin.

### "The ampersand is the hinge."

- **Voice.** Plain and declarative, it passes once a definition follows.
- **Nonpartisan.** It takes no side.
- **Newcomer.** V5 never defines "hinge". Next sentences: "A hinge is the joint a door swings on. In our name it joins a commitment to the action that keeps it."
- **Locked lines.** The editorial commitment, "Clarity is our duty. Action is our promise.", already names the two parts it joins.
- **02 Voice role: a line inside the guide.** It opens "Why it works" in 04 and captions the hinge diagram in Inter. The mockups' Lora Italic needs a named exception, since V5 keeps it for quotations.

## 5. The position label

Only position pieces, Act pieces that ask readers to support a specific measure, carry it (DC-01). Swap "supports" for "opposes" where needed.

| | Label | Disclosure line | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Our position | "Oath & Action supports [measure]. Read our explainer on it, which sets out the main arguments, before you decide." | "Our" names who holds the view |
| B | Opinion | "Signed opinion by [name], arguing for [measure]. Our tracker and explainer on it take no position." | Newspaper convention |
| C | Position: Support | "Oath & Action asks you to support [measure]. How we chose this position, and who funds us, is on our methods page." | Reads like a tracker status chip |

**Where it sits.** The label opens the kicker, the small line above the headline, so readers meet it first. The disclosure sits directly above the ask button, after the hinge divider (a hairline broken by a small ampersand in its surface's ink). Social tiles carry the label top left, and email opens its preheader with it. Set it in Charcoal at reading size, since the ask holds the view's one working red (Rule 05.3).

**Recommendation: A.** Its disclosure sends the reader to the explainer, so the rest of the coverage reads as nonpartisan. Keep B for signed guest pieces.
