# Voice line and headlines

Phase 1, oa-editor, October 21, 2026. Everything here is a proposal.

## 1. Voice line

Original, in force until Dustin approves a revision (DC-04): "We explain the rules. We expose the gaps. We connect people to action. We act."

V5 gives Track the job "Follow the decision and keep its record within reach." The original says "expose the gaps" for it, which assumes a wrong to uncover. All four sentences share one shape. "We act" makes the publication the actor, though V5's Act pillar hands the step to the reader.

All three keep the pillar verbs in order.

| | Revision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| A | "We explain the rules. Then we track each decision about them and connect you with whoever makes it, so you're ready to act." | The smallest change. It keeps V5's opening sentence and gives Act to the reader. |
| B | "We'll explain the rules and track what happens to them. When a decision is coming, we'll connect you with the people making it, so you can act in time." | It splits at the hinge: the commitment (Explain, Track), then the action (Connect, Act). "We'll" makes each a promise, and "in time" ties Act to a date. |
| C | "Bring us a question about a rule. We'll explain it and track it with you, then connect you with whoever decides so you can act on what you know." | It opens with the reader, and "with you" carries DC-05's "you and me". It runs longest and repeats V5's "Bring your questions." |

**Recommendation: B.** It says Dustin's idea out loud, a commitment and then the action that keeps it, in words a newcomer knows. Its opening echoes the reader promise, so 02 Voice should set the two apart. The original remains the fallback.

## 2. Candidate lines

The brand lines table, with each line's role, belongs to 02 Voice (CD review C8). The locked lines, quoted from Master Brief V2 section 3:

- Tagline: "A country that answers to its people."
- Mission: "Help people understand and improve the rules that shape their lives."
- Reader promise: "We explain how a decision is made and give you a practical way to take part."
- Editorial commitment: "Clarity is our duty. Action is our promise."
- Pillars: Explain → Track → Connect → Act

### "From spectators to stewards."

- **Voice rules.** Its "from X to Y" turn is a contrasting pair that lands as a slogan, and it names no mechanism.
- **Nonpartisan reading.** It favors no party. It does scold the reader as a spectator.
- **Newcomer clarity.** "Steward" is rare in everyday speech, and the line never says what one does.
- **Beside the locked lines.** The square mockup puts it in the tagline's slot. V4.2 lists it as the "Historical tagline" and marks it "Retired from current applications." The banner mockup sets "stewards" in red on Prussian, at 3.19:1.
- **Role in the 02 Voice table: none.** Its row records the retired first tagline, and 01 Purpose can tell that history.

### "Democracy has a structure."

- **Voice rules.** Four plain present-tense words name a mechanism. It passes.
- **Nonpartisan reading.** Editorial judgment, unsourced: "democracy" has carried campaign weight lately, and some readers answer it with "a republic." The risk is low.
- **Newcomer clarity.** "Structure" stays abstract until the reader sees the lockmark, the 3 by 3 grid of nodes beside the wordmark.
- **Beside the locked lines.** It explains a symbol, a job no locked line does. V5 already says the lockmark "says that democracy has a locatable structure and that one point moves it."
- **Role in the 02 Voice table: the lockmark's explanatory line.** Use it in 08 The Signal Grid and on the About page, followed by a sentence naming the lever, the larger red node at bottom right. "Every public decision has a structure." works if the word worries Dustin.

### "The ampersand is the hinge."

- **Voice rules.** Plain and declarative, it passes once a definition follows.
- **Nonpartisan reading.** It takes no side.
- **Newcomer clarity.** V5 never defines "hinge". Proposed next sentences: "A hinge is the joint a door swings on. In our name it joins a commitment to the action that keeps it."
- **Beside the locked lines.** The editorial commitment, "Clarity is our duty. Action is our promise.", already names the two parts it joins.
- **Role in the 02 Voice table: a line inside the guide.** It opens "Why it works" in 04 and captions the hinge diagram, set in Inter. The mockups' Lora Italic needs a named exception, since V5 keeps it for quotations.

## 4. Hinge pairs

A pair names a sequence, so I don't count it as a contrasting pair. Each works in any state. I rechecked all twenty against that rule. ## 5. The position label

Only position pieces, the Act pieces that ask readers to support a specific measure, carry this label (DC-01). Swap "supports" for "opposes" where needed.

| | Label | Disclosure line | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Our position | "Oath & Action supports [measure]. Read our explainer on it, which sets out the main arguments, before you decide." | "Our" names who holds the view |
| B | Opinion | "Signed opinion by [name], arguing for [measure]. Our tracker and explainer on it take no position." | The newspaper convention for signed pieces |
| C | Position: Support | "Oath & Action asks you to support [measure]. How we chose this position, and who funds us, is on our methods page." | Reads like a tracker status chip |

**Where it sits.** The label opens the kicker, the small label line above the headline, so a reader meets it first. The disclosure sits directly above the ask button, after the hinge divider, the hairline broken by a small ampersand. On a social tile the label takes the top-left corner, and in email it opens the preheader. Set it in Charcoal at reading size, since the ask already uses red.

**Recommendation: A.** Its disclosure sends the reader to the explainer, which keeps the rest of the coverage readable as nonpartisan. Keep B for signed guest pieces.
