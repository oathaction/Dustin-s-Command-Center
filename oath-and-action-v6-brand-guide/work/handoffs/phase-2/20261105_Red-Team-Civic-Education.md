# Red team: civic education review of the five atlas systems

November 5, 2026. I judged each system on whether a first-time adult reader learns from it, against the editorial brief and the October 30 civic education memo. Scores run from 1 (does not teach) to 5 (teaches with no help). The ten-second results below are my predictions from the 1440 px captures. No raters took the test.

## 1. System by system

### System 1, Times Explainer: 4

Below the overview it teaches better than any other system. Its Elections page is the only one that draws the safe-seat loop and the accountability loop meeting at risk of losing office, and its R10 page is as complete as any. The first screen of the overview does not show the system.

**Must fix**

1. *Overview.* The first screen holds a headline, a paragraph and a strip of part names, with no drawing. The eight-part diagram starts about 4,600 px down at 1440 and 10,600 px down on a phone, so a ten-second reader cannot say how the parts connect. Fix: move the whole-system diagram up under the deck and let the numbered walk-through follow it.
2. *Overview.* "Who Do They Answer To?" appears only in the footer, and the headline "Wealth at the top can shape the rules that add to it." is a thesis about the rich. A rater who reads only that line may name a side at question 4. Fix: lead with the atlas question and set the finding beneath it as a marked studio draft.
3. *Overview.* There is no evidence key. The how-to-read box says "Open a part to see each connection and how strong its evidence is," so ten-second question 5 fails. Fix: add the five textures with their glosses to that box.

**Should fix**

4. *Reform R-01 and Elections.* R-01 prints "A plus sign means the two variables move the same way. It does not mean the change is good." and then shows a display-size "+M" under Democratic quality. The Elections reform list prints effects the same way, as in "+M (moderate positive)". On these rows a plus does mean better, so the page teaches the sign two ways. Fix: state effects in words ("moderate improvement expected") with bars and no plus glyph, as System 4 does.
5. *Reform R-01.* "The problem it answers" opens with Unite America's seat counts, so the first evidence a reader meets on the editor's own cause comes from a group that funds primary-reform campaigns. Fix: open with the R10 summary ("In a safe seat, the primary decides who wins, and few people vote in it"), then the counts, then the two issue records that name the problem (I026 and X01).
6. *Overview, everyday questions.* The answer panel lists "Housing-scarcity loop (R8)" and three reforms as plain text with no links, so the question ends there. Fix: link every part, loop and reform the panel names.

**Polish**

7. *Elections, problems.* Rows repeat "Touches safe seats, seats settled in the primary, risk of losing office, incumbents control election rules and incumbents defeated" under the title, which pushes the real case aside. Fix: show touched conditions only inside the opened record.
8. *Overview.* "From wealth down to policy" and "Outside forces press in from the top" give the vertical story a rank order. Fix: use "next" for the order and keep "down" out of the copy.

### System 2, Economist Register: 3

It has the best first screen of the five. The atlas question sits in the kicker, with all the counts, an evidence key, the plus rule and a five-part circuit with its return line. The deeper pages say less.

**Must fix**

1. *Overview.* The Elections row opens by default on "THE FIX AIMED AT IT", a full navy panel for R-01 showing "Democratic quality moderate positive". It is the only reform on the overview and the one the editor campaigns for, and the panel carries neither the disclosure nor "The Atlas takes no position on Question 3 or Ballot Measure 2." Fix: put both lines in the panel, or load every row closed.
2. *Loop R10.* The loop's plain summary never appears. The deck reads "Safe-seat loop. Four steps, each a numbered connection. One turn takes 2 to 10 years," and the right column quotes the model's dense `how_to_read` sentence. Fix: set the summary under the title.
3. *Elections, reforms.* Each of the ten rows gives a title, codes ("L42 L56"), effects and a route. None says what the reform does. Fix: add the one-sentence summary under each title, as Systems 1, 3 and 4 do.

**Should fix**

4. *Reform R-01.* The disclosure sits in the status box at the foot of the page. The page also leaves out implementation, costs, legal authority and alternatives. Alternatives matter most: they show other ways to answer the same problem. Fix: move the disclosure beside the summary and add the four missing sections.
5. *Overview, circuit.* Arrows carry bare counts ("5", "4"), and Your household and Technology appear only as rows of "7 in 4 out". A count alone does not tell a reader what passes between parts. Fix: name one connection on each main flow ("such as Concentrated wealth funds more political spending"), as Systems 1, 3 and 5 do, and draw household and technology as named boxes.
6. *Elections.* Nothing says that R10 and B4 meet at risk of losing office, or that B4 works only where seats are competitive. That is the memo's idea for this level. Fix: add the sentence above the loop cards and set B4 beside R10.

**Polish**

7. *Loop R10.* "Red marks the three points where reform R-01 acts; the Prussian tab names it." Readers do not know the palette. Fix: "the dark tab names it."
8. *Elections, problems.* The Touches column prints "C1 C2 C3 C4 C5". Fix: use short names, or drop the column.

### System 3, Reference Sheets: 3

The first screen shows the five core parts with their questions, and every condition sits just below. The R10 sheet is the only one to teach why a loop feeds itself: "Two of the four steps lower the next condition. An even number of lowering steps makes a loop reinforcing." Codes on every line and a wired Elections plate make it read like a technical manual.

**Must fix**

1. *Overview.* The deck states the chain as fact: "Concentrated wealth and market power decide who gets heard in politics. Officials in safe seats have less reason to listen to anyone else." Some of those links are plausible or contested, and the overview has no evidence key. Fix: add the texture key and one line saying every link carries a grade.
2. *Overview.* The map legend has a mark for "Where reform R-01 acts" and sets a red target on C2. The one reform singled out on the overview is the editor's, with no disclosure. Fix: drop the target from the overview, or print the no-position line and the disclosure beside the legend.
3. *Elections, plate 02.2.* All fifteen arriving and leaving connections run as crossing wires into one box. This is the "too many lines" Dustin turned down. Fix: lead with "The same connections in words", which teaches well, and offer the wiring as a toggle.

**Should fix**

4. *Overview, questions.* "Why did my rent go up?" jumps to loop R8 in the catalog, and the reader never sees the short answer. Fix: open the question's answer first, with its loop as a link.
5. *Loop R10.* R-01's targets carry red brackets joined by red wires to the reform card. The wires cross the loop and can read as more causal lines. Fix: outline the target boxes and arrows in red and label them, as Systems 4 and 5 do.
6. *Loop R10.* The headline "Safe seats keep themselves safe." reads as fate, which is memo misreading 4. Fix: write a headline that names the weakest step or the reforms that act on the loop.
7. *Elections.* The same gap as System 2, finding 6: no line says R10 and B4 meet at C3.

**Polish**

8. *Throughout.* Codes lead every name ("A1 Wealth at the top"), and condition cards say "Kind: Condition (auxiliary)". Fix: set codes after the name in a lighter weight, and replace "auxiliary" with plain words.
9. *Loop catalog.* Each card prints the return step above the first box, so the reader meets the last step first. Fix: draw it at the bottom, where the chain closes.

### System 4, Night Edition: 4

The overview shows every part and condition with bundled flows inside two screens. Any name opens a panel with its full record. The R10 page marks where all three reforms act, and the catalog legend gives the clearest plus rule. Its opening words overstate the evidence.

**Must fix**

1. *Overview.* The deck ends "This atlas traces how that happens, one documented connection at a time." Only 32 of 118 connections are graded documented, and 49 are plausible, contested or hypothesis. Fix: write "one graded connection at a time", and add the texture key to the overview.
2. *Overview.* The headline "Policy that favors insiders adds to the wealth that funds political spending." and the line "The people we elect often seem to hear someone else before they hear us" frame the page as a grievance before the map appears. Fix: lead with "Who do they answer to?", already on the right-hand card, and run the finding as a smaller studio-draft line.

**Should fix**

3. *Elections.* The 39 problems come after the 10 reforms, so fixes arrive before the problems they answer. Fix: put problems first.
4. *Overview.* Flow arrows carry counts only, and the return line is the one flow named in words. Fix: name one connection per main flow.
5. *Reform R-01.* "The problem it answers" opens with Unite America's counts. Fix: open with the R10 summary.
6. *Elections.* The headline "The risk of losing office is where the safe-seat loop meets the loops that hold officials to account" is right, but the drawing under it shows only the five inside connections, so B4 never appears. Fix: draw B4 beside R10 and add that it works only where seats are competitive.

**Polish**

7. *How to read.* "Newsprint: the route you are reading" names a palette color readers cannot picture. Fix: "Light: the route you are reading."
8. *Overview.* "The loops that keep it turning" leans toward fate. Fix: lead with the deck's own line that every loop shows where a reform can interrupt it.

### System 5, Architecture: 3

It has the plainest title, "Who do they answer to?", and the warmest way in, through 16 questions from a reader's week. The Elections floor plan sets the safe-seat chain in one row, and "Why it matters" under each condition is the best gloss in the set. The building teaches a hierarchy the model does not have, and the reform text leaves out what the reforms do.

**Must fix**

1. *Overview.* "Each floor passes something up to the next, and policy sends its effects back down." Policy is the roof, household and technology sit "below the heavy ground line", and flows read "pass up". The memo's note on the layers reference says "Upward arrows teach a hierarchy the model lacks." The page's own tally shows 15 connections running back down and 18 skipping a floor. Fix: label each flow with what passes in both directions, drop "up" and "down" from the copy, and give the return line the weight of the upward arrows.
2. *Elections, reforms.* Each reform's line is the opening of its rationale, which describes the problem. R-04 reads "A durable majority can use its hold on the legislature to draw maps and set access rules" and never says that a commission would draw them. Fix: print the reform's summary sentence.
3. *Reform R-01.* Success measures, alternatives, legal authority, implementation and costs are missing, and the disclosure sits at the foot of the page. Fix: add the sections and move the disclosure beside the summary.

**Should fix**

4. *Overview.* No part, condition or connection appears in the first screen. On a phone the building starts after 3,300 px of questions. Fix: put a compact strip of the floors, each with its question, above the question cards.
5. *Overview.* "Start from an entry document, an ordinary paper a reader already handles... used as a starting point into the map" is design-memo language. Fix: "Pick a question about a bill or a notice you already know."
6. *Loop R10.* "The weakest step" sits in small gray type, the quietest label on the drawing. Fix: give it the weight System 1 gives it, and add System 3's line, "the loop holds only as firmly as this link."
7. *Elections.* The same R10 and B4 gap as Systems 2 and 3.

**Polish**

8. *Overview and How to read.* "On Newsprint" names a palette, and "A positive sign does not mean a benefit" uses the word the memo bans. Fix: "A plus sign does not mean the change is good."
9. *Loop catalog.* The tiny building on each card means nothing until a reader finds its legend line. Fix: drop it, or name the floors.

## 2. Across the five

### What to borrow

- **From System 1:** the Elections drawing "Two loops meet at risk of losing office", with "The accountability loop works only where seats are competitive"; overview flows that name an example connection ("5 connections run from Wealth to Voice, such as Concentrated wealth funds more political spending"); the R-01 evidence headline "The problem is better established than the remedy."
- **From System 2:** the first screen as a whole; register rows that list every condition with an evidence bar per part; step sentences such as "More seats settled in the primary means less risk of losing office."
- **From System 3:** the sign arithmetic on the loop page; "The weakest step: the loop holds only as firmly as this link."; problem filters by condition; the loop index at the top of the catalog; line glosses such as "Sign +, the two move the same way."
- **From System 4:** "Built from the signs: start with more, flip at each minus."; "R-02 aims here" and "R-03 aims here" on the R10 drawing; the catalog legend "raises: a plus sign. The two move the same way, which is not the same as good."; reform effects in words with no plus glyph; the breadcrumb Overview / Part C, Elections / Safe-seat loop / Reform R-01.
- **From System 5:** the atlas question as headline; "Why it matters" under each condition; the read-aloud line set before the R10 drawing, where the memo puts it.

### Fixes every system needs

- **Loop catalog.** No catalog card shows the first sentence of its loop's summary, which memo section 6 asks for; System 3 shows it only in the panel after a click. The read-aloud line says what moves, and nothing on the card says why it matters, so the loops do not teach on their own. Add the sentence under each title. R8's would read "When homes are scarce, the homes that exist sell for more, and owners' equity grows."
- **R1's qualification.** "On most issues the two groups agree, so its reach depends on the issue" appears in the visible text of none of the 30 views. The memo pairs it with misreading 3, arrows read as blame. Put it on R1's catalog card.
- **"Democratic quality".** Every reform list starts a row with "Democratic quality", often next to "positive", beside reforms the editor campaigns for. A cold reader can take it for the party. Ask Dustin whether the family may print as "quality of democracy".

### Ranking

1. **Night Edition.** Within two screens the overview shows what each part is and how the parts connect, and every name opens to its record; it needs the overclaim and the headline fixed.
2. **Times Explainer.** It teaches best below the overview, and with the diagram moved to the top it could take first place.
3. **Economist Register.** It has the best ten-second screen, with thinner loop and reform pages and an R-01 panel that lacks its disclosure.
4. **Reference Sheets.** It holds the most complete record, set in codes and wires that read as a manual.
5. **Architecture.** It has the warmest entry, built on the ladder the memo warns against, and its reform text omits what the reforms do.
