Dustin's direction is DR-104 to DR-107; the brief is `work/handoffs/phase-2/20261107_Board-6-Brief.md`. I read the four makers' notes, the research memo and the engineer's builder notes. ## Decision or verdict

Three picks are ready: numbered steps for the tracker, Set A for the pillar icons and S1 for the stacked lockup. On color I disagree with the color designer. Patina is the best of the six sheets, and I still recommend keeping Pillar Red as the lead, with the red's party reading added to the reader check Dustin already approved (DR-68).

The three faults my first review found are fixed. I confirmed each one on the rebuilt pages at 1440 and 390 px:

1. **Tracker, step 6.** The sixth step was named Law, and trackers name the step a bill is at now, so H.1180, a bill waiting for the governor, read "Step 6 of 6: Law." Step 6 is now Governor. The table defines it as "The governor signs or vetoes the bill, and lawmakers can override a veto." A new plate, "When a bill becomes law", draws H.1180 after signing in all four options, and each reads "Became law on April 2. It takes effect July 1." The word Law no longer appears as a step name anywhere on the page, in the lists, keys, comparison cells, bill pages or T1.
2. **Pillar icons, the current pillar.** At 48 px and larger the row marked the current pillar by red alone. In Chromium, the current word now computes as weight 700 with a 2 px underline at both 48 and 24 px in all three sets, and the other words carry none. In grayscale the current pillar now reads at once. Use rule 2 on the page says the underline marks the pillar at every size, with red as a second sign from 48 px.
3. **Color sheets, the cost of a move.** The page now says any move off Pillar Red replaces red in 12 color tokens, or nine if red stays the error color. It also names the 244 two-color Civic Line files and their 732 two-color tiles, Rules 05.3 and 05.5, chapters 03 to 05 and every identity proof. It names Patina's extra moves: focus ring, success check and diverging scale. C1's "Why" carries the same cost and records my keep-red view. I counted the files myself: 244 two-color icon files and 732 tile SVGs that carry red, each tile with two PNG renders. My first draft wrongly counted six tiles, because I counted the tile folders.

## Section 1: a simpler tracker, and one job for the grid

**Pick:** six plain steps (Filed, Hearing, Committee vote, First chamber, Second chamber, Governor). Use Option B (numbered steps) in lists and on story cards and Option D (the vertical timeline with the same numbered marks) on a bill's own page. The lockmark keeps four places: masthead, page header, footer and sign-off. Every coding job retires, and chapter 08 becomes a short chapter called The lockmark.

**Agree with the maker?** Yes, now that step 6 is Governor. A number gives a reader words to say ("step 2 of 6") with no key, and it survives gray print. In grayscale every state still tells apart by shape, which I checked on the comparison myself. Option A at 360 px loses its step names, and a row of nodes on a line sits close to the lockmark's own drawing, which is the confusion Dustin asked us to end.

**Still needs work (none of it blocks the pick):**
- Step 5: in most states the second chamber's committee holds its own hearing. Say so in step 5's line, and show that the take-part mark can sit on step 5.
- No list draws a stopped bill yet. The page now says a veto that stands ends the line at step 6 as Stopped, and one drawn example in a list would show it.
- Line up the four column heads in the comparison on one top line. The Recommended tags still push B and D up.
- Sign-off: the 24 px lockmark master scaled to 40 px reads soft. Set the sign-off from the display master at 48 px, where Rule 08.1 already places it.
- The tracker's marks and states belong with status marks in chapter 11, where Master Brief section 11 puts them, and its live behavior in chapter 18.
- Gate rule 4: Master Brief section 3 lists the Signal Grid as retained identity. Dustin's answer to B6-3 is the named approval for retiring its coding jobs.

**Risks:** six steps simplify real procedure, such as conference committees and second readings, and a bill's own page has to carry those dates. No reader has seen the steps yet. The maker's T2 already sends them to the reader check.

## Section 2: four pillar icons

**Pick:** Set A, the four marks drawn for the pillars (CL-019 the open book, CL-020 the route to a magnifier, CL-021 the overlapping rings, CL-022 the pressing hand), with the word beside each icon and the current pillar underlined at every size.

**Agree with the maker?** Yes. Sets B and C borrow drawings that already name topics (Public Records, Household Income, Petition, Civic Ties, Voting), so a reader would meet one drawing with two meanings. A ballot narrows Act to voting, and a petition can read as advocacy.

**The kicker question:** I answer no. The hub recommends that the pillar mark stays out of kickers (B6-5). Rule 01.21 has seven content labels. Analysis, Report and Our position name no pillar, and Guide does not say Connect, so a pillar mark in those kickers would code a pillar with no word beside it. That is the job Dustin asked the grid to stop doing, moved to an icon. A kicker carries a topic icon only when the topic's own word sits beside it. The pillar marks live in the pillar row, on section openers and on tiles. This also settles the clash between sections 1 and 2.

**Still needs work:**
- Pixel-fit masters at 16 and 24 px for the four pillar marks. Master Brief section 6 asks icons to sit on the pixel grid at both sizes, and today's line measures 0.42 px at 16 px. New drawings need their own approval. This keeps the section's craft score at 3.
- On a phone the row still falls into two pairs and drops the arrow between Track and Connect. Stack it in one column, or keep the arrow at the end of the first line.
- Act measures 63.9 px against Explain's 79.4 px at 128 px. Set it larger in placement, with no redraw.
- The kicker card crops chapter 01's tracker card to "Stop Insider Trading Act Tracker". In the guide that card keeps its deck, which names both provisions of H.R. 7008 (DR-98).

**Risks:** many readers will call Connect a Venn diagram, and two rings recall two-circle payment-card logos. The naming test is the studio's own reading.

## Section 3: stacked lockups

**Pick:** S1, ampersand ends line one: OATH & over ACTION, flush left.

**Agree with the maker?** Yes. It is the wordmark split after B, so a reader who knows the masthead knows the stack. It reads in the name's order, packs into the smallest box and keeps the largest capitals in a round avatar (49 px against L2's 43). A script of my own matched all 17 contours in each of the six display files to the wordmark and B masters by translation alone, worst offset 1.1 × 10⁻¹³ units. S6 stays useful as a test: if Dustin prefers it, L2's large B was never the trouble.

**Still needs work (unchanged since the first review; the page was not rebuilt):**
- On a phone the "Beside L2" plates scroll with no line saying so, and a reader sees "OATH ACTIO". Add the tracker page's "Wider than this screen" line.
- "Below these sizes, use L8" should say L8 is the monogram lockup (H11).
- After the pick, chapter 03's proofs show L2 and S1 side by side at 48 and 96 px and at display size, as Master Brief section 3 asks, and the asset package writes S1's Charcoal and Prussian White files.

**Risks:** the first line stops 0.46 cap heights short of the second, so the right edge steps in at B. That step is the wordmark's own spacing, and I would keep it.

## Section 4: six color schemes

**Pick:** keep Pillar Red as the lead. If Dustin moves, Patina is the only scheme worth taking forward.

**Agree with the maker?** No on the lead, and yes that Patina is the strongest of the six. Patina passes 3 to 1 on both grounds with no keyline, carries a White label at 6.01 to 1 and has no party reading in the memo's sources. Those gains are real. It also has the same lightness as Stone (1.01 to 1), and a reader with deuteranopia sees almost no difference between them (0.9). Pillar Red stays distinct from Stone for that reader (32.6). Patina sits 1.27 to 1 from Verdigris, so the focus ring, the success check and the diverging scale all move, and its sheet now shows all three. Red already does its jobs under rules the guide has paid for (Rules 05.3 and 05.5), and it gives Act its urgency. The open question is how readers read red for party. That is a question for readers, and the reader check can answer it before anything changes.

**Red team on the six:** Federal Ink puts B 1.41 to 1 from the letters, so the ampersand vanishes in grayscale and one-color print. Highlighter turns B Moss green on paper, so the wordmark changes color with its ground. I would strike both. Ballot Violet says "between the parties", which is a different claim from one standard of evidence (DR-93). Signal Orange sits 2.4 from the Guardian's opinion orange and matches Wear Orange on firearms pieces. No sheet lets a party own a color: each prints party marks in Charcoal with the name, and none sets two colors against each other as sides.

**Still needs work:** if Dustin moves, a press proof on newsprint and the reader check come before his named approval. The strip of seven at 14 percent still cannot be read, and it is the hub's lead capture for this section.
