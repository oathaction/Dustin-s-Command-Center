# Atlas concepts: civic education memo

Phase 2, Stage 1, October 30, 2026. From the civic education adviser to the studio. Every ID, label, count and quotation comes from `work/boards/phase-2/maps/20261028_Maps-Model-Data.json`. Section 5 holds studio drafts.

## 1. The reader

Three illustrative readers arrive cold: a newsletter reader coming from a story on Massachusetts Question 3, a student who must explain one loop in class, and a local organizer who wants to know who decides a rule. Most picture government as a chain of command where every bad result has a villain. They read a plus sign as good news and an arrow as proof, and many take any election diagram for a party scorecard.

In ten seconds they want to know what the page covers, whether it takes a side, and where to click. The overview answers those before anyone traces a wire.

## 5. The overview's first lines (studio drafts)

**A.** Who do officials answer to?
This map links 51 conditions, from household wealth to court rulings, through 118 connections. Each connection carries a grade for the strength of its evidence.

**B.** Can your representative lose their seat?
"In a safe seat, the primary decides who wins, and few people vote in it." That pattern is one of 23 loops on this map of 51 conditions and the connections between them.

**C.** Where economic power meets public office.
The map follows 118 connections among 51 conditions, from household wealth to the risk of losing office. In 23 places, a chain of causes returns to where it started.

**How to read this (23 words):** Each name is a condition that can rise or fall; an arrow shows one raising or lowering another. Broken lines mark weaker evidence.

## 6. The loop catalog

Each entry runs about 60 words, in this order:

1. The loop's own route across the concept's map, with sign and texture on each step (DR-15).
2. "R10 · Reinforcing, feeds itself".
3. The V7 title in full. A shortened title drops the claim.
4. The first sentence of `summary`. R8: "When homes are scarce, the homes that exist sell for more, and owners' equity grows."
5. The read-aloud line.
6. A status line. R10: "Running up · 2 to 10 years · Weakest step L53, Plausible · 3 reforms".
7. For R2 and R16, "closed with a hypothesized step".

The loop page keeps `how_to_read`, `evidence_summary`, `qualification`, the reference mode and reform detail.

Order the page by the model's `groups` (A, B, C, D, then the three sketches) to match the overview, with two-step loops first in each group. R8 then leads as the worked example: two steps, both well supported, on housing. Head each group with its area's `text` and kind count ("3 reinforcing, 1 balancing" for C). Open with the totals, 18 reinforcing and 5 balancing, and "Improvement or decline can reinforce itself."

## 7. Ten-second test script

**Show** the 1440 px overview capture alone. Agent raters get the instruction "You have ten seconds. Answer from your first look." People later see a timed full-screen slide, then the questions. Use five raters per concept, each agent playing one reader from section 1.

**Ask:**

1. In one sentence, what is this page about?
2. Where would you click first?
3. What does an arrow from one name to another tell you?
4. Does this page favor a political party? Which one?
5. Are all the connections equally certain? How can you tell?

**Pass, per answer:**

- Q1 names who officials answer to, or how economic and public life connect. A party or a single issue fails.
- Q2 names a control on the page.
- Q3 says one raises or lowers the other. "Good" or "harm" fails.
- Q4 is no, or cannot tell.
- Q5 mentions the line patterns.

A concept passes when four of five raters pass Q1 to Q3 and none names a party at Q4. Score Q5 apart, since a miss there points at the key. After the click into R10, a rater who says the more-and-less chain in order passes the loop view.

## 8. What the references teach

- **Loop Overviews Annotated, left:** Each thumbnail is the loop itself, so shape reads at a glance. Unsigned arrows show that things connect without saying how.
- **Loop Overviews Annotated, right:** It carries the most text per loop of any reference. Identical circle glyphs leave nothing to tell R10 from R12.
- **Concept 07, Loop Card R10:** The best teaching page, with verbs on the wires and a "Read it aloud" line. It marks only L25 in red, though R-01 targets L08, L25 and C2.
- **Concept 09, The Circuit:** Cards listing each area's variables teach scope fast. The deck asks a newcomer to hold seven connection counts and to read "from left to right".
- **Civic Architecture Layers:** Stacked layers and a breadcrumb teach the levels. Upward arrows teach a hierarchy the model lacks.
- **Civic Architecture R8 Loop:** Named steps and the reform under the return line teach a loop well. The red plus on L39 and "(positive)" in the side panel invite misreading 1.
- **Civic Circuit Grouped Overview:** Five areas on one ring read in a second and teach that the system is one loop. Its R8 text departs from L39.
- **Civic Landscape:** "Find your place in the system." is the most inviting first line here. Invented variables such as "Housing affordability" describe a different model.
- **Decision Grid:** Clean boxes and a one-sentence R8 explanation read fast. Flow words on one ring teach a single route.
- **Fine Structure Dark:** The 3D boxes add depth without meaning, and small low-contrast text spends the first seconds.
