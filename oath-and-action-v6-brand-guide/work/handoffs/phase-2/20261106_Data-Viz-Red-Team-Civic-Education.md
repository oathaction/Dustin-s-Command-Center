# Data visualization round: civic education review

Oath & Action Brand Guide, Edition 06. Phase 2, November 6, 2026. Red team lens 2, civic education. Written by the civic education reviewer for the main session and the creative director.

I read each concept as a first-time adult or high school reader would meet it, with no training in charts. For every specimen I asked three things: can the reader say in one sentence what it shows, can they tell how to read it, and can they tell how sure it is. I then read each concept's `system` view, and its "How to read our charts" most closely, as the guide a teacher would hand out. Scores run from 1 (does not teach) to 5 (teaches with no help). One standard applies to all three. Classroom judgments below are mine; no students or readers took part.

## 1. Verdict per concept

### 01 Explainer: 4

Explainer teaches at the point of need. Each evidence label carries a plain sentence about where the numbers came from ("Official statistic: what survey respondents reported"), notes sit beside the marks they explain, and S04 is the only chart in the round that tells a reader what a missing margin of error means: "a change of a point or two may sit within survey error." Its "How to read our charts" is the only guide that defines all four evidence kinds and says which title words signal doubt. It leaves out glosses the other two print: what cloture is, what the Supplemental Poverty Measure counts, what ranked-choice voting is, and what the S07 map cannot tell a city.

**Score 4.** The best teacher of the three, held back by one misleading map and four missing glosses.

### 02 Register: 3

Register's tables are the best look-up material for a class, and its contents table puts every reader question and evidence kind on the first screen. It prints the plain glosses Explainer lacks (cloture, the two poverty measures, ranked-choice voting, the limits of the S07 map), and its S06 arrows survive a black-and-white copy. Its S10 title claims more than a count of claims can show, its reader guide defines two of the four evidence kinds and never says what red means, and several notes speak to designers ("Light fills fall under 3:1 against the ground").

**Score 3.** Strong reference pages with a thin reader guide and one overclaiming title.

### 03 Instrument: 3

Instrument gives a reader who wants to check the most tools: a Chart and Table switch on every module, record numbers with a line on how to search them, labeled method breaks on S02, and an S08 header that shows 31 log entries across sixty years beside 33 in two. The evidence slot drops the grade words for codes no reader can decode ("Atlas sources N40, K1908, W035"), its guide names the evidence kinds without saying what any of them means, and on S10 the plausible and contested textures cannot be told apart, so the one chart about certainty does not show it.

**Score 3.** Good tools for checking, weak at telling a reader how sure each chart is.

## 2. Findings per concept

### 01 Explainer

**Must fix**

**EX-M1. S07, White and Newsprint, every width.** The reader question asks where a city can adopt ranked-choice voting. The key reads "No state ban" for 31 states and DC, and nothing visible says that a state without a ban may still give its cities no way to adopt it, or what ranked-choice voting is. Ohio's home-rule opening appears only inside the closed table. A reader will take every light square as a place where a city may adopt it, which the source does not say. Fix: add Register's two notes, "A state without a ban may still give its cities no way to adopt ranked-choice voting. The map shows bans only." and "Ranked-choice voting lets voters rank candidates; the count runs in rounds until one has a majority.", and set the Ohio line as a note on its tile.

**Should fix**

**EX-S1. S01.** The Senate bar reads "53 for" and "47 against", then "7 votes short of the 60 needed". A student who sees 53 beat 47 and still fail will ask why, and the chart never says. Register answers it in one line: "Cloture is the Senate vote that ends debate. Taking up this bill needed three-fifths of the Senate, 60 votes." Fix: add that line under the Senate bar. Change "The House passed the ban" to "The House passed a ban, H.R. 7008", since the survey asked about a ban three years before this bill reached a vote.

**EX-S2. S02.** The two lines are labeled "Official measure" and "Supplemental measure" and nothing says what either counts. The chart's story (the supplemental line fell in 2021 and the official line barely moved) only makes sense once a reader knows the supplemental measure counts tax credits. Fix: use Register's labels, "Official measure, which counts pretax cash income only" and "Supplemental Poverty Measure, which adds tax credits and other aid".

**EX-S3. S03 and S08.** The meaning of "toward voters" and "away from voters" sits in a closed disclosure, "How the atlas codes direction". Those words carry a judgment, and the reader meets them in the title. Fix: print a one-sentence definition as a visible note, as Register and Instrument do, and keep the full coding note in the disclosure.

**EX-S4. S04.** The dot scale starts at 40 percent, and the measure line ("Citizens who reported voting in November, percent, by age group, 2020 and 2024 presidential elections, United States") does not say so. Explainer's own anatomy says the measure line "says when a scale is cropped." A reader fresh from S01's 0 to 100 bars may read the 18 to 24 dot as about half the 65 and over dot. Fix: end the measure line with "; the scale starts at 40 percent".

**EX-S5. System, How to read, item 1, against S02 and S05.** The guide says "An Estimate says what would happen if something else happened first." S02 and S05 then print "FINDING" over "Official statistic: Census Bureau survey estimates". A careful student will ask which it is. Fix: change the grade line to "Official statistic: a Census Bureau survey", and see SH-S2 for a line on survey error.

**EX-S6. S09.** The title "Fifteen of the 24 rule changes the atlas dates since 1870 fall in two stretches: 1964 to 1978 and 2010 to 2026." and the two shaded bands point at clusters in the atlas's own choice of 24 events. The measure line says "the atlas chose which to date", and students will still read the bands as a fact about history. The title also answers when, where the reader question asks which. Fix: title the chart with what it holds ("The atlas traces 24 rule changes since 1870 on four threads: who counts, who governs, who owns and who checks") and drop the bands, or label them "Where the atlas's picks cluster".

**Polish**

**EX-P1. S05.** The bracket figures "28.7", "25.8", "27.3" and "29.6" print with no unit, at the height of the 50 to 60 percent gridlines, so they can read as rates. Fix: "27.3 pts", as Register prints "29.6 pts".

**EX-P2. S08.** The first column, "To 2012", holds 48 years beside one column for each year after it, and nothing visible says so. On the 390 px drawing the label also runs into its squares. Fix: a note such as "The first column holds every entry from 1965 to 2012", and clear the label.

**EX-P3. S10.** Rows print topic names ("Clean drinking water"). Register prints the reader's question under each ("Who makes sure my tap water is safe?"), which tells a student what each answer covers. Fix: add the question under the name.

### 02 Register

**Must fix**

**RG-M1. S10, every view.** The title reads "Documented fact carries most of every everyday answer, from 59 percent of the claims on algorithms to 96 percent on tap water." The source line beneath it says "a claim's grade covers that claim, not the whole answer." A count of claims cannot show how much of an answer rests on fact, and "documented" means on the public record, as the legend says. A reader will repeat that the atlas's answers are mostly fact. Fix: "Most claims behind every everyday answer are documented on the public record, from 59 percent for algorithms to 96 percent for tap water."

**RG-M2. System, "How to read our charts".** Item 1 defines two of the four kinds: "A Finding is measured; an Estimate depends on assumptions." Item 2 says "its verb says how sure the evidence is" and never says which verbs. No item says what the red point on S02 means, or what "documented record", "official statistic", "self-reported" and "secondary compilation" mean after the label. A reader with no training cannot use this guide to judge how sure a chart is, which is the guide's main job. Fix: define all four kinds in a sentence each (Explainer's item 1 works), say that "would" and "may" mark less certain claims, add Explainer's red line ("When a chart uses red, it marks the single point the title names. Red never means a party, good or bad."), and add one line glossing the grade words.

**Should fix**

**RG-S1. S04.** Note 3 reads "The table gives no margins of error." and stops, while note 2 says "Every group under 65 reported voting less in 2024 than in 2020". A reader is not told what a missing margin means for a change of 1.0 point. Fix: add Explainer's clause, "so a change of a point or two may sit within survey error."

**RG-S2. S06 and S10 notes, and "V7" in reader text.** Notes written for designers reach readers: "Up is Verdigris and down is Prussian", "Grades never take red.", and "Light fills fall under 3:1 against the ground; the table under "Show the table" prints every count, a studio judgment." Readers do not know palette names or contrast ratios. Reader-facing lines also say "V7" ("in Atlas V7's words", "The instruments are V7's", "the studio's reading of V7's words"). Fix: move the palette and contrast lines to the system sheet, and write "the atlas" for V7.

**RG-S3. Every chart.** No chart carries a credit. Explainer and Instrument print "Chart: Oath & Action" beside every source line. A chart that travels alone does not say who made it, the first thing a civics class asks of any graphic. Fix: add the credit to the source row.

**RG-S4. S09.** "The atlas dates today's rules to 24 changes since 1870, six on each of four threads." "Dates today's rules to" is hard to parse, and "six on each" describes how the atlas built its list. Fix: use Instrument's shape, "The atlas traces 24 documented rule changes on four threads, from the Fifteenth Amendment in 1870 to Trump v. Slaughter in 2026."

**RG-S5. S08.** The title gives only one side: "Seventeen of the 24 rule changes the atlas codes as moving away from voters came in 2025 and 2026." The 8 toward-voters changes in the same years sit in note 1. Fix: name both counts, as Explainer does ("Since January 2025, 17 changes in the atlas's log moved the rules away from voters and 8 moved them toward voters."), and see SH-M1.

**Polish**

**RG-P1. S02.** The leader from "Supplemental Poverty Measure, which adds tax credits and other aid" runs about 300 px on a diagonal across both lines to the 2010 point, and reads as a third series. Fix: set the label beside its line.

**RG-P2. S10.** The column "Points from all 433 claims" (+21.7 down to −15.0) needs a gloss. Fix: head it "Documented share compared with all claims (74.1%)".

**RG-P3. S07.** "On wide screens two digits under a code give the year the ban was enacted" speaks about screens to a reader who may hold a still image. Fix: "Two digits under a code give the year the ban became law."

### 03 Instrument

**Must fix**

**IN-M1. Slot A, every module and view.** The label reads "FINDING" or "ESTIMATE" alone. The right of the slot holds "Atlas sources N40, K1908, W035", "Census P60-290 Tables 3 and 8; atlas RL15, RL16" and "Census HVS Table 22". No module says in words whether its numbers are an official statistic, a survey's self-report, a secondary compilation or the atlas's own coding. Nine of ten modules read "FINDING", so the kind alone separates nothing, and the guide explains RL and PW codes but not N, K or W codes. A reader cannot tell how sure any module is from its label. Fix: add the grade in words after the kind, as Explainer does ("Official statistic: what survey respondents reported"; "Secondary compilation: Ballotpedia, checked against the atlas"), and replace N, K and W codes with source names or move them into the table.

**IN-M2. S10, White, Newsprint, Charcoal, 1440 and 390 px.** Plausible claims are drawn as "2 px dashes, 8 on and 4 off" and contested claims as "dash-dot at 8, 3, 2 and 3". Both render as rows of short dashes inside thin boxes. I rendered the module at twice size and still could not find the boundary between the two where both sit in one bar (Grocery prices, Housing costs, Pay and working conditions). The chart exists to show how sure each answer is, and the guide teaches "dashes mean plausible, dash-dot means contested." Fix: give contested a fill that differs at a glance, such as Explainer's diagonal hatch or Register's light fill with a hatch, and print the contested count at the end of each bar where it is above zero.

**Should fix**

**IN-S1. S01.** The title reads "The Senate fell 7 votes short of taking up a stock-trading ban that 88% of Democrats and 87% of Republicans favored in 2023." The reader question asks whether the parties agree, and the title answers what the Senate did. It also ties a 2023 survey about a ban in general to a 2026 bill, as if voters had judged that bill. Teaching the difference between support for an idea and support for a bill is basic civics. Fix: lead with the agreement, as Explainer and Register do ("Nearly nine in ten Republican and Democratic voters favored a ban on stock trading by members of Congress in 2023."), and let the drawing carry the votes.

**IN-S2. S04.** The title ends "every group under 65 fell from 2020", and the module says nothing about survey error. The 45 to 64 change is 1.0 point. Fix: add Explainer's sentence on survey error, and end the title on the change the data carries most clearly: "the youngest fell most, by 3.7 points".

**IN-S3. System, How to read.** Item 1 lists "a finding, an association, an estimate or an illustration" without saying what any means. No item says which title words signal doubt. The seven items run with no lead words, so a reader cannot scan them. Fix: use Explainer's items 1 and 2, and open each item with a bold lead phrase.

**IN-S4. S07.** No visible line says that each square is one state set on a grid at one size; the studio strip says it, and the strip is not chart copy. No line says what ranked-choice voting is. A reader new to tile maps looks for state shapes. Fix: add "Each square is one state, whatever its size." and Register's definition of ranked-choice voting.

**IN-S5. S05.** The title and the Black panel give the Black gap. Nothing visible gives the Hispanic gap, which narrowed from 28.7 points in 2003 to 25.8 in 2025. The reader question asks whether the gap closed, and for one group it narrowed. Fix: add a note in the Hispanic panel, as Register's note 1 does.

**IN-S6. S02.** The measure line ends "Supplemental Poverty Measure in Charcoal, official measure in Stone". Readers do not know palette names. Fix: "in black" and "in gray".

**Polish**

**IN-P1. S07, S08 and S09.** "Point at a state to read its law.", "Point at a square to read its record." and "Point at a dot to read the event." A phone reader taps. Fix: "Tap or point at".

**IN-P2. S08.** Squares print "03", "04", "05". The measure line says "numbered by its record", and a reader may still take them for counts. Fix: "numbered by its record (03 is RL03, row 3 of the log)".

**IN-P3. S03 and S04.** Both titles join two clauses with a semicolon and run past 20 words. A reader cannot say either in one breath. Fix: keep the first clause as the title and move the second into a note.

## 3. Shared findings

**SH-M1. Must fix, all three. S03 and S08 teach the repair log as a count of rule changes.** The S08 question is "Have the rules been moving toward voters or away from them?", and every concept answers from the atlas's log of 64 entries. No visible line says the log holds only the changes the atlas chose to record. Explainer comes closest ("The log is the atlas's record and runs denser in recent years."), and its plain line, "counts what the atlas recorded, not every rule change", sits inside the closed table. Instrument's headers show the density ("31 changes" for 1965 to 2024, "33 changes" for 2025 and 2026) and do not say why. Register says "Counts are entries in the atlas's log." A class will take these charts as a measure of where American rules have gone. Fix, on both charts in all three: "The log holds the 64 changes the atlas chose to record, not every rule change in the country. It records more of recent years: 33 of the 64 entries fall in 2025 and 2026."

**SH-S1. Should fix, all three. Readers cannot find "How to read our charts".** It is the last section of a builder's sheet, below hex tables and Rule 05.3. Its heading sits 7,554 px down at 1440 px and 17,583 px down at 390 px on Explainer, 8,311 and 21,521 px on Register, and 8,652 and 15,812 px on Instrument. No chart links to it. Register's guide says it is "linked from every chart", and no link exists. Only Explainer's view list names it ("the rules on one sheet, and how to read our charts"). Fix: link "How to read our charts" from every chart's source row, and set the guide as a short block near the top of the White view as well as on the system sheet.

**SH-S2. Should fix, all three. No guide teaches survey error.** S02, S04 and S05 come from Census surveys. Register prints margins ("Margins of error run from 0.4 to 0.9 points at the 90 percent level") and never says what a margin is. Explainer's S04 line is the only one that tells a reader what to do with it. Fix: one guide line, such as "Survey numbers come from a sample of people, so each has a margin of error. A margin of 0.5 points means the true figure most likely sits within half a point either way, and a smaller change may be chance."

**SH-S3. Should fix, all three. The coding judgments behind S03 and S08 stay out of sight.** Register and Instrument never say that a failed ballot measure counts as "voters kept existing rules" whichever way it leaned, which is part of why "none of 17" initiatives moved the rules away from voters; Explainer says "Eight failed, which left the rules in force." None shows the coding note's own caution on the ranked-choice and open-primary entries: "readers may weigh these entries differently". Only Explainer says that RL47 counts 19 state bans as one entry. Fix: one visible note on each chart carrying these three points in plain words.

**SH-S4. Should fix, all three. S06 asks readers to judge estimates they cannot see or decode.** Register and Instrument rest the title on "if their assumptions hold" and never show an assumption; their tables give the atlas's reason for each cell and leave the assumptions out. Explainer's table lists them ("The Senate keeps the legislative filibuster"). All three leave "fusion voting", "inspectors general", "Forbearance" and "Government efficacy" unglossed, and none says that "up" means better for every family (for income and wealth equality, more equal). Fix: Register and Instrument add each scenario's assumptions to the table; all three add one line under the title, "Up means better for each family", and a short gloss for each scenario name.

**SH-S5. Should fix, Explainer and Instrument. S06 tints lose direction on a black-and-white copy.** Explainer's notes measure its up tint at #BDBDBD and its down tint at #B6B6B6 in grayscale. In both grayscale captures the "Down" cells of the baseline row and the "Up" cells of row iii print as close mid-grays, and only a small triangle carries direction. Teachers photocopy in black and white. Register's boxed arrows survive the grayscale capture. Fix: drop the tint, or adopt Register's boxed arrows at a size that reads on a copy.

**SH-S6. Should fix, all three. S05's gray lines are never named.** Each panel draws the other three groups in gray, and no line says so. A first-time reader in the "All households" panel sees three unlabeled lines. Fix: one note in the first panel, "Gray lines: the other three groups."

**SH-S7. Should fix, all three. S09 never says what any event did.** Names such as "Shelby County v. Holder", "McGovern-Fraser rules" and "Nuclear option for nominations" mean nothing to a new reader. All three tables carry a "What it did" column ("Barred race as a voting qualification..."). Fix: print the first clause of "What it did" under each name in Instrument's lists and in the phone layouts of the other two.

**SH-S8. Should fix, Register and Instrument. S02's title sets the credit beside the low.** Register writes "while the expanded child tax credit was paid" and Instrument "the year the expanded child tax credit paid out"; the Census caution sits in a note below the chart. Readers take the title as the claim, and timing in a title reads as cause. Explainer keeps the credit out of its title and sets the caution at the 2021 point. Fix: follow Explainer, and write the caution plainly in all three: "The timing alone does not show how much of the drop came from the credit."

**SH-S9. Should fix, all three. The social cards carry no address.** Each card keeps its source line, and none tells a reader where to find the table, the notes or the full chart. A card in a feed travels without its page. Fix: add a short web address at the foot of each card.

**SH-P1. Polish, all three. "The studio" means nothing to a reader.** Reader-facing notes say "The studio grouped an agency rule..." (Explainer S03), "The grouping is the studio's" (Instrument S03), "the studio's reading" (every S06) and "The split at January 1, 2025 is the studio's" (Instrument S08). Fix: "Oath & Action's reading", "our grouping".

## 4. Which concept goes to a classroom

I would hand Explainer to a classroom. Its labels explain themselves in a sentence, its notes teach the odd encoding where a student is looking, its S04 says what survey error means for a small change, its S06 table lists the assumptions behind every estimate, and its seven-step guide is the one I could print as a handout with light edits. Before it goes, it needs EX-M1, SH-M1 and four lines borrowed from Register: the cloture note on S01, the two measure labels on S02, the ranked-choice definition and map limit on S07, and the boxed S06 arrows that survive a photocopy. Register's tables suit a second lesson on looking things up.
