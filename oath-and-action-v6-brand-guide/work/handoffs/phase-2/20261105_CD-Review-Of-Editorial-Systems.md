# Creative director review: the five editorial atlas systems

Oath & Action Brand Guide, Edition 06. Phase 2, November 5, 2026. The creative director (`oa-creative-director`, run as a subagent), after the fix pass, for Board 2. A system ships only at 4 or above on every line.

## Decision or verdict

**Pick System 1, Times Explainer.** It answers Dustin's request most directly. The first screen asks "Who do they answer to?" and draws all eight parts with the flows between them; the page then walks the circuit part by part, naming each flow in a sentence, with every condition, loop, problem and reform one click down. It grew from the Editorial Desk he liked and fixes the two faults he named in it. It clears all seven lines. Its weakness is recognition: with the wordmark covered it could belong to any good newsroom, so the finishing work gives it the layer rule and Newsprint ground of his Concepts 07 and 09.

**Runner-up: System 3, Reference Sheets.** It is the best-made system here and the closest to Concept 09, and its R10 sheet teaches the rule behind a loop better than any other page. It also clears all seven lines. Codes, sheet numbers and technical words make it read as a manual to a first-time reader, and Dustin set The New York Times and The Economist as the bar.

After the fix pass the five first screens show nearly the same circuit, so the choice rests on the pages beneath it.

## Reasons

### 1. Times Explainer

- Craft 4: assured type and clean pills at 2x; one label runs 23 px past the Policy box in the lower overview drawing.
- Clarity 5: the first screen shows the system, then each flow is named in a sentence ("5 connections run from Wealth to Voice, such as...").
- Brand 4: locked palette and type, red only on R-01's targets; with the wordmark covered, nothing says Oath & Action.
- Usefulness 5: every question panel links its parts, loops and reforms.
- Evidence 4: drafts marked, stance box atop R-01; the rent answer has no source.
- Accessibility 4: panel as a column, skip link, nothing under 13 px at 320 px.
- Beauty 4: its Elections drawing, two loops meeting at risk of losing office, is the best in the set; the plain White boxes carry no signature.

At its best it teaches the way a great newspaper graphic does, in order, with numbers explained in words. Anonymity and length hold it back: the overview runs about eight desktop screens.

### 2. Economist Register

- Craft 4: a tight register with textured evidence bars; the readout runs words together ("influenceWhich").
- Clarity 4: a strong first screen; the catalog's vertical chains read as forms.
- Brand 3: on R10 and R-01, Prussian "R-01" tabs sit in red-outlined boxes beside red arrows, a red and blue pair on the pages about the editor's own cause. Instruction: set those tabs in Charcoal wherever red works.
- Usefulness 5: one table compares all eight parts at once.
- Evidence 4: disclosures wherever a reform is argued; shared rent-source gap.
- Accessibility 4: 44 px phone targets; step sentences now chain correctly.
- Beauty 4: a weekly's data page; 23 vertical chains grow monotonous.

At its best a reader compares every part at once and sees how sure the atlas is about each. Density and the red and blue pairing hold it back.

### 3. Reference Sheets

- Craft 5: text measured in the browser, crossings minimized by search; the most considered detail at 2x.
- Clarity 4: the clearest circuit and best loop lesson; codes and a thesis headline cost a newcomer.
- Brand 4: Newsprint and layer rules carry Concept 09; the circled sign still needs a decision-log entry.
- Usefulness 5: issue filters, an index on every sheet and a conventions sheet a producer can build from.
- Evidence 4: R10's headline names the plausible step; the catalog still says "18 feed themselves" without "can".
- Accessibility 4: jumps keep the view's address; issue rows take Tab.
- Beauty 4: museum-grade documentation that reads cool to a household reader.

At its best it is exact and ownable, and it would make the strongest systems chapter in the guide. A first-time reader meets IDs and sheet numbers before stories.

### 4. Night Edition

- Craft 4: clean cards, luminous lines; the overview draws the system twice.
- Clarity 4: eight parts in the first screen; the two-question headline takes half of it and the counts are faint.
- Brand 4: Charcoal ground, Civic Line icons as drawn, red discipline clean.
- Usefulness 5: every name opens its full record; problems now come before reforms.
- Evidence 3: the paragraph under "The full map" says wealth and market power "decide who gets heard" and "Policy then tilts toward insiders", stating graded links as fact. Instruction: rewrite with "can", matched to the grades.
- Accessibility 4: visible skip link, issues as Tab stops; the heaviest page at 1.49 MB.
- Beauty 5: the most striking page here; the R10 page and the icon loop cards are portfolio work.

At its best it is Dustin's dark atlas finished to a studio standard. One overclaiming paragraph holds it back, and long pages tire on Charcoal.

### 5. Architecture

- Craft 3: the glossary collapses to one word per line at every width, and the phone route table hides its check dates. Instruction: a two-column definition list; stack the route table on phones.
- Clarity 3: the stack teaches a ranking the model lacks, Wealth at the ground and Policy on the roof. Instruction: draw the first-screen glance as the circuit and keep the building one level down.
- Brand 4: icons exact, red clean.
- Usefulness 5: 16 questions lead the way in, and the panel lights what each one reaches.
- Evidence 4: disclosures on issue panels; shared rent-source gap.
- Accessibility 4: the best panel, a column inside the view.
- Beauty 4: icon tiles make the building the most ownable drawing below the fold.

At its best it has the warmest way in and the plainest glosses, such as "Why it matters" under each condition. The metaphor itself ranks the parts.

## Risks for Dustin to weigh

- System 4 is the most striking page and grew from the dark atlas Dustin built. As the main system it would make every future map dark-first, which limits print and reports; as the pick's dark mode it keeps what he made.

- The five first screens now look alike, so the board should show each system's R10 and Elections pages beside its overview.
