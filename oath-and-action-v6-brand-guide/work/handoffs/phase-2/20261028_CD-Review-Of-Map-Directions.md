## 1. Verdict

No direction meets Dustin's bar as it stands. All three are rigorous and honest. All three draw the loop as technical notation: black and gray linework, sign discs, tick marks, pointer triangles and small italic labels. A first-time reader meets ten to twenty marks per loop before meeting an idea. The catalog pages are even grids of small drawings with orphan rows, and no view uses scale, fills or the brand's four surfaces to build a composition. A renowned studio would show the verification work and keep the drawings in the archive.

The board's recommendation, Orbit loops with Weave's fabric as the whole-system map, goes to Dustin as the record of round one, together with the round-two brief in section 4, and stays open until round two reports. The current system stays the fallback under G1-11.

Round one proved that the studio can draw any treatment from V7 by script and prove every mark matches the model. The brief below puts that capability to work on composition, scale and a shape a newcomer reads without a key.

**Current system, beauty 3.** The Concepts V2 plate has hierarchy, a reading key and a calm rhythm, and the Concept 09 Circuit has a poster's structure. It is handsome and quiet, and it is what Dustin already has.

**Orbit** (board section `#dir-orbit`)
- Craft 3. - Accessibility 4. Real 390 px phone pages exist and pass the checks. On the board at 390 px, the ring map shows only a fragment inside a sideways scroller.

## 3. What holds each direction back

### Shared

- **Notation budget.** Every scale carries the full vocabulary plus four studio additions. In `20261028_Orbit-Mini-R10.svg` I count 4 ID discs, 4 sign labels with 2 record squares, 4 tick sets, 6 pointers, 4 arrowheads and 1 "weakest". A miniature needs its shape, its kind, its line textures and its names. The rest belongs on the card.
- **Pages built as grids.** All three catalogs are equal cells in a four- or five-column grid. None has a lead specimen, a change of scale, an area band or a reading guide on the page itself; the key sits in a separate "Shared marks" section. R16 and B5 sit alone on their rows.
- **Surfaces.** The catalogs sit on Newsprint, the cards on White and the decisions panel on Prussian. No drawing uses Charcoal or Prussian as a ground, and no drawing uses a fill beyond ID discs and tiles.
- **The board at 390 px.** The rubric table squeezes the reason column out of view and leaves rows hundreds of pixels tall. The in-board R8 cards and whole maps scroll sideways and open on a fragment, such as a red arc and one label. Exploration captions are clipped.

### Orbit

- Catalog, `#dir-orbit`: the bow that gives each loop "its own shape" is too slight to see, so the page reads as one ring repeated with two, three or four stations, which is the fault the board found in the current system.
- `20261028_Orbit-Card-R8.svg`: the ring sits in a large White field with step labels at the far left and right and an empty lower quadrant. The card has no picture of the loop's footprint in the record, though V7 names a series for every loop in `reference_mode`.
- `20261028_Orbit-Whole-System-Map.svg`: a hairball of chords across one ring of 51 variables.

### Line

- Catalog, `#dir-line`: the miniature is a set of ID tiles, so it drops the names that make the R8 card readable. It misses Dustin's literal ask, a smaller image of the card.
- `20261028_Line-Card-R8.svg`: Concept 08 re-set to Gate 1 words. Little is gained over what Dustin has.
- `20261028_Line-Whole-System-Map.svg`: long arcs fan at the hub variables, stations show IDs only, and the area labels mix letters and words.
- The phone layout exists in words only.

### Weave

- Catalog, `#dir-weave`: the miniatures lose the loop. Nothing in them comes back around.
- `20261028_Weave-Card-R8.svg`: the loop's own two threads sit small at center left, and the six stubs to the rest of the system carry IDs a newcomer cannot read.
- `20261028_Weave-Whole-System-Map.svg`: about two thirds of the width holds connections on no loop, and the 20 loop bands are squeezed into narrow slivers at the left. `20261028_Recommended-Reform-View-R-01-Fabric.svg` turns the whole C2 row red.
- The phone layout exists in words only.

## 4. Round-two brief

### Push, drop and keep

- Drop Weave as a direction. Keep the fabric as the register for whichever direction wins.
- Drop Orbit's delay bow and ring map. Its single geometry function and its ring move into Direction B.
- Drop Line's miniature and arc map. Its R8 card moves into Direction A.
- Retire the four studio additions at miniature size: the delay bow, the sign label at its smallest, the reform pointer and the loop-kind arrow. Dustin has not approved them, and the new directions replace them.
- Develop three directions, and no new explorations: A, Field Guide, which honors the literal ask; B, Snowball and Thermostat, a bold loop shape; C, Loop Lines, a bold whole-system map. I expect C to win.

### Rules for all three directions

**Surfaces and red (Rule 05.3, D2-01).**
- Catalog page: Newsprint ground with White card tiles. It names no single reform, so it carries no working red.
- Loop card and reform view: White. Pillar Red marks only the targets of the one reform the view names: on R8, R-30 at L39 and A5; on R-01's view, L25, L08 and C2. Pillar Red measures 4.17:1 on White and 2.96:1 on Newsprint.
- Whole-system poster: Prussian, with Newsprint ink (9.42:1) and White ink (13.28:1). Red never sits on Prussian, so a reform view of the map moves to White. Stone measures 2.20:1 on Prussian and Verdigris 2.80:1, so neither carries content there.
- Charcoal: feature bands such as page heads and the "how to read" strip, with Newsprint ink (13.29:1).

**Scale and type.**
- Display: Inter Tight 800 at 96 px for page heads and counts at 1440 px, 56 px at 390 px. Card headlines 44 px and 30 px.
- Labels: Inter, with a 13 px floor; station names 15 to 16 px semibold.
- The read-aloud sentence quotes V7's `how_to_read`, so it is set in Lora Italic, the locked face for editorial quotation, at 20 px on cards. Drawings carry no other italic.
- Stroke weight stays constant within a view. Master Brief section 14 allows width only for a defined measure.
- No label is truncated. A long name wraps to two lines.
- Every row of cards fills. No row holds one card alone.

### Direction A: Field Guide

**Creative idea.** Dustin's reference card came from Concept 08, the Loop Field Guide. Round two takes that name at its word. Each loop is a specimen card drawn one way, and the catalog is a set of field guide plates where every card is the R8 card at a smaller size. The page head teaches one card at full size with callouts, and a reader then reads all 23 the way a birder reads a guide.

**What a first-time reader grasps without a key.** Boxes are things that change. The line across the top runs forward, and the line underneath brings the last change back to the first box.

**Miniature.** The R8 card's drawing at about 40 percent, in a 300 by 220 px tile at 1440 px. Boxes 120 by 44 px with a 4 px Charcoal spine and V7's chain label in Inter 13 semibold, wrapping to two lines. Two steps: two boxes side by side with the return underneath. Three steps: two boxes over one. Four steps: two over two, a rectangle route clockwise from top left. The step count gives each loop a different silhouette. Each line carries its sign in a 16 px disc, as in Concept 08, and its M1 texture. Caption: kind in words, one turn in words, least certain step by ID and grade.

**R8 card.** White. An editorial headline that names the mechanism, in Inter Tight 800 at 44 px; Concept 08's "How housing scarcity sustains itself" is the model, and the editor writes it from V7's `how_to_read`. Under it, V7's title with the Loop R8 chip, then the read-aloud sentence in Lora Italic. Two boxes with "Higher:" rules at 16 px. L38 runs forward in Charcoal with its sign, grade word and ticks. L39 returns underneath in Pillar Red, and A5's box takes a red keyline, because R-30 acts there. The right rail keeps Concept 08's order: feeds itself, what the return line means, least certain step, one turn, now. The foot carries R-30's effect pips with no total and its routes with check dates. The masthead drops "From spectators to stewards." (G1-01). On a phone the boxes stack, and the return line runs up the left side.

**Whole-system map.** Concept 09, The Circuit, redrawn to this bar; Dustin's saved screenshots favor 09. Five area cards on a fixed grid, A to E left to right in the order effects mostly travel, economy and technology in a lower band, drivers as tabs on the top edge. Inside each card, variables on loops are boxes in the card style. The 46 connections on named loops are individual wires on a 24 px orthogonal grid with one corner radius. The other 72 are counted bundles between cards, as today. In the live atlas one loop lights in Charcoal and the rest drop to Stone (4.29:1 on Newsprint).

**Catalog page.** A Charcoal head: "Twenty-three loops" in Newsprint Inter Tight 800 at 96 px, one deck line, and R8 at full card size with five hairline callouts (the box, the forward line, the return line, the sign, the texture). Then one plate per area. Each plate opens with a band holding the area letter at 120 px, its name, a one-line deck and its count. The area's lead specimen spans two columns at double size: R8 for A, and elsewhere the loop with the most reforms V7 lists, a rule a reader can hear in one sentence. The rest sit at single size in four columns with 24 px gutters. When a plate would leave one card alone, the lead card spans three columns. The sketched loops close the page as their own plate.

**Color, scale and type.** Newsprint ground, White tiles, Charcoal head and bands; Prussian unused. Type as in the shared rules.

**Keep from the approved vocabulary.** M1 textures and the hollow line at every scale, the record square on the card; M2 words in captions and callouts; M3 chips; M4 ticks, pips and the authority diamond on the card; M5's 07 Loop Card and 09 Circuit bases.

**Risk.** It is the direction closest to what Dustin has, so its beauty has to come from the page composition and the finish.

### Direction B: Snowball and Thermostat

**Creative idea.** The shape tells the reader what the loop does before any word. A loop that feeds itself is one turn of a spiral: it leaves its first variable, passes each step and arrives back at the same variable one lap further out, so the start appears twice. A loop that pushes back is a ring that closes on its start. The current catalog's key already prints the words: "Snowball loop: each turn builds on the last." and "Thermostat loop: the system pushes back."

**What a first-time reader grasps without a key.** Going around the shape follows the steps in order. A shape that comes back further out builds on itself. A shape that closes on itself settles.

**Honesty rules.** Every reinforcing loop uses the same geometry: one turn of an Archimedean spiral from radius r to 1.3 r, stations at equal angles clockwise from twelve o'clock. Every balancing loop is a circle at r. Angle names the variable, and the outward step means one more turn and never an amount. Each catalog prints the line "Every snowball is drawn the same. The shape shows the kind of loop and never its speed or strength." V7's `direction` field (running up on 10 loops, running down on 6, and the cases V7 calls running, disabled or a design target) appears in words only. V7's `valence`, its note on harm and benefit, never sets a shape or a color.

**Miniature.** 150 px at 1440 px, 120 px at 390 px. Stations are 26 px Charcoal discs with the ID in Newsprint. The first station's echo sits on the outer lap as an outlined disc with the same ID. Each segment carries its M1 texture, with no signs, ticks or pointers. B2, B3 and R9 take the same shape in the hollow line with open stations.

**R8 card.** A 520 px spiral on White: A5 at twelve o'clock, A6 at six, and A5's echo at twelve on the outer lap, labeled "Housing scarcity, one turn later". Station names sit horizontal outside the curve in Inter 16 semibold with their "Higher:" rules. Step names run outside each segment with the sign, grade word and ticks. L39 and A5's disc take Pillar Red for R-30. Beside the spiral, the "Now" panel prints "Running up" and V7's series, "Owner-renter net worth gap (SCF) 1989 to 2022". A chart of that series appears only when the research lead supplies sourced values with their period. Below sit the step list and the R-30 panel, as in A.

**Whole-system map.** B has no map form of its own, because a spiral cannot share a station with six other loops on one drawing. It uses Direction C's poster, with the spiral and the ring as line bullets. The board says so plainly.

**Catalog page.** A poster wall. The head on Charcoal sets one spiral and one ring at 240 px in Newsprint beside "18 feed themselves" and "5 push back" in Inter Tight 800 at 96 px. Area rows follow on Newsprint, shapes at 150 px with 48 px gaps, each with its chip, title and one caption line. The five thermostats stand out by shape inside their area rows; the live atlas can sort by kind.

**Color, scale and type.** Newsprint and Charcoal; Prussian unused on this page; red on the card only.

**Keep from the approved vocabulary.** M1 to M4 as in A. The spiral and the ring at 24 px become the loop-kind mark in place of the studio's arrow, pending Dustin's approval.

**Test first.** Draw R8, R10 and B4 at miniature and card size before the full set, and run the reader check on them. If the spiral reads as a drain, a target or runaway growth, stop and fold the ring into A.

### Direction C: Loop Lines

**Creative idea.** Draw the loop network as a transit map. Each numbered loop is a closed line, each variable on a loop is a station, and a variable on several loops is an interchange. V7 suits this form: the 20 numbered loops run through 25 variables, all in the five core areas. E1 sits on seven loops, and C3 and C4 on four each. Six connections carry more than one loop, L42 carries three, and lines run side by side there. The catalog miniature is each loop's own line lifted from the map, so each loop's shape comes from where its variables live in the system.

**What a first-time reader grasps without a key.** A loop is a line that runs in a circle. Ride it and you come back where you got on. Where lines meet, loops share a variable.

**Grid.** Fixed station positions on an octilinear grid (0, 45 and 90 degrees), one corner radius on every bend after INAT, and even station spacing, because V7 holds no distances and delay never sets length. Districts A to E are fields named on the map itself. E sits at the center because seven loops pass through E1, with A to D around it in the order effects mostly travel. Economy, technology and the outside drivers form the outer frame, with an entry tick and a count where their connections reach the core. Station positions live in one 25-row table that the verifier reads, and lines are routed by script from V7.

**Lines.** One ink for all 20, because 20 loops cannot each own a hue and grade, sign and kind must survive grayscale. Each line carries its M3 chip as a bullet at its first station, with its kind glyph. Segments keep their M1 textures, and each sign sits as a 13 px glyph at mid-segment. An interchange is an open capsule across the parallel lines. B2, B3 and R9 have no stations, so they sit in a labeled box at the frame edge, listed in words.

**Miniature.** The loop's line at one shared scale, station names in Inter 13, with a Stone ghost of the lines that leave each interchange. Cards span one or two columns by the loop's reach, counted in areas crossed, so size on the page means reach across the system, a measure a reader can check. R8 is a short shuttle between Housing scarcity and Owner home equity. R10 is a rectangle inside C. R1 runs from A to B to E and back.

**R8 card.** The line map inside a train car. R8 at full size as a closed shuttle on White, station names in Inter Tight 700 at 22 px with their "Higher:" rules, L38 named on the upper track and L39 on the lower, each with its sign, grade word and ticks. A locator inset shows the poster with R8 lit. L39 and A5's station take Pillar Red for R-30.

**Whole-system map.** The poster. Prussian ground, Newsprint lines, White station names in Inter 15 semibold, district names in Inter Tight 800. A note in the frame reads: "This map draws the 20 numbered loops: 25 variables and 46 connections. The register lists all 51 variables and 118 connections." Print as a 24 by 36 inch PDF; on screen at 1440 px. The live atlas uses a Newsprint version with one line lit and the rest in Stone. The R-01 reform view sits on White, with L25, L08 and C2 in red.

**Catalog page.** A line guide. The head is the poster as a full-width Prussian band, 1440 by 560 px, with "Twenty loop lines" in White Inter Tight 800. The line cards follow in district order, so the page mirrors the map: A's lines first, then B, C and D, with lines that cross areas placed after the last area they reach. White cards on Newsprint. The sketched loops close the page.

**Color, scale and type.** Prussian for the poster with no red; Newsprint for the working map and the catalog; White for the card and the reform view.

**Keep from the approved vocabulary.** M1 textures, where a dashed segment reads like track still under construction, which suits a less certain grade; the hollow line; M2 words in the legend; M3 chips as line bullets; M4 on the cards. The authority diamond stays off the network, because no authority connection closes a loop. Harry Beck drew the Underground from circuit diagrams (research 2.1), so this direction grows out of the Circuit Dustin favored.

**Risks.** A transit map suggests travel time, so the poster states that spacing is even and carries no meaning. The 1972 Vignelli diagram was replaced for dropping familiar anchors (research 2.2), so every station keeps its everyday V7 name. No TfL or MTA styling appears anywhere.

### Which I expect to win

Direction C. It is one drawing at three scales: the miniature is the loop's line, the card is that line at full size with names, and the poster is every line together, so a reader who learns one view can read the others. Most adults already read transit maps, so the metaphor needs no key. Its variety comes from the data, since each loop's shape follows where its variables sit. It also gives the atlas a signature image Dustin can print. Direction A is the safe path if C stalls. Direction B's spiral and ring are likely to survive inside the winner as the loop-kind mark. If the poster cannot hold 20 closed lines at 1440 px without crossings that hide a route, A's Circuit becomes the whole-system map, and C's line extracts still serve the catalog.
