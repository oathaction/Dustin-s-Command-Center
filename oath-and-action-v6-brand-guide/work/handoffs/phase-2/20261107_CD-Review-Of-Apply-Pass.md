### The end mark at true size

I measured ink rows at eight times size in Chromium, on Rule 04.6's own paragraph and on a test copy with capitals on the same line. The capitals of 17 px Inter stand 12.4 px tall. B, from its 16 px master placed at 16 px and lowered 1 px, puts ink across 15.9 px: its top 2.6 px above the cap line, its tail 1 px below the baseline. The master's file draws the glyph from 0 to 16 in a box 14 wide and 16 tall, so the "14 px tall" in D2-333 and in the rule sentence is the box's width. At true size B reads as a bold glyph from a larger size. It towers over "minutes." and outweighs the whole last line.

I then set the same paragraph with B scaled to the cap height and to 14 px, at 1x and 2x, and compared all three. At cap height and 2x, B sits inside the line as a quiet closing mark, the mark Dustin approved. At 1x its edges soften, and it still reads as our ampersand.

End mark alone: Craft 3, oversized and heavy against the text. Clarity 4, it reads as an end mark. Brand fidelity 3, it misses the cap height DR-118 set. Usefulness 3, the rule says 14 px and the specification draws 16. Evidence honesty 4, the specification row gives the size the page draws. Accessibility 4, it is hidden from screen readers and the text stays whole.

My recommendation: set B at the text's cap height, scaled from the 16 px master with its baseline on the text's baseline, and add a line to Rule 03.4 that the end mark sits outside the 16 px minimum because it follows a word and names nothing alone. If the studio wants the floor kept, the floor goes to Dustin as a question with both versions at true size, since a routine call cannot hold a named approval.

### L6's four pillar dots (identity designer's question 1)

I agree with the identity designer: redraw L6 without the four dots and keep its lockmark at L6's own proportion. The dots are the fifth edition's four-dot pillar row, which DR-111 replaced with icons and words. They code the pillars with no words beside them, the job DR-110 took from the grid. Taking them out removes one element, redraws no letter and changes no proportion, and L3 already sets the lockmark at a lockup's own proportion, so L6 keeps a precedent. Retiring L6 would drop a fifth-edition lockup and the report's Prussian closing strip to fix one detail. L6 needs 884 px, so the rule should also say that a narrower page closes with the footer place.

### Rule 01.11's reason (the editor's question)

Approve a change. W13 asks a writer to combine the sentences in a run or recast one, and the editor chose to combine. I would recast the first and keep the second as Dustin approved it:

> A case from each party, told the same way, puts a mechanism that both parties use in front of the reader. Where the record shows one side doing more, the finding stands as the record shows it.

The second sentence carries DR-93, and it stays word for word. The two sentences run 20 and 16 words and open on different frames. The editor's single sentence runs 38 words, joins two different cases with "and", and its phrase "a mechanism both parties use in front of the reader" can be misread on a first pass. If Dustin prefers to choose only between the two versions on the board, take the editor's: chapter 01 should not break a rule chapter 02 teaches.
