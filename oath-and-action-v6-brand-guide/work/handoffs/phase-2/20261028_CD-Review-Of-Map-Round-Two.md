## 1. Verdict

Round two did what round one could not. Two of the three directions beat the current system, and one image now meets Dustin's bar.

**Direction C, Loop Lines.** The poster is the first image in two rounds that a renowned studio would hang. It has twenty closed lines on an octilinear grid with zero crossings and one corner radius. Parallel tracks run on concentric arcs, the E1 hub has eight spokes, and the ground is Prussian. It passes the wall test. The rest of the direction is behind the poster:
- The catalog lifts each line at one shared scale, so R8 becomes a 60 px shuttle in a 310 px tile, and most tiles read as empty White.
- Two catalog tiles print names on top of each other at 1x (R10 and R12).
- On the R10 card, "Least certain step" sits on L53's own dashed line.
- On a phone, the poster, working map and reform view scroll the whole page sideways to 1440 px.

As a ten-second read, the poster says "a network" before it says "loops". Its job is orientation.

**Direction A, Field Guide.** A is the most finished work in either round, and it is the closest to Dustin's literal ask: every catalog tile is the R8 card at a smaller size. The R8 card, the catalog head with its five callouts and the R-01 view read at once. Its label audit covers 2,084 labels, and I found one clipped mark (fix 9). Its whole-system circuit is a wiring diagram, dense between areas D and E, and fails the wall test. A beats the current system on every rubric line except beauty, where it ties.

**Direction B, Snowball and Thermostat.** B is honest and careful, and its catalog head ("18 feed themselves", "5 push back") is the strongest typographic opener of the three. The shape still needs its key. At catalog size the echo disc sits 5.5 px from the start disc, so a snowball reads first as a ring with a doubled disc at twelve o'clock. Eighteen of the 23 loops are snowballs, so the wall shows one shape repeated, with less variety than Dustin asked for. An outward turn on the six loops running down also asks the words to correct the eye. B does not clearly beat the current system, and it retires as a direction.

**The studio's pick** is a combination (section 3): C's poster for the whole system and A's catalog and cards for the loops. With the fixes in section 4 it meets the bar on the signature image. The catalog comes close as a calm, well-made guide.

### Reasons

**A**
- Craft 4. Wraps are measured with the real fonts, rows fill, and no label collides.
- Clarity 4. The names sit in the boxes, the return line is labeled, and the head teaches one card.
- Usefulness 5. Every page has a phone form, and the spec is exact.
- Evidence honesty 5. The verifier found 0 mismatches and caught 11 of 11 planted errors. Headlines are labeled as studio drafts, R-06's missing back-reference is printed on the R8 card, and undated routes read "no check date in V7".
- Beauty 3. The head and the R8 card would be shown. The plates are a fine field guide and read as no one's signature, and the circuit fails the wall.

**B**
- Clarity 3. The direction rests on the shape reading without a key, and at 150 px it does not yet.
- Evidence 4. The outward turn on loops running down is a standing risk. Its V7.1 finding (14 reforms list a loop that does not list them back) is a useful catch.
- Beauty 3. I agree with the maker's score.

**C**
- Craft 3. The poster is a 4. The catalog collisions in R10 and R12, the R10 card label, and the phone R10 card pull the direction down. On the phone R10 card, five route cells run past the White card at 401 px ("Octob", "Unche"), and the step list for L08 and L42 prints "Documented: on the public record. Documented on the public record." twice. C's check has no label-against-label test, so it missed all of these.
- Clarity 3, for four reasons:
  - With one ink, a reader cannot tell which line a bullet names through the hub.
  - On the screen poster the kind glyph is about 12 px, and the spiral reads as the numeral 6 beside the ID ("6 R5"). B measured the pair as alike at 16 px (0.694 against the 0.60 limit).
  - The working map's lit line has the same weight as the unlit ones. Charcoal against Stone measures 3.10:1, which does not separate them.
  - The frame labels ("5 to A", the vertical side text) need decoding.
- Accessibility 3. The poster has no phone form.
- Beauty 4. The poster carries the direction.

## 3. Recommendation: the studio's pick

**"Loop Lines" for the system, "Field Guide" for the loops.**
- **Whole-system map:** C's poster on Prussian as the atlas's signature image and the 24 by 36 inch print. C's Newsprint working map serves the live atlas, and C's reform view on White shows R-01 on the whole system.
- **Catalog, loop cards and reform view:** A's pages. A's tiles are the shrunken R8 card Dustin pointed to.
- **The bridge:** C's locator inset (the working map with the loop lit) goes on every A card and in each catalog area band. A reader then sees each loop on its card and finds it on the map.
- **Kind mark:** B's spiral and ring go to Dustin as the loop-kind mark at 24 px and up, B's own measured floor.
- **Retired:** A's circuit and the rest of Direction B. The round-one fabric stays as the register of all 118 connections.

**Why the combination.** My round-one brief predicted C would win as one drawing at three scales. The poster won and C's miniature lost. At one shared scale the small loops shrink to stubs, and Dustin asked for a page of small R8 cards. A's catalog is that page. A's circuit cannot carry the atlas on a wall, and C's poster can.

**The cost.** A reader meets two drawings: boxes on the cards and lines on the map. Shared names (V7's chain labels), shared M1 textures and the locator keep them one system. The sign mark should also be one mark everywhere (decision 3).

## 4. Fixes before Dustin sees it

Ranked. Each is one pass for a maker. Fixes 1 to 6 are the poster's; 7 to 11 finish the pick.

1. **Tie every line bullet to its line.** Set each bullet on a segment that only its loop rides. Where none exists, use a 1 px leader to its own track. No bullet floats in open field. Clear the R4a bullet off the plus tag on the C1 to D7 pair, where it touches at 2x.
2. **Take the kind glyph out of the screen poster's bullets.** At about 12 px it reads as a 6. Each ID's R or B carries the kind, so add one legend line: "R: feeds itself. B: pushes back." Keep the glyph where it reaches 24 px: the print PDF (about 29 px), the cards and the catalog chips.
3. **Make the lit line read on the working map and the reform view.** Draw the lit loop at 6 px Charcoal, with Charcoal-filled stations and Newsprint IDs. Draw every other line at 2 px Stone. Check the result in grayscale.
4. **Write the frame in plain words.** Replace the ticks, "5 to A" and the vertical side text with one horizontal line per band, for example "Outside drivers, 5 variables. 12 connections reach the loops: 5 into area A, 4 into area B, 3 elsewhere."
5. **Make the areas read as territories.** The field tint #2C3B64 measures 1.21:1 against Prussian, and at poster distance the fields read as unrelated panels. Proof the current tint and one stronger step in grayscale at 25 percent size, and keep the step where the five areas read. Label the B1 and E2 patches with their letters.
6. **Give the poster, working map and reform view a 390 px form.** Fit the drawing to the screen width in a focusable region with a link to full size, then "The lines in words". Today each page scrolls sideways to 1440 px.
7. **Add C's locator inset to A's pages.** It goes in the rail of the R8, R10 and R-01 views (with fix 3 applied), and as a small locator in each catalog area band with that plate's loops lit.
8. **Replace A's circuit with C's views.** "R-01 on the whole system" becomes C's reform view, and the circuit page retires from the board.
9. **Fix A's phone R10 card and lead tiles.** The L42 sign pill on the return line is cut in half by the card's left edge. R15's lead tile holds about 120 px of blank White between its caption and its drawing; center each lead drawing in its tile.
10. **Port A's label audit into C's check.** Copy A's label-against-label and label-against-mark test into `maps_r2_c_check.js` and rerun it on the poster and working map.
11. **Show the sign-mark choice on the board.** Set A's R8 card twice, once with the circled sign and once with C's pointing tag, at true size, for decision 3.

If C's catalog and cards stay in the appendix, fix them in the same pass or leave them out:
- the R10 and R12 tile collisions;
- the "Least certain step" label on L53's line;
- the clipped route column on the phone R10 card;
- the repeated "Documented on the public record".

## 5. Decisions for Dustin

1. **Direction.** The studio's pick (A's loops with C's poster); C throughout; A throughout with its circuit; or the current system (G1-11). Recommended: the pick.
2. **The poster as a product.** Approve a paper proof at 24 by 36 inches before any release. The PDF embeds its fonts as Type 3, and no one has printed it.
3. **One sign mark everywhere.** Either the circled sign from Concept 08, which A uses with arrowheads, or C's pointing tag, which carries both the sign and the direction of the step. Recommended: the pointing tag, a new mark beside M1 to M4.
4. **New marks.** B's spiral and ring as the loop-kind mark at 24 px and up, and the M3 bullet form for line bullets. Recommended: adopt both, with the size floor.
5. **The field tint token** (#2C3B64, or the stronger step from fix 5). Recommended: register whichever step passes the proof.
6. **The octagon.** R4b and R1b ride the long way around the C and D cluster to keep zero crossings. Recommended: keep it. The legend already says length carries no meaning.
7. **The reader check.** Five readers new to the brand, ten seconds each on the R8 card, the R10 card and the poster, answering the four questions in my round-one brief. Recommended: run it before Gate 2.
8. **The R8 headline.** The three makers wrote three. C reused Concept 08's "How housing scarcity sustains itself", the line Dustin already saw on his reference card. Recommended: the editor works from that one.
9. **V7.1.** Add B's finding to the issue list: 14 reforms list a loop that does not list them back, R-06 on R8 among them.

## Risks for Dustin to weigh

- **The poster draws 20 loops, 25 variables and 46 connections.** The note in its frame says so and points to the register. It stays honest only if that note never leaves the print.
- **Two drawing styles.** The locator and the shared names hold them together. Readers may still expect the card to look like the map. The reader check will show it.
- **The catalog's beauty sits at 3.** A further pass on the catalog alone could follow Dustin's choice of direction.
- **Upkeep.** The station table and the field table need review whenever V7.1 changes a loop. The verifier fails on any mismatch, so the risk is time.
