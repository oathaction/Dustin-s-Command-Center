## Rounds 1 and 2's calls, now rules for every round 3 chart

Every maker applies these from the first draft, so the review does not find them again. Round 2 brief's ten calls hold; round 2's review added these.

1. **Rule 05.1 holds (D3-890, RF-1).** Pillar Red stays within 5 percent of the drawing at every width; the kit fails past it. An area (a map region, a node, a flow band, a matrix cell) that the title names takes the subject ink, never red, since its red share follows the data (D3-916). A mark keeps the red point only while it stays within 5 percent at every width (D3-917).
2. **The party test is Rule 14.6's list** (the data lead's round 2 rulings, "How the test runs"): a state, a Congress or chamber whose majority readers know, an election result, money spent to elect a candidate, and a policy readers link to one party. A mark on the list takes the subject ink through `labels.named`, never the red point. Party data takes the neutral party ink with each party named on its mark, solid and outline in the same ink where sides must be told apart at a glance (D2-269, Rule 01.2); no red and blue, no Prussian for a party (DR-48, DR-58, DR-64).
3. **Bands and areas that carry meaning meet 3 to 1** against what lies under them, by fill or by a 1 px context-ink edge (RF-2, D3-903, D3-918). Map regions on a sequential scale whose lightest step sits under 3 to 1 against the ground take the edge.
4. **A total or national figure is set apart one way** (ruling (b), D3-677): a line across a plot dashed in the text's ink with its own label; a point as an open ring; a row in a listing form first and set apart; on a map, beside the map with its name, never as a region.
5. **Published margins first (RF-11a).** Print the publisher's margin for the figure drawn; compute one only where the publisher prints none, and say so in the sidecar. Classes on a map sit wide enough that places differ in class only past their margins, with a note (DR-64, Rule 14.9).
6. **Words clear of lines.** The kit clears a word a quiet line crosses, and fails a data line, a stroke drawn after a word, or a dot half under a word (D3-946 to D3-948). Draw words after links and borders, through `K.parts.clear_label` where they must sit on them.
7. **Row names and values** in listing forms through `K.parts.row_name` and `K.parts.row_value` (D3-901).
8. **One ink word in reader notes (D3-914, D3-1042):** a named mark "in the text’s ink", other marks "gray", bands "shaded"; "subject ink" stays a builders' word in "For builders" lines.
9. **The width sweep (D3-904, D3-941, N1).** `build` and `check` draw every whole width from 256 px (224 for a rule plate) to the widest the guide gives the chart (720, or 869 for a wide chart). Run it before you report; a fault at any width is yours.
10. **Atlas text stays as stored (D3-349, D3-893).** A spec's `typography` key curls quotes, holds dates and spans with no-break spaces (`nbsp`) and applies listed replacements in printed words only. Anything that would change how the atlas names or qualifies something goes to the data lead, and any change to the atlas joins the V7.1 list for board 2.
11. **H09.** No chart, note or sentence splits a period at an inauguration date or gives counts on both sides of one (D3-619).
12. **Every figure in a title or note is checked by script against the data**, with the check named in `notes_internal`. Read every title from the left and the right.
13. **Read the whole output (D3-618).** A kit build's last lines can show passes while an earlier spec failed. Count the check files.
