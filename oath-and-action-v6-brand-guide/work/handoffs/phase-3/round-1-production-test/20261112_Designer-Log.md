# Production test 1, round 1: a designer makes a new chart from chapter 14 and the kit

Simulated panel, untested with real people (DR-202). One simulated designer, run by the UX and accessibility lead on November 12, 2026, for `work/handoffs/phase-3/20261112_Phase-3-Round-1-Review-Brief.md`. The designer knows layout and type, has never used the chart kit, and works only from chapter 14 in the strict build, the entry files it prints, the kit folder and the data folder. Every number below was checked by script against `work/build/data/treasury-mts-outlays-by-function.csv`.

The task: pick the form for "How did the federal government's spending by function change between fiscal 2016 and 2026?" from the rules and entries, then build it from `treasury-mts-outlays-by-function` with a correct title, source line and table, and test it with the kit's checks.

## Result

The designer chose the right form in two reads and had a chart passing every kit check after five spec versions (V1 to V5). The chart that passed still carries two faults the checks did not see: its source line names fiscal 2026 alone, and in 12 of its 19 rows the two dots sit on each other. The true source line (V6) fails the check until the research lead adds a variant. Files: `20261112_P01-Spec-V1.json` to `-V6.json`, `20261112_P01-Check-V5.json`, `20261112_P01-Dumbbell-Newsprint-1440.svg`, `20261112_P01-Dumbbell.csv`, `20261112_P01-Capture-V5-Newsprint-1440.png` and `-390.png`.

## Steps, where the designer looked, and each guess

1. **Choosing the form.** Read the chapter opener: the question "How has it moved?" and "What moved, and which way?" both seemed to fit. Read Rule 14.1, then the entries in the comparison and shift groups. Rule 14.20 (slope chart, "many items at two points in time") fit first; its "Use instead" sent them on: "More than about ten lines would cross into a tangle, so each item needs a row of its own: Rule 14.19". The data has 19 functions besides the Total line, so Rule 14.19, the dumbbell ("two values in each row and the gap between"). Rule 14.26 (change lollipop) was weighed and set aside, since the question asks about spending, so each function's size matters as much as its change. Time: two reads. This part works.
2. **Guess 1: nominal or real dollars.** Rules 14.1 to 14.14 and the entries say nothing about adjusting dollars for inflation over ten years. The designer found "not adjusted for inflation" in S33's and S27's measure lines and copied the pattern into the measure line. A rule or an entry line would have settled it.
3. **Starting the spec.** The entry's builders line, "copy specs/s04.json and point it at a new dataset", gave the starter. The kit README's table of starter specs lists only the 16 round 0 forms and says "19 forms in 17 files" and "specs/ The specs for S01 to S16"; the 26 round 1 forms are missing from it. The entry's line was enough here.
4. **V1** (`P01-Spec-V1.json`): copied S04, set the dataset, `where` with `{"function": {"not": ["Total"]}}` (the README's `not` example worked first time), `encode`, `labels.from` and `to`, the `billions-value` format, a pivot table and a title read from the data. The check: 15 faults. Long Treasury names ran off the phone artboard ("Education, Training, Employment, and Social Services", 365 px in a 358 px drawing); tick labels overlapped at 558 px; "1,667.1" ran past the right edge at 558 and 256 px; "−155.0" past the left edge at 256 px.
5. **V2**: shortened eight names in the drawing with `labels.categories` (the table keeps Treasury's names) and set `labels.domain` to `[-200, 1800]` to make room. **Guess 2**: the domain is in the printed unit. It is not: the check returned values placed at x = 21,779,159,435. The form's header says only "domain ([lo, hi])".
6. **V3**: domain in dollars, `[-2e11, 1.8e12]`. 12 faults, all at phone widths: the dumbbell's phone layout keeps no room for a value printed at either end of its scale.
7. **V4 and V5**: widened the domain by guesses until the end values fit: `[-5e11, 2.5e12]` left two faults at 296 and 256 px, `[-7.5e11, 2.5e12]` passed. The scale now runs 37 percent past the data, and a quarter of the plot at 1440 px stands empty.
8. **V5 builds**: `PASS P01 dumbbell: 38 rows, 4 drawings, 0 faults`, exports and page written.
9. **Looking at it** (`P01-Capture-V5-Newsprint-1440.png`): in 12 of 19 rows, the open 2016 dot sits under or against the filled 2026 dot (Transportation 92.9 to 142.9, Justice 57.1 to 109.9, Agriculture 20.2 to 52.6 and nine more), because one dollar scale holds Social Security at $1,667.1 billion and Energy at $21.0 billion. The values print, so the table and labels carry the change, but the drawing shows no change for most rows. No check caught it. Rule 14.40 covers this for lines (index when sizes are too far apart); no entry says what to do for two points in time.
10. **The source line.** V5 left `source_line` out, as the README says, and printed the sidecar's own line: "outlays by function, fiscal 2026". The chart shows 2016 and 2026, so the line names the wrong period (Rule 13.6, H04), and the check passed it. The sidecar's one other variant says "fiscal 2015 to 2026", also wrong. **V6** wrote the true line, "fiscal 2016 and 2026", and the check failed it: "the source line differs from treasury-mts-outlays-by-function.csv's printed_source_line and its variants ... ask the research lead to add a variant". The honest path is blocked until another person edits the sidecar; the misleading path passes.
11. **The title.** V1's title said "the largest rise of any budget function". By script, net interest rose most in dollars (+$811.0 billion) and energy most in percent (+459 percent). V6 says "the largest rise in dollars".
12. **The table.** The pivot printed 19 rows, one per function, with Treasury's full names and both years: equal to the data.

## What would have saved the designer time

- A source line variant for any span of the file's years, or a check that reads the years the line names against the years the spec draws (engineer, research lead).
- The dumbbell keeping room for its end values on phones, and its header saying `domain` takes the data's own unit (engineer).
- A check for two marks of one row drawn on each other, and an entry line under Rule 14.19 for items of very different sizes (engineer; maker A for the words).
- The README's starter table brought up to the 42 specs (engineer).
- One sentence in chapter 14 on dollars across years: say "not adjusted for inflation" in the measure line, or adjust and name the index (editor).
