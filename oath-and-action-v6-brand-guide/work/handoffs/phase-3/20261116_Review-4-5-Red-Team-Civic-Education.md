## 2. Must fix

**CE-M1. Rule 14.143's walk tells a newcomer the reverse of the loop.** Where: `s-14-143-correct` (status line and still list), words from `phase3_ch14_complex_build.py`. The status line opens "Start in Loop B4 at the quality of public services, and suppose it falls.", and the first step then reads "The quality of public services lowers the risk of losing office." Read in order, a newcomer hears that the fall lowered the risk; the atlas says poorer service raises it (Connection L23, "Poor service raises incumbents' risk of losing"). Loop R10 does the same: "suppose they rise", then "How often the primary decides the winner lowers the risk of losing office" and "The risk of losing office can lower officeholders' control over election rules", where under the scenario the risk falls and control rises. Neither loop ever says where the change ends up, so "balancing" and "reinforcing" stay labels: the walk ends "with one minus sign on its steps it is balancing" and never shows the push back. Rule 15.6's own spec asks for "One sentence that carries more or less around the loop and back to the start", and Rule 15.6's plate prints one ("More seats with little electoral competition, the primary decides the winner more often, less risk of losing office..."). Fix: keep each connection's sentence, and add the scenario's direction after it at every step, as Rule 15.6's plate does: "Services fall, so the risk of losing office rises." End each loop on its result: "Services rise again, against the first fall: the loop pushes back, so with one minus sign it is balancing." and "Safe seats rise again, on top of the first rise: the loop feeds itself, so with two minus signs it is reinforcing." Or drop "suppose it falls" and "suppose they rise" from the words. Owner: the complex examples maker, words by the editor. Rules 15.19 and 11.16 walk Loop R1, whose three steps all raise, so "suppose it rises" reads true there.

**CE-M2. Rule 15.23 puts the Senate's cloture vote only under step 4, against Rule 15.22.** Where: `s-15-23-correct`, Route FS block, from `phase3_ch15_build.py`; the manifest note says "step 4 holds stages 2 to 5; step 5 holds stages 6 and 7". The plate lists "Stage 3: Suspension of the rules", "Stage 4: Cloture" and "Stage 5: Reconciliation" under "4 First chamber" and only stages 6 and 7 under "5 Second chamber". Rule 15.22 says each of those paths serves either chamber: "Then stage 6, or stage 7 if the House votes second." and "With 60, on to the Senate's vote, stage 2 or stage 6." A bill that starts in the House meets cloture at step 5, the most common path to the Senate, and this table, whose one job is to say which stages sit behind each step, hides it there. Fix: list stages 3 to 5 under step 5 as well, or add one row under step 5, "Stages 3 to 5, by the same paths as the first chamber". Owner: the systems designer.

**CE-M3. Rule 15.23's correct plate calls the law's stages "steps".** Where: `s-15-23-correct`, the records request and agency rule blocks. The subheads read "A federal request's steps in the law, behind the tracker's six steps" and "The law's steps for a federal rule, behind the tracker's six steps", while each block's foot reads "Stages from 5 U.S.C. ...". Rule 15.23 exists to teach "Steps and stages do not line up by number", and the brief asks whether a reader can tell a tracker's step from a route's stage. On two of five blocks the plate uses one word for both. Fix: "A federal request's stages in the law, behind the tracker's six steps" and "The law's stages for a federal rule, behind the tracker's six steps". Owner: the systems designer, words by the editor.

**CE-M4. Rule 15.22 puts "Signed" on the line every enacted bill passes, and its own paths go around it.** Where: `s-15-22-correct`, stage 9 (class `st`, a filled square on the dark line). The key says the dark line holds "A stage every enacted bill passes, in order", and Rule 15.22's text says "Set the stages every change on the route passes through on one dark line in filled squares". Stage 10 then ends "With two-thirds in each, then stage 13." and stage 11 "Then stage 13.", so a law passed over a veto or without a signature never passes stage 9. A newcomer reads the key and learns that every law is signed, a common mistake the plate then contradicts four lines later. The systems designer's notes list "signed" among "the stages every enacted bill passes". Fix: draw stage 9 as the first of the three outcomes at the President, beside 10 and 11, each going on to stage 13, so the dark line runs from stage 8 to stage 13; or, if the spine stays, change the key to "A stage most enacted bills pass" and say under stage 9 "or stage 10 or 11". The first keeps the rule true. Owner: the systems designer.

## 3. Should fix

**On Rule 15.22, the federal statute (owner: the systems designer, words by the editor, facts by the research lead)**

**CE-S1. Stage 2 states a majority as a fixed count.** "A simple majority: 218 of 435 in the House, or 51 of 100 in the Senate." A bill passes with a majority of the members voting, a quorum present, and a tied Senate vote can pass with the Vice President's vote, so a newcomer who learns "218" cannot read a real 215 to 210 tally. Stage 3 on the same plate counts "two-thirds of the members voting", so the plate counts two ways. Fix: "A majority of the members voting: 218 when all 435 House members vote, or 51 when all 100 senators vote, and the Vice President can break a tie in the Senate."

**CE-S2. Stage 7 sends every bill to a conference committee.** "One text for both chambers: A conference committee settles the differences, and both chambers approve the same text." The stage sits on the line "every enacted bill passes", and most bills reach one text with no conference, by one chamber passing the other's text or by amending it back and forth. Rule 11.1 already treats a conference as one way among others. Fix: "Where the chambers passed different texts, they settle the differences by amending each other's text or in a conference committee, and both pass the same text."

**CE-S3. Stage 8's name is a clerk's word that chapter 11 fails.** "Enrolled and presented". Rule 11.1's failure caption says "A newcomer cannot say what Engrossed or Enrolled means", and Rule 15.23's plate prints "Stage 8: Enrolled and presented" beside the tracker's step 6, "President". Fix: name the stage "Sent to the President", and keep the clerk's word inside the line if wanted: "The final text is printed, which the clerks call enrolling, and sent to the President, who has 10 days, Sundays excepted, to act." Rule 15.23 reads the name from the stage row, so it follows.

**CE-S4. Reconciliation is named and never explained, with two more terms in its line.** "In the Senate, for a budget reconciliation bill ... A part that does not change spending or revenue is struck when a senator raises a point of order and the chair agrees." A newcomer learns how reconciliation works and never what it is, and "point of order" and "the chair" are floor terms. Fix: "A reconciliation bill carries out the spending and tax targets Congress sets in its budget resolution. Debate is held to 20 hours ... A senator can object to a part that does not change spending or revenue, and the presiding officer strikes it." Stage 14's "rescission bill" takes a short gloss in the same pass: "a bill that cancels the money".

**CE-S5. Stage 19's example is wider than the rule that chose it.** "The latest such ruling we found came on June 30, 2026, when the Supreme Court held ..." The sentence before it speaks of any court ("A court can rule that a law ... violates the Constitution"), and lower courts hold federal provisions unconstitutional often, while the rule that chose the case (D3-1887, D3-2101) is the Supreme Court's most recent such decision in the opinion lists read to November 9, 2026. Read from either side, the plate should state that rule so a reader can check it. Fix: "The Supreme Court's latest such ruling we found, in its opinions to November 9, 2026, came on June 30, 2026, when it held ..." This also answers named place 10.

**CE-S6. Stage 10's stop line breaks into three columns.** In the 1440 px capture, "Short of two-thirds in either chamber, the bill dies." sets as "Short of", "two-thirds" and "in either chamber, the bill dies." side by side, with extra space around "two-thirds" in the next line too. The line is a flex container (`p.sq`) and the `span.nw2` around "two-thirds" becomes its own flex item. It is the one stage where a newcomer learns that a veto can kill a bill. Fix: wrap the line's words in one span inside `p.sq`, or keep `nw2` out of flex lines. Seen at 1440 px only, since the other widths were not yet captured when I read them.

**On Rule 15.23 (owner: the systems designer)**

**CE-S8. Four rows say "The agency decides".** Under steps 2 to 5 of the records request: "The agency decides, within 20 working days that start here", "The agency decides, inside the same 20 working days" twice, and "The agency decides within 20 working days, or 10 more in unusual circumstances". A newcomer cannot tell when the agency decides, and the run is the parallel shape W13 removes. **CE-S9. "Guidance without comment" stands where Rule 11.9 puts a rule issued without comment.** Rule 11.9: "A rule the agency issued without asking for comment first keeps steps 2 to 4 under slashes ... An interim final rule is one such rule." Rule 15.23's row "In place of steps 2 to 4" names only guidance, which is a different thing and is never defined. Fix: "A rule issued without advance comment, such as an interim final rule, or guidance, which explains a rule and takes no comment".

**CE-S10. A filled square means two things across the two chapters.** In chapter 11 a filled square is "Stopped". On Rules 15.22 and 15.23 a filled square is a stage every enacted bill passes, and a small square is a path that stops; Rule 15.23 sets both beside the tracker's step names. The drift reader rules on marks; from this lens, a newcomer who learned chapter 11 first reads stage 1's filled square beside "1 Filed" as stopped. Fix, if the drift reader agrees: say it once in Rule 15.23's lead line, "Squares here are Rule 15.22's stages; a tracker's steps keep their numbers in words."

**Terms (owners as named)**

**CE-S11. Court terms used and never explained.** "injunction" in Rules 11.11 and 11.12 and on Rule 15.23's plate; "discovery" and "motions" on Rule 15.23's plate and "Discovery, settlement or trial" on Rule 15.24's; "doctrine" in "A later ruling that replaces earlier doctrine". Fix: gloss once each where a reader first meets it: "an injunction, a court order to do or stop doing something"; "discovery, when each side gathers evidence from the other"; "doctrine, the rules courts build from their rulings". Owners: the tracker designer for chapter 11, the systems designer for chapter 15, words by the editor.

**CE-S12. Rule 15.21 counts four effect codes, and the atlas holds seven.** "the three delay classes and the four ordinal effect codes". The atlas's glossary lists seven codes ("+L large positive, +M moderate positive, +S small positive, 0 no expected effect, -S small negative, -M moderate negative, ? direction uncertain"), and Rule 14.144's own key prints all seven. Four is the count of outcome families, which Rule 15.20 states correctly ("the atlas's four ordinal codes", one a family). "Ordinal" is not glossed anywhere in chapter 15; chapter 13 glosses it ("a rank of small, moderate or large with no measured amount"). Fix: "the seven effect codes, ranks of direction and rough size with no measured amount". Owner: the systems designer, words by the editor.

**CE-S13. Rule 14.142 never says what "90 percent" means.** Rule 14.142: "give its size, its confidence, such as the Bureau's 90 percent"; S96's note and source line: "the Bureau of Labor Statistics' 90 percent margin of error". Chapter 13 defines a margin of error ("the range around the figure that sampling allows"), and nothing in the guide that I found explains the 90 percent, which many adults read as "90 percent accurate". The rule's "the Bureau" is also never named in the rule's own text. Fix: in Rule 14.142, "the Bureau of Labor Statistics' 90 percent margin, a range that would hold the true change in nine samples out of ten", and in S96's first note, "A column that ends inside the band is a change the survey cannot tell apart from no change, nine times in ten." Owner: the complex examples maker, words by the editor, wording checked by the statistics reader.

**CE-S14. Rule 14.144 prints the band as a bare word.** Each card reads "Band, the kind of change" then "Rules". Rule 15.11's spec asks for "The atlas's word and its definition, quoted", and Rule 15.20's panel prints it: "Rules: 'Changes incentives, penalties and constraints: who may run, how votes count, what firms may do (level 5).'" A newcomer reading "Rules" in a guide made of rules cannot tell it is a kind of change. Fix: print the atlas's definition after the word, as Rule 15.20 does. Owner: the complex examples maker.

**On the trackers' words (owners: the tracker designer and the editor; for board 3)**

**CE-S15. "Certify" names three acts in one tracker, and the step table uses a fourth verb.** Rule 11.10's step table says at step 2 "The attorney general clears the measure", while the end word for step 2 is "Not certified by the attorney general". Step 5 says "The state's election officer certifies the measure for the ballot", and step 6 "the state certifies the result", which the rule's title calls "the certified count". A newcomer meets "Not certified" and "the certified count" in one list and cannot tell them apart. Fix: say at step 2 "The attorney general certifies that the measure is in proper form and on an allowed subject, and writes the title and summary"; gloss "certifies" once in Rule 11.10's text ("certifies, makes it official in writing"); keep the law's end word.

**CE-S16. Rule 11.10 never names the kinds of measure its marks tell apart.** Its text describes "where the Legislature votes on the measure first" and "a measure the Legislature sends to the voters itself", and only the specs and the why name them: "where the initiative is indirect", "a direct initiative". The term a reader will see on a ballot, a legislative referral, appears on Rules 15.23 and 15.24 and never in Rule 11.10. The rule also opens "A ballot measure here is a citizen initiative" and then covers referrals. Fix: "A ballot measure here is a citizen initiative, which voters propose by petition, or a legislative referral, which the Legislature sends to the voters itself. An initiative is indirect where the Legislature votes on it first and direct where it goes straight to the ballot."

**CE-S17. "Passed by the Legislature" may read as law where a governor still acts.** Rule 11.10 ends an indirect initiative the Legislature passes with slashes on steps 5 and 6 and "Passed by the Legislature on May 2." Rule 11.1's why teaches the opposite habit for bills: "Naming step 6 for the person who signs keeps a bill that waits for a signature from reading as law." The round 5 editor's notes say "Massachusetts's path after enactment was not read". Fix: the research lead reads whether the governor signs an initiative the Legislature passes in Massachusetts and Ohio, and where one does, the words say so: "Passed by the Legislature on May 2. It goes to the governor." Board 3 shows the end word with that answer.

**CE-S18. "And sent back" replaces the court's own word, against the rule's own claim.** Rule 11.11 says a higher court's ruling "reads as the court enters it", and its builders line says "print that verb", yet the court enters "remanded" and the tracker prints "and sent back". Rule 15.24's court doctrine card prints "Decision, rehearing or remand" with no gloss, so a reader meets both words and no link between them. Fix for board 3: keep "and sent back" on the card and gloss "remanded" once in Rule 11.11's text ("and sent back, which the court enters as remanded"), and on Rule 15.24's card write "or a remand, which sends the case back".

**CE-S19. "Affirmed" on a card says nothing plain.** The rule glosses it once in its text, "which leaves the ruling below in place", but a reader of a real card sees only "Affirmed by the appeals court on May 6." The tie already carries "The lower court's ruling stands.", and the denied petition "The appeals court's ruling stands." Fix: add the same sentence after every "Affirmed" end word, so each ending that leaves a ruling in place says so in the same words. For board 3.

**CE-S20. An unappealed ending takes two marks in one group.** Rule 11.8 keeps an unappealed request's step 6 "under a slash once the time to appeal has passed"; Rule 11.11's spec ends an unappealed case "A square on step 2", under the key word "Stopped", for a case the plaintiff or defendant won. A reader who learned Rule 11.8 expects a slash, and "Stopped" reads as a case that failed. Rule 11.8 is the approved rule. Fix: a question for board 3 below.

**CE-S21. "Not approved by voters" and "Rejected by voters" sound alike.** Rule 11.10's spec ends a measure "Not approved by voters where most said yes but a second test failed, with a sentence that names it". A newcomer reads the two as one outcome. Fix for board 3: "Fell short of the vote the law requires", then the sentence that names the test.
