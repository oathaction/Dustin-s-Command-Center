# Production test 2: a reader new to the subject reads five round 3 items

Oath & Action Edition 06, Phase 3, round 3 review, November 13, 2026. **Simulated panel, untested with real people.** The UX and accessibility lead played an adult reader who follows the news but has never made a chart, reading in the built guide at 1440 px and on the entry files' words. For each item: what it taught, what confused the reader, and the word that stopped them.

## 1. Structure: Rule 14.96, the process diagram, with S66

- **Taught.** A rule moves through five numbered steps, and three outside hands can step in: White House review, Congress with the President, and a court. The numbered boxes and the table in the same order made the path easy to follow, read aloud or seen.
- **Confused.** The table's "Then" column mixes the next step with the branches ("Step 2; for a significant rule, White House review first; guidance can skip steps 2 and 3"), so the reader had to read the row twice to find the main path.
- **Stopped at:** "OMB", printed in the table as "Office of Information and Regulatory Affairs (OMB)" and never spelled out. Second: "notice and comment", used in note 2 and the table before the reader learns that steps 2 and 3 are the notice and the comment.

## 2. Flow: Rule 14.110, the Sankey, with S76

- **Taught.** Each band's width is its dollars; money in on the left, money out on the right; cash on hand at both ends makes the two sides balance; loans sit apart so a loan never reads as a gift.
- **Confused.** "The chart traces no dollar received to a dollar spent" made sense only after the reader saw that all bands pass through one box. The title's "$1,384.1 million" read as an odd way to write about $1.4 billion.
- **Stopped at:** "Sankey" (the entry's title uses the name and never says what it is) and "node" ("one node", "the node balances", "the node holds the committees"). The reader guessed "node" meant the box in the middle. Third: "net" and "nets transfers" in note 3.

## 3. Maps: Rule 14.120, the county choropleth, with S81

- **Taught.** A county map shows a broad pattern; small counties have wide margins, so the guide's own map refuses to rank any county and says how many could fall in the next class (1,707 of 3,143).
- **Confused.** The table's states run Alaska, Alabama, Arkansas, Arizona, then California, and "District of Columbia" before Delaware. The reader looked for Alabama first and thought it was missing. The note says "the download lists every county", and the page offers no download.
- **Stopped at:** "choropleth" (used in the title, explained nowhere in chapter 14) and "sequential steps" in the rule text.

## 4. The avoid entry 14.124, with its sketch

- **Taught.** Party colors take a side before anyone reads a number, so the guide shows who won by panels and the margin by shade.
- **Confused.**
  - The drawing on the "do not publish" side is calm and gray, twelve equal squares in two shades. Without the bar, the reader would have taken it for a clean way to show winners. They looked for red and blue and found them only in the key's words ("Party A, shown red").
  - The entry's second reason ("a party that wins large, thinly settled places looks bigger than its count") is not in the drawing: every square is the same size.
  - "Use it when: Never: ... gray panels by party", while the "Use instead" half is drawn in green steps, not gray.
- **Stopped at:** "Use it when: Never", which reads as a contradiction until the second clause.

## 5. Rule 14.9's class map paragraph

- **Taught.** A darker class reads as higher, so a map ranks, and a survey map has to keep its classes wide enough that a place's margin cannot move it two classes.
- **Confused.** "Make each class at least as wide as the widest margin of error drawn". The reader asked whether that means the margin (the "plus or minus" figure) or the whole range from low to high. The chapter's own specimen S78 says "wider than any state's 90 percent margin of error" and gives Wyoming's ±1.9 against 4-point classes, which fits either reading.
- **Stopped at:** "their gap's margin" ("differ by more than their gap's margin"). The first paragraph names "the gap's own margin of error", but how to work it out is in the kit's README, not the rule.

## Words that stopped the reader, in one list

OMB; notice and comment; Sankey; node; nets; choropleth; sequential steps; Use it when: Never; gap's margin.
