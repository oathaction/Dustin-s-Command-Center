One skeleton per pillar, drawn two ways

This board proposes new pillar marks for Explain, Track, Connect and Act. Each pillar starts as one skeleton on a shared 24-unit grid. Two directions grow from the same four skeletons: A, a geometric solid, and B, a line with one accent. Every mark carries one node, the point where its two parts meet. The studio recommends Direction A.

Everything here is a sketch for a decision at Gate 1. No master changes. Decision DC-02 allows the redraw; the drawings live in work/boards/phase-1/symbols/. Each proposal sits beside the V5 original it would replace. Defect fix marks a change that repairs a measured problem. Option marks a choice for you.

1. Four skeletons on one grid

Each skeleton is the drawing both directions share: the parts, their proportions and where the node sits. The grid is 24 units square, one unit to a pixel at 24 px, with a 2-unit margin. 48 and 96 px scale the 24 px master by 2 and 4. The 16 px masters are separate drawings of the same skeletons, fitted to whole pixels.

Explain

A square, broken open. Its top-right quarter lifts out along the diagonal; the node sits where it came from.

No letter C, no copyright sign: square primitives only, and the node sits between two pieces.

Track

Three stages on a rising line. The node stands on the stage a decision has reached.

No status light: one node, no row of colored circles. No tree, so it stays apart from the Signal Grid.

Connect

One circle in two equal halves, held apart. The node sits in the space between them: the common ground.

No chain link: the halves never interlock. Same size and color on both sides, so neither side leads.

Act

A door swung open toward the reader. The node is the handle, the place a reader takes hold.

No letter D, no play button, no outbound arrow: straight edges, a frame and a leaf, no arrowhead.

2. One node runs through all four

Dustin described the hinge as a promise, then acting, and as the point where two sides meet. Each pillar mark is drawn as two parts and the point that joins them. In Explain the node sits where a quarter of the square lifts out. In Track it stands on the stage a decision has reached. In Connect it holds the space between two equal halves. In Act it is the handle of the open door.

The node always sits on the horizontal center line. When the four marks run in order, one line can pass through every node, so the row reads as a single path from Explain to Act. That is the same grammar as the hinge diagram and the Signal Grid: a node and a line. A reader who learns one mark learns where to look in the other three.

Shared elements considered

The node adds no new shape, because every mark already needs one point of focus. It sits in the same place every time, and it is the only place a pillar mark may ever carry red. It also keeps the ampersand out of the pillar marks, so the wordmark keeps sole use of it.

A. The red handle, where the Act mark is the call to act

Under the red rule in section 12, the Act handle is red only when the Act mark is itself the call to act, as on a social tile, and the handle then spends the view's one working red. On Newsprint and White the handle sits inside a 1 px Charcoal ring, because red on Newsprint measures 2.96:1 and an informative mark needs 3:1. On Charcoal the seat is cut out and red meets Charcoal at 4.49:1. Red never sits on Prussian. At 16 px the handle is 2 px, too small to carry color, so Act stays one color there.

5. Track and Act no longer look alike at 16 px

Defect fix

In V5 both marks run from a node at the bottom left to an accent at the top right, and at 16 px they correlate at 0.81. The new Track is a stair, wide and low. The new Act is a door, tall and narrow. The measure is the one the Phase 0 audit used: overlap of the filled silhouettes, and correlation after a 3 by 3 blur, which stands in for a glance. A score is their mean.

6. Topic grammar proof in Direction A

Option

Four V5 topics redrawn with Direction A's rules: filled primitives, square corners and one node with a real job. The node is the filled oval on the ballot, the seal at the foot of the bill, the seal on the envelope and the face of the coin. In V5, Contact an official and Campaign money shared one silhouette at 16 px, a landscape box with a mark in the middle. Campaign money is now a stack of coins with one coin standing, so the two subjects no longer share an outline.

Defect fix applies to one change only: splitting Contact an official from Campaign money, which shared one silhouette in V5. The grammar itself, for all four topics and the sixteen still to draw, is an option for you.

How each check was run

  • Silhouette. At 16 px, at least 60 percent of inked pixels are solid (crisp share) and ink covers at least 25 percent of the field. V5's pillar masters score 0.30 to 0.57 crisp share.
  • Pixel fit. In the 16 and 24 px masters, every horizontal and vertical straight edge, and every horizontal or vertical tangent of the node, seats, discs and arcs, lies within 0.06 px of a whole pixel. Round caps, round joins and corner fillets are exempt.
  • Consistency. The node and its 1 px ring match the family specification at 16 and 24 px. A declared exception counts as a fail and is named in the cell.
  • Optical balance. At 96 px, ink sits within 20 percent of the median of its set, and the center of mass sits within 12 percent of the field center on both axes.
  • Similarity. Every score against another pillar in the same set, and against each Phase 0 watch item, is under 0.60. Tiles are compared inside the plate and inherit the watch-item results of the drawing they hold. Topic against pillar must be under 0.70, because the two never share a slot.
  • Monochrome. Rendered in Charcoal on Newsprint and in Newsprint on Charcoal, the node is its own connected shape at 16 and 24 px.
  • Red discipline. Red appears only on the Act handle, and only where the Act mark is the call to act, as the red rule in section 12 sets out. Checked in the drawings and in every composition on this board.
  • Contrast. Ink against every surface (WCAG ratio), and the ring around the red handle against the red, measured on rendered pixels.
  • Type. Applies to compositions. Results for the board's headings and the composition headlines are listed below.

  1. Symbol direction. Options: A, geometric solid; or B, line with one accent.Studio recommendation: A. It leads with the filled master, which passes the 16 px silhouette test for all four pillars. B leads with the line, which fails it for Track and Connect.
  2. The four skeletons. Options: Approve the exploded square, the stair, the split circle and the open door, or ask for changes to any one.Studio recommendation: Approve, with one Phase 2 change: raise Track's stair so its mass sits nearer the center.
  3. The shared element. Options: One node on the center line in every mark; the ampersand's crossing; a ring; a baseline bar.Studio recommendation: The node. It ties the marks to the hinge and the Signal Grid without adding a shape, and leaves the ampersand to the wordmark.
  4. Red in the pillar marks. Options: Adopt the red rule, which also settles whether the Act handle counts; or red in all four marks, as in study 06.Studio recommendation: Adopt the red rule. The wordmark's ampersand and the lockmark's lever are signature red and do not count. Each view (one screen, card, slide or printed page) then carries one working red, in this order of precedence: an error the reader must fix; the step a reader can take now; the one point the headline names, which in a systems figure is where a reform acts. A hinge headline's ampersand uses the view's working red. The hinge divider's ampersand is Charcoal. The Act handle is red only when the Act mark is itself the call to act, as on a social tile.
