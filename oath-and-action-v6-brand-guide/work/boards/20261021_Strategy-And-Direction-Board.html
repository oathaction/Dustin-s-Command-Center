1 · Positioning and the brand idea

The ampersand is the hinge, and the hinge turns in a loop

Recommendation. Make the hinge the organizing idea of the whole system Option. Your idea (DC-05) is a commitment, then action, and every locked line already has that shape.

Positioning

For people in every state who live under rules they rarely get to read, Oath & Action is a nonpartisan publication and set of tools that explains how a decision is made and gives a practical way to take part. Claims carry a source and an evidence grade.

What sets it apart: every piece turns at the hinge divider to "What you can do," and every position is labeled.

Brand narrative (232 words)

Every public rule starts with people who promised to serve: a council that adopts a budget, a legislature that passes a bill. Then the rule reaches a household as a rent renewal, a pay stub, a ballot or a power bill.

Oath & Action follows that path for readers in every state. We read the rule and show who holds the decision. The record stays within reach while the decision moves. When it is time to act, we point to the office that answers for it and the step a reader can take.

The ampersand in our name is the hinge between a promise and the action that keeps it. It joins a rule to its result and a problem to the fixes on the table. It also joins people: you and a neighbor who lives under the same rule and may vote another way. You both get the same record and the same way in.

The hinge turns in a loop. People who take part and see the result can hold the next promise to account, and the people who made it answer to them. Our tagline names that goal: "A country that answers to its people."

We are nonpartisan. When credible sources disagree, we show both readings and the evidence for each. When we take a position, we label it before the headline and summarize the strongest case against it.

Naming rules

  1. Names describe the product. Only the atlas is a question.
  2. No product name contains an ampersand. A partner's is written "and" (V5).
  3. Every piece carries a content label, such as Explainer, Tracker or Report.
  4. "Dispatch from the Front" becomes "Dispatch," as V5's kicker already says Option.
  5. Drop "scorecards." Grading officials is an advocacy format.

What keeps a position piece fair

  1. The label leads. On a shared card it holds the top-left corner.
  2. The case against is written as its supporters would put it, sourced, at least a third of the argument's length.
  3. Each claim keeps its evidence grade and a verb to match.
  4. Opponents are named by office or role, never as a party villain.
  5. Process facts match the neutral tracker word for word.

6 · Nonpartisan rule set

Twenty rules a producer can check

Recommendation. Adopt NP-01 to NP-20 Option. The NP prefix cannot collide with atlas IDs.

Color

  1. NP-01 Pillar Red and Prussian never share a figure (lockups: identity board).
  2. NP-02 Two sides never take red against blue or Verdigris. Use Charcoal and Stone.
  3. NP-03 Diverging scales center on Newsprint, avoid red and blue, and name both ends in words.
  4. NP-04 Red has one job per layout and never marks a party, side or person.
  5. NP-05 Maps never color places by party or result.

Imagery

  1. NP-06 Domes, columns and flags appear only in a captioned photograph of that place.
  2. NP-07 No party symbols, candidates or protest slogans, except as sourced evidence.
  3. NP-08 Photograph many kinds of people at public work, officials included.

Rotation

  1. NP-09 Per chapter, no V7 domain supplies over a quarter of examples, and no state over a fifth.
  2. NP-10 Rotate levels of government and regions.
  3. NP-11 When majorities of either party use a mechanism, show both, as in Loop R4a, "Majorities draw maps that help them keep control."
  4. NP-12 Log each example's domain, state and level, reviewed at each gate.

Language

  1. NP-13 Name the office. Name a party only when party is the mechanism, with a source.
  2. NP-14 No grievance framing: "the rule you are angry about," "rigged," "broken," "crisis."
  3. NP-15 No war or sports metaphors.
  4. NP-16 Contested claims show both readings at equal length.
  5. NP-17 A supporter of the current rule would accept its description.

Hinge pairs

  1. NP-18 Both halves share one color and weight. Only the ampersand is red.
  2. NP-19 People pairs share a rule, and their order rotates.
  3. NP-20 "Left," "right" or party names enter a hinge headline only to report a sourced agreement.

7 · Art direction

Take the heavy capitals and the serif ampersand, and leave the domes

What the slides share: Newsprint, condensed capitals, one red serif ampersand, the grid at bottom right, Prussian panels, scenes of people. p07 recurs five times.

Condensed heavy wordmark

p01, p07, p08, p13, p40, banner

Take heavy capitals against a calligraphic ampersand: two kinds of letter, joined. The square poster uses a wide sans.

Avoid condensed capitals in every headline (p17, p40).

Serif red ampersand

p07, p15, p41

Take circle and square containers in one size system.

Avoid Verdigris or Stone ampersands (p07) and seals with a ring of words or "EST. 2026" (p03, p07, p41). V5: "Seals imply an authority we do not claim."

Hinge circle

p04, p07, p12, p41, mockups

Take the circle and its two pin dots. It can become a wordless badge.

Avoid double arrowheads, dashed arcs, labels under 13 px.

Pillar badges

p01, p04, p08, p40, p45

Take four filled circles in pillar order as navigation.

Avoid the ampersand as a pillar icon, Prussian beside red, and the names "Expose" and "Strengthen" (p01, p03, p17).

Signal Grid as pattern

p15, square poster

Take the node field at low density on covers and merchandise Option.

Avoid lit nodes in a pattern, pattern behind text, a 5 by 5 lockmark.

Documentary photography

p01, p05, p15, p17, p40

Take people doing the work, in black and white.

Avoid using any as a photograph: all are generated. Also flags as props (p05) and buildings without people (p03).

Editorial social templates

p01, p17, p40, p41

Take a fixed corner grid and numbered carousels (p41).

Avoid unsourced numbers such as "12.4M" (p01, p04, p17, p41), slogan triplets (p17, p40), all-red tiles (p40, p41).

Data-led cards

p14 above all

Take a sentence title, the illustrative label inside each chart, red only on the point the title names.

Avoid icon-bullet percentages with a vague source (p17).

Clichés to avoid

The Capitol dome and columns (p03, p08, p24, p25, p41). Scales, stars, shields, the map silhouette and the handshake (p25). Megaphones (p15, p40). "Democracy is not a spectator sport" (p25). "Be the change" (p17).
