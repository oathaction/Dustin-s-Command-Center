I4 Option Approved at Gate 1; B as refined goes to Gate 2

Ampersand B, refined

Every lockup carries the ampersand, so B's refinement pass came first. Gate 1 set four tests for it: raise its stroke contrast from about 1.5 to 1 toward 1.7 to 1, set its crossing on the hinge diagram's center line, refine it at twice size, and check it in print and against other serif ampersands. If B failed any of them, option A would take its place.

Result: B passes all four, so option A stays the unused fallback. The drawing is redrawn by hand as clean cubic contours, 27 nodes against the first drawing's 41, on the same broad-nib axis.

What changed

Contrast. The bowl stem rises to 0.91 of the wordmark's stem and the hairlines fall to 88.5 to 91.6 font units. Thick over thin now measures 1.74 to 1 (the first drawing, by the same stations, 1.51). Measured as means of the thick and hairline stations, it is 1.66 to 1 (1.42 before).

Crossing. B's pen path crosses itself twice. The waist crossing, between the loop and the bowl, is its pivot, and it moves down 37.5 font units to the glyph's vertical ink center. In the hinge diagram, where the ampersand is centered on its ink, the crossing now sits 0.1 diagram units from the center line that joins the four nodes. The arm crossing stays low: lifting it to the center line would need a new skeleton.

Balance. The lower crossing makes the loop taller, so the loop is 13 units narrower than in the first drawing and the bowl reaches 8 units further left, which keeps the visual weight in the bowl. The serif drops 7 units and the beak gives up 4, so the beak keeps 54 units of clear space to the loop (the first drawing had 64).

Overshoot. The loop top and the bowl bottom overshoot by 9.4 font units, 1.34 wordmark units, the same as the wordmark's O. The first drawing overshot by 1.43 and 1.71.

Silhouette against every serif ampersand on this machine

I checked B against 33 faces: every style of the eight serif families installed here (Bitstream Charter, Caladea, Courier 10 Pitch, DejaVu Serif, FreeMono, FreeSerif, Liberation Mono, Liberation Serif) and the three Lora files from V5. Each glyph is scaled to the same ink height, centered on its ink box, rendered at 200 px, and compared by silhouette overlap (1.0 is identical). Two rules were set before the run. Recognition: B overlaps the V5 glyph, Lora Bold, at 0.70 or more, so a reader still knows it. Distinctness: B overlaps no other face as closely as one face's Regular overlaps its own Bold (the median of eight families), the point at which two ampersands count as one design.

Recognition passes at 0.734. Distinctness passes: the nearest face is Liberation Mono Bold at 0.686, under the 0.698 line. The margin is narrow, and the nearest faces are typewriter slabs, whose resemblance is mass more than form. The first drawing would have failed: it overlapped Courier 10 Pitch Bold at 0.713. At 16 px every ampersand looks alike, so the small-size scores in the table are for the record only. Commercial faces are not on this machine and were not checked.

Rule 05.3, word for word (decision D2-01)

The wordmark's ampersand and the lockmark's lever are signature red and do not count, and neither do the ampersands inside the hinge diagram and the hinge-circle badge, which are identity marks. The hinge diagram never shares a view with the wordmark and never stands in for the hinge divider. An icon's red accent counts as signature red when the icon stands alone, as an app tile, an avatar or a single feature icon. In a row or a menu, icons go one color and only the current one keeps its red. Each view (one screen, card, slide or printed page) then carries one working red, in this order of precedence: an error the reader must fix; the step a reader can take now; the one point the headline names, which in a systems figure is where a reform acts. One job may take several marks, such as each target of one reform. A hinge headline's ampersand uses the view's working red. The hinge divider's ampersand takes its surface's ink: Charcoal on Newsprint and White, Newsprint on Charcoal, White on Prussian. In the guide itself, specimens, labeled failure cases and palette swatches do not count. Red never sits on Prussian.
