COVERAGE PLAN

Every way Oath & Action shows data

The plan lists 122 forms in 16 families. Each one says what Oath & Action uses it for, names real data for its specimen and sets a tier. Chapter 14 is built from it at the start of Phase 3.

Core, 50

A finished specimen on real data, a generator in the kit, a “use when” and “avoid when” rule, phone and still-image versions

Extended, 43

A finished specimen on real or labeled illustrative data and a rule; the generator follows when a story needs it

Reference, 23

A small drawn sketch and a rule that says when the form is the right choice and what to use instead

Avoid, 6

Named with the reason and the form to use instead

Rules that hold across every form

  • Explainer’s anatomy on every chart: evidence label, sentence title with a verb matched to the grade, measure line, the figure, notes in the drawing, the source line in section 15 order, and a table or text equivalent.
  • Newsprint is the ground, with Deep Stone for small text and a 1 px Charcoal keyline on any red mark. Charcoal is the dark mode.
  • One working red per view (Rule 05.3). Red never encodes a series, a category or a scale.
  • Party marks take neutral inks with the party’s name printed, and arrows, position and words carry direction (DR-48, DR-58).
  • Diverging scales use pair B, Prussian through Newsprint to Verdigris, with a boxed arrow or a printed sign carrying direction (DR-54).
  • Still image first for Substack, email and social; interactive layers (filters, tabs, layer switchers, Chart and Table switches) on the atlas site and in reports (DR-59).
  • Data from Atlas V7 and primary public sources; FRED copies of BLS series are labeled as FRED copies (DR-60).

THE 16 FAMILIES

Each family opens with the question it answers

Rows already drawn in the Explainer system are shaded and underlined. “Rule only” forms are named with the reason and the form to use instead. “Sketch and rule” forms get a small drawing and a rule that says when they are the right choice.

Each round runs the red team (statistics, civic education, accessibility, nonpartisanship, craft and production), a reader panel, one fix pass and the creative director’s scores. A balance check runs every round (DR-55): across the set, readers on each side meet a finding they would rather skip, and every title is read from the left and the right before it ships.
