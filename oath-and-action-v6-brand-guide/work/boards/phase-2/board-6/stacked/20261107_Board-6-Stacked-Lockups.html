More stacked lockups

This board sets six ways to stack the name beside today’s L2. All six keep today’s wordmark width and ampersand B as refined. We set each word from the wordmark master’s own letters and spacing and placed B from its own master, redrawing no letter.

“I don’t know that I like that. Can you give me a few more variations of stacked?”

Dustin Cathcart, November 7, 2026, on L2: OATH over ACTION with B 1.28 times larger, set off at the right.

All six beside today’s L2

Every lockup here sets its capitals 33 px tall, so L2 stands 72 px tall. All seven come from the display masters, as L2 does, and the lockups in each row share one top line. Readers take in L2 as OATH, ACTION, then B, because B sits after both words. Each new stack puts B between the words, in the order people say the name.

Our recommendation

S1, ampersand ends line one

  • It is the wordmark split after B. OATH, B and the white between H and B are exactly as the wordmark master draws them, so a reader who knows the masthead knows this stack at once.
  • It reads in the name’s order and keeps B at the size of the capitals, as the wordmark does.
  • It packs the name into the smallest box of the six, 5.09x by 2.13x. In a 400 px round avatar its capitals reach 49 px, against 43 px for L2, and it holds 14 px capitals in tiles down to 115 px.
  • Its lines hold 0.203x of white, the same white as each side of B. L2 holds 0.195x, so the leading, the space between lines, barely moves and the block keeps L2’s density.
  • At its minimum it is drawn from the 16 px wordmark master on whole pixels, 79 px wide. Its crisp share there, the part of its ink that lands on whole pixels, is 0.69, against 0.58 for L2 at its 90 px minimum; the guide passes 0.60.

If you would rather set the name on a center line, choose S4 in its place. We recommend shipping one stacked lockup, so producers never have to choose between two.

How we spaced them

  • Every join holds the white the wordmark holds beside B. We measure white as an area: slice the space between two shapes into thin strips, count each strip up to 0.15x past the nearest point, and average the strips. The wordmark master holds 0.203x between H and B and 0.203x between B and A, the hinge spacing set in October (change C-007).
  • Beside B, each stack keeps the master’s own placement, so the white there is the wordmark’s. Between lines the strips run upright, and we moved the lower line until its white matched the hinge. A strip where one line has no ink, such as the gap between two letters, counts as open up to the 0.15x limit.
  • Today’s L2 holds 0.195x between its lines by the same measure, close to the hinge already, and its B stands 0.10x clear of the N.
  • We center B on its own line by its ink, as the hinge diagram does. Flush-left lines start at their ink, as L2’s do.
  • The 24 px and 16 px wordmark masters carry wider spacing for small sizes, an average of 0.238x and 0.295x on the two sides of B. The small drawings follow their own master and move lines in whole pixels, so their white lands within half a pixel of that target.
  • Like the wordmark, each stack has three drawings: the 16 px drawing for capitals 14 to 17 px, the 24 px drawing for capitals 18 to 36 px and the display drawing from 37 px. Set color to the surface’s text token and --amp to its signature token; on Prussian set both to White.
  • x means one cap height of the wordmark.
