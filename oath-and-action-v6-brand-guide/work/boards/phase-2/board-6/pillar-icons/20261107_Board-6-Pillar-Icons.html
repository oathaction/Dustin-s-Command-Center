Board 6 · Pillar icons

Four pillar icons from the Civic Line

You asked us to revisit the civic icons and show a few thoughts on the four pillar icons. We read all 122 Civic Line drawings and drew three sets of four from them. Each set stands first in the pillar row, so you can compare them in one view, and then in use. Each pillar is now its icon with its word, with no grid corner.

Every icon on this page is a Civic Line master as supplied, in one of its four editions or its Charcoal tile, placed at the size shown. Nothing is redrawn or recolored.

02

Our recommendation: keep Set A and change how it is used

Set A is the four marks you approved on October 29. We would keep them because they are the only four of the 122 drawn for the pillars, so no topic page or reform page uses the same drawing. Sets B and C each borrow two or three drawings that already name a topic, such as Voting or Public Records, and a reader would meet one drawing with two meanings. Set A’s hand also covers any next step a reader takes, where a ballot or a signed page names one kind of step.

Five changes in how Set A is used

  1. The word sits beside the icon in every row, and always below 48 px.
  2. In a row the four icons go one color, and the current pillar keeps its red from 48 px up. Below 48 px, bold type and an underline mark the current pillar’s word.
  3. At 16 px the icon is decoration beside its word. Its line is 0.42 px wide there, and no drawing on this page keeps a clear shape at that size.
  4. The grid corner retires, and the icon takes its place in kickers and on section openers.
  5. The app icon and avatars use the two-color tile on Charcoal, from 76 px.

You would accept two weaknesses. Track is the busiest of the four and the faintest at 16 px. Act measures about a fifth smaller than Explain in the same box (63.9 against 79.4 px at 128 px). If you would rather the pillars show people and public acts, Set C is our second choice. Set B ties its four together best, at the cost of three topic drawings and two pillars that both show writing.

One question for you

Below 48 px a pillar mark sits with its word. In a kicker the content label sits beside the icon instead: Explainer, Tracker and Act carry the pillar’s word, and Guide does not name Connect. We recommend the label counts as the word in a kicker, because it already tells the reader what kind of piece it is, and the pillar’s own word stands on its section opener and in the pillar row. If you would rather, the kicker keeps a topic icon and the pillar mark stays out of it.

03

How we chose from the 122

We read all 122 drawings on the five contact sheets, then narrowed them in three steps.

  1. We kept the 72 drawings on sheets 01 to 03. The 50 reform drawings on sheets 04 and 05 each stand for a proposal the atlas weighs, and a pillar mark on a proposal would look like an endorsement.
  2. We kept the 29 of those that still read at 24 px, the kicker size, in the October 29 trace review.
  3. We set aside ten whose subject names a harm or one policy area, such as Market Power or Healthcare, and Feedback Loop, which raters took for a refresh sign in all three earlier icon tests.

That left the 19 below, sorted by the pillar each could serve. Icons in the three sets carry the set’s name.

04

Set A, drawn for the pillars Recommended

Explain: CL-019 · Track: CL-020 · Connect: CL-021 · Act: CL-022

These four were drawn as one row for the pillars on the first Civic Line sheet, and you approved them on October 29. Each holds one idea, with one red part doing the pillar’s work: the rays over the book, the pin and the handle, the shared lens and the pressed ring. They hold together through the line and the red, since their subjects differ. Set A keeps today’s drawings and changes how they are used, as the recommendation lists.

Closest pair in shape at 24 px: Explain and Connect, more alike than 60 percent of the 7,381 pairs in the Civic Line.

Why each icon fits

ExplainCL-019 Explain

An open book with three rays rising from its spine. A reader sees something opened up and understood, which is Explain’s job: making the rules and their effects understandable.

Watch. The same book appears in CL-068 Education and R-13 Congressional Capacity, so on a page about schools two books would sit close together.

TrackCL-020 Track

A route winds from a map pin to a magnifier, the way a parcel is tracked from place to place. Track follows a decision and keeps its record within reach.

Watch. It holds three subjects, the most of the four. Its magnifier is the Civic Line’s sign for oversight, so a newcomer may give it to Explain. It is the faintest of the four at 16 px.

ConnectCL-021 Connect

Two equal rings overlap, and the lens they share is red: a reader and the office that decides, meeting where they overlap.

Watch. Many readers will call it a Venn diagram first. Its rings are drawn as outlines, which keeps it apart from payment-card logos built from two filled circles.

ActCL-022 Act

A finger presses a round control, with short rays where it lands. It says do it now, which suits a next step of any kind.

Watch. It is the tap gesture from phone screens, so on a website Act can read as a button to press. Its drawing measures about a fifth smaller than Explain’s in the same box.

Sizes

Each icon at 16 and 24 px in one color, and at 48 and 128 px in two colors. The line is 0.42 px wide at 16 px and 0.63 px at 24 px, and red under a full pixel reads as pink, so both small sizes stay one color. Under each ladder, the 16 and 24 px icons appear as the screen draws them, enlarged four times.

Pairs that risk confusion

Set A

  • Track and Explain: the magnifier in Track reads as “look into”, which a newcomer may give to Explain.
  • Explain and Connect: they are the most alike in shape in Set A, and a newcomer who reads the rings as “compare” may give them to Explain.

Most alike in shape at 24 px: Explain and Connect, 60th percentile; Explain and Track, 17th percentile.

Set B

  • Connect and Act: the letter and the signed page both show writing, and either could be the other.
  • Track and Explain: the folder and the book both hold reading, and a newcomer may give the folder to Explain.

Most alike in shape at 24 px: Track and Connect, 36th percentile; Track and Act, 32nd percentile.

Set C

  • Connect and Act match at once, but a newcomer may read the ballot as Act meaning “vote” and nothing else.
  • Track and Explain keep Set A’s small risk with the magnifier.

Most alike in shape at 24 px: Connect and Act, 75th percentile; Explain and Act, 44th percentile.

Likeness in shape: we drew all 122 icons at 24 px, blurred each slightly and compared every pair, 7,381 in all. A pair at the 90th percentile looks more alike than nine pairs in ten.

What raters confused in the earlier icon tests

  • Lids and opened boxes read as cameras and notification badges. No set here uses a lid.
  • Boxes holding a card read as printers or trays, in all three tests. Set C’s ballot box and Set B’s folder carry that risk.
  • Loops with arrows read as a refresh or sync sign, in all three tests. We left Feedback Loop out.
  • Two halves facing each other read as a no-entry sign or a broadcast signal. Set A’s rings overlap and close, and a newcomer names them a Venn diagram.
  • A house with a red door competed with Act’s door. No set here uses a house or a door.

Those tests used other drawings, from the three concepts before the Civic Line, and agent raters in place of people.
