ReformR-01All-party primaries with ranked general elections

On this loop it acts at L25, Decisive primaries reduce general-election pressure and L08, Safe seats make the primary decisive and C2, How often the primary decides the winner, marked in red.

Every voter, whatever their party, could vote in one first-round election, and two or more candidates would advance to a November ballot that all voters use. In seats that one party usually wins, the choice now made in a low-turnout party primary would move to the full electorate. In Massachusetts, unenrolled voters already pick one party's ballot. Under Question 3, every voter would see all candidates on one primary ballot from 2028. The Atlas takes no position on Question 3 or Ballot Measure 2.

Atlas V7 lists two more reforms on this loop: R-02, Ranked-choice voting for single-winner races, R-03, Proportional elections in multi-seat House districts. This card names one reform, so only its targets are red.

Evidence for the problem: Plausible. Evidence for the remedy: Contested, credible studies disagree.

Confidence in the effects (V7): medium (participation); low (moderation).
