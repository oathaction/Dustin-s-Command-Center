Atlas V7 title: Electoral consequences can improve public performance

The system corrects itself

Follow the ring clockwise from D5, Public service quality. It passes C3 and C5 and closes on D5 where it began.

How it should work

Poor public performance raises the risk that incumbents lose. Where seats are competitive, that risk turns into defeats or forced retirements. Officials who remain, and those who replace them, have reason to perform better, which eases the pressure again.

ReformR-07Public matching for small donations and vouchers

On this loop it acts at C3, Risk of losing office, marked in red. Outside this loop it also acts at B1, Money spent to influence politics.

Candidates who join a voluntary program would receive public money that multiplies small donations, or voters would receive vouchers to give to the candidates they choose. Candidates without wealthy networks would gain in the first election cycle, because small donors would become worth their time. Incumbents who rely on large-donor networks would lose ground over several cycles.

Atlas V7 lists three more reforms on this loop: R-01, All-party primaries with ranked general elections, R-02, Ranked-choice voting for single-winner races, R-06, Hold local elections alongside November races. This card names one reform, so only its targets are red.

Evidence for the problem: Documented. Evidence for the remedy: Plausible, a coherent mechanism with some evidence.

Confidence in the effects (V7): medium.
