Safe seats can let officeholders write the rules that keep seats safe

LoopR10Safe seats shift accountability to small primary electorates

Read it aloud“Seats with little electoral competition raise how often the primary decides the winner, which lowers the risk of losing office; lower risk raises officeholders' control over election rules, which raises the number of seats with little electoral competition.”

Feeds itself

Reinforcing loop. V7: “A feedback loop whose returning effect amplifies the first change. Improvement or decline can reinforce itself.”

What the return line means

L42, Incumbent-drawn maps create safe seats. Officeholders who draw district lines can pack or split voters to make their seats safer, and redrawing between censuses repeats the effect sooner.

Least certain step

L53, plausible. V7: “The weakest step is L53: Officials safe from defeat write rules for themselves, graded plausible: its evidence is the 2025 mid-decade redistricting in states with one-party control and legal analysis.”

One turn

2 to 10 years. V7 gives a loop the delay of its slowest step.

Now

Running up. V7 tracks it by “Share of House seats settled in primaries, 2012 to 2024”.

Where a reform acts

ReformR-01 All-party primaries with ranked general elections

Red marks where R-01 acts in this loop: L25, Decisive primaries reduce general-election pressure; L08, Safe seats make the primary decisive; C2, How often the primary decides the winner. Reform R-47 also acts on C2; V7 does not list it on this loop.

Evidence that the problem exists: Plausible. Evidence that the remedy works: Contested. Confidence in the effects, in V7’s words: “medium (participation); low (moderation)”.
