Every loop draws its own route

The atlas has 23 feedback loops. Twelve have two steps, seven have three and four have four, so a loop icon drawn by its step count repeats three shapes. This study draws each loop as the path it takes across one shared plan of the system. The plan is the same in every thumbnail, so each one also shows where in the system its loop lives.

Headlines on this page are studio drafts. Every label, sign and grade is read from the Atlas V7 model export by script (DR-20). Decision DR-15.

01

One plan of the system, shared by every thumbnail

The plan has three tiers. The five outside drivers run across the top. The civic core sits in the middle. The economy and technology layers run along the bottom, where V7 puts them under the core.

The core is a ring in V7's letter order. A, B and C run across the top row, then D and E come back along the bottom. The core's 38 connections between areas join only A and B, B and C, C and D, D and E, E and A, B and D, and B and E, so in the ring every one of them joins two neighbors. In a single row from A to E, 10 of them would jump over an area.

Where each variable sits

  1. Each area holds a grid of slots: two columns by three rows for A, B and C, two by two for E, four by two for D, and two rows in each area of the bottom tier.
  2. Each variable takes the slot nearest the average place of everything it connects to, counting all 118 connections of every type. One assignment per area finds the arrangement with the least total distance.
  3. The script repeats the assignments until no variable moves (7 rounds), and tries all 24 orders of the bottom tier's four areas. It keeps the order with the shortest total length of connections: Credit, trade and environment, Household budgets and wealth, Production and income, Technology mechanisms.

The margin

The three sketched loops have stations that are not variables in the model. They sit in a margin on the left, outside every area, beside the variables they are tied to: the station a sketch names (R9 touches E2), or else the variables its reforms target.

Plan size 6.6 by 7.64 columns; with the margin, 7.80 by 8.14. Positions for all 51 variables are in 20261030_Loop-Route-Geometry.json.

How to read a route

R10, “Safe seats shift accountability to small primary electorates”, at twice catalog size. Each step is an arc between two stations. Arcs bow outward from the middle of the loop, so a route always reads as a closed circuit. A two-station loop becomes a small ring.

StepWhat it saysSignEvidence and lineDelay
L08Safe seats raises Primary decisiveness+Documented, solidunder 2 years
L25Primary decisiveness lowers Electoral pressure−Well supported, solidunder 2 years
L53Electoral pressure lowers Incumbent control of election rules−Plausible, dashed2 to 10 years
L42Incumbent control of election rules raises Safe seats. This step closes the loop.+Documented, solid2 to 10 years

Weakest step: L53, plausible. Delay stays out of the thumbnail; the loop card carries it.

Solid: documented (on the public record) or well supported (strong research evidence)
Dashed: plausible (some evidence)
Dash and dot: contested (evidence disputed)
Dotted: hypothesis (untested)
Tag with a plus: the two variables move the same way. The tag points the way the step runs. A plus does not mean the change is good.
Tag with a minus: the two variables move opposite ways.
Ring: the loop's first station in V7. The bar marks the closing step, the one that returns to the ring.
Hollow line and open station: a sketched step with no numbered connection, off the map.
Heavier outline: an area where the loop stops. Lighter outlines are the rest of the system.

How small a thumbnail can go

The marks keep their size while the plan shrinks, so short steps run out of room first. The script finds, for each loop, the narrowest width at which every step still holds its tag and one whole repeat of its line pattern. R12 sets the floor at 310 px through its closing step; every other loop reads at 282 px or less.

Compact256 to 308 pxroute, V7 line code, a chevron for direction, closing bar, start ring, stations, plan; signs move to the caption
Locatorunder 256 pxroute as a plain line, stations, plan; the caption carries everything else

06

Twenty-three different pictures

Chromium renders every thumbnail, and the script scores all 253 pairs with the studio's D1-16 method: the mean of the overlap of the two filled route shapes and the correlation of the two drawings after a 3 by 3 blur. Two marks count as distinct under 0.60. The shared plan is left in, which raises every score.

At 318 px the highest score is 0.57, for R1 and R6, and the median is 0.23. None of the 253 pairs reaches the limit. At 200 px, in the locator form, the highest is 0.62, for R1 and R6, which is over the limit.

A stricter squint test blurs each drawing by about a fortieth of its width before correlating. It has no limit in the decision log, so it is reported, not used to pass or fail. The closest pair there is R4a and R12 at 0.77.

Open points

The sign mark is the pointing tag, the studio's recommendation in Choice 3 of the October 28 review, which Dustin has not yet decided. If he picks the circled sign, each step needs an arrowhead as well, and the floor rises.

The plan is a proposal for every concept to adapt. A concept may stretch a tier or redraw the outlines; the ring order and the slot of each variable are what keep its thumbnails comparable with the others.

Two-step loops make small rings. Their size and place on the plan carry the difference between them. R13 and R18 both sit in area D and share D2. From D2, R13's other station (D1) sits straight below and R18's (D4) below and to the left, and the pair scores 0.28 at 318 px.

No reader has seen these thumbnails. The ten-second test and a blind matching test (five readers, a thumbnail and three titles) would show whether the measured distinctness holds for people.

The sketched loops sit beside the variables their reforms target. That placement comes from the reforms, not from a connection in the model, and each card says so.

The faint outlines of the areas a loop does not visit are context. They are drawn in Stone at 0.6 px; the outlines of the areas it visits are 1.3 px.
