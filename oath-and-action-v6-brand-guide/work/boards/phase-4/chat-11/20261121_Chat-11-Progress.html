The website

The application kit, the studio’s tool that draws each application from the guide’s tokens and components, drew every page below, and chapter 20’s plates show the same drawings. Each page comes in the light theme at 1440, 1024, 390 and 360 px and in the dark theme at 1440 and 390 px, as you set in DR-311. The home page

The home page opens on the pillar row: four Charcoal cells, Explain, Track, Connect and Act, each heading one piece. The first leads to our explainer on special districts. Track follows H.R. 7008, the Stop Insider Trading Act, a bill that would limit stock purchases by members of Congress and require photo ID to vote in federal elections. Connect asks who sets an electric bill, and Act carries the FDA’s proposed rule on NAC, N-acetyl-L-cysteine, with the red take-part mark on its open comment period. Further down, the atlas’s section opens on its own title bar and shows chart S10, the atlas’s claims by evidence grade.

The article

The article finishes chat 10’s studio sample, What a special district does. Its first screen sets the count, 39,555 special districts in 2022 of the 90,888 governments the Census Bureau counted, on a Charcoal panel beside the opening paragraph. Chart S86, the count of governments by type, comes next, and the page ends on a step a reader in any state can take: read their state’s summary in the Census Bureau’s report on how each state organizes its governments.

Source: U.S. Census Bureau, 2022 Census of Governments: Organization, tables CG2200ORG01 and CG2200ORG02, released August 24, 2023, United States. Retrieved November 12, 2026. School districts run by a state, county, city or township count with that government.

The Act page

The Act page is a process piece, Rule 01.22’s form for a procedure any reader can follow. It shows how to comment on the FDA’s proposed rule on NAC, docket FDA-2025-N-2699, before comments close on January 19, 2027. Its status panel, Where the rule stands, sits beside the headline: step 3, Comments open, carries the take-part mark, and the red ask, “Comment at Regulations.gov”, stands under the steps. The section “What the record shows” quotes the rule with its page numbers. The rule records no objection to the change so far, so the section prints what the record holds and adds no opponents’ paragraph to match the petitioners’ length (D4-108). The four steps, the comment panel with the ask again, and the rule at a glance follow.

Color by screen: Rule 05.1 under DR-310

Rule 05.1 sets how much of a view is Newsprint, the brand’s paper color, how much is dark and how much is red. You answered DR-310 so that a long page meets it screen by screen: “On a long page the limits hold for the first screen and for each section’s opening screen; screens of running text between them may hold more Newsprint.”

The kit measures each page’s first screen and the screen where each section opens, one window tall, on its own captures. A light screen must hold 60 to 85 percent Newsprint, at most one dark panel a section and no more than 5 percent red. A dark screen is judged on red alone, since the Newsprint limit belongs to the light view and DR-204 sets the dark theme. All 90 screens pass. The highest Newsprint share is 83.7 percent, the Act page’s “What the FDA proposes” screen at 1440 px; the most red is 3.7 percent, the Act page’s “How to send a comment” screen at 390 px, light theme.

Figures are percent of the screen. Dark counts Charcoal and Prussian together. Read from the kit’s plates, work/build/app-kit/plates/a06.json to a08.json.

Chapter 21’s plan, in brief

Chapter 21 shows our systems atlas, Who Do They Answer To?, as a reader meets it inside the website. Every atlas page sits under the site’s masthead with the atlas’s own title bar beneath (DR-307). Every ID sits in a typed chip, the small label that names a record’s kind beside its ID (Rule 18.24), and every claim links to the record that grades it. The data visualization lead read every ID, sign, grade and count in the plan from the atlas model by script, and the build reads them again.

The plan draws four views, each at 1440 and 390 px on Newsprint and in the dark theme. They rotate across health care, wages, Congress and the agencies, and presidential power over watchdogs, and each prints a finding the record holds that one side may want to skip (DR-55, DR-93).

The whole system drawn once, its eight parts as boxes with a count on every flow between them, from the model’s 51 variables and 118 connections. Coming to Phase 4 board 1

Each item below changes approved work or a rule you set, so it waits for your named answer at board 1.

ED11-1

Rule 05.1’s specification row

DR-310 added its sentence to Rule 05.1’s text. The rule’s specification row, “Newsprint in each view”, still reads 60 to 85 percent for every view, so a builder who reads only the row holds every screen of a long page to 85.

The studio recommends the change, in the editor’s words for the row: “60 to 85 percent; on a long page, screens of running text between the opening screens may hold more”.

ED11-2

The wordmark at 360 px against Rule 03.6

Rule 03.6 sets L1, the wordmark alone, from its 24 px master on a site under 48em, 768 px. At 360 px that master clears Menu by 4 px, so chapter 20 draws L1 from the 16 px master on screens under 390 px. The two rules disagree at 360 px.

The studio recommends keeping the 16 px master at 360 px, as the guide’s own masthead has done through 372 px since your reply 10 on board 3 (DR-291), and adding the exception to Rule 03.6 as a row: “Site, under 24.375em: L1 from the 16 px master, 152 px wide”.

DD11-NF1

Rule 01.22’s “same length” where the record holds one side

Rule 01.22 asks a process piece to set out what supporters and opponents have said at the same length. The FDA’s rule on NAC records no objector, so equal length would mean writing an opponent the record lacks. The Act page follows DR-93, one standard of evidence with no invented counterweight, and prints the record at its own length (D4-108).

The studio recommends that Rule 01.22 read “sets out what the record shows from each side, at the length the record holds”, the wording chapter 20’s Rule 20.8 already uses.

ED-F1

A layer color in the atlas

The creative director’s finishing list for the atlas asks for a colored rule down each part box, Prussian, Verdigris or Stone by layer, to give the atlas a signature of its own. Rule 15.25’s approved resolution (G3-03) reads “Layers coded by a frame and a name, never a color”, so chapter 21’s plan frames the parts as Rule 15.9 does.

The studio recommends running the wall test first. In chat 12 the creative director covers the wordmark on chapter 21’s built views and asks whether they still read as ours. The question comes to you only if that test fails, as a choice between reopening Rule 15.25’s row for color and keeping the frames.

AL11-1

Chapter 18’s menu: the current-page mark

Rule 18.6 marks the part a reader is in with aria-current="page", the code a screen reader announces as “current page”. On the article, which sits inside Explain, a screen reader calls the Explain link the current page, though it leads to Explain’s front page. The website follows Rule 18.6 as approved.

The studio recommends the accessibility lead’s fix: “page” on a part’s own front page and “true”, announced as “current”, on the pages inside it. The bold words and the 2 px underline stay as they are.

AL11-2

Chapter 18’s menu: the focus fix W1

The menu’s title row stays at the top while the menu scrolls, and the menu sets no scroll padding. At 320 by 256 px, the size a 1,280 px window reaches at 400 percent zoom, a part reached with Shift+Tab can sit hidden under the row, which fails WCAG 2.4.11, Focus Not Obscured. The website carries the fix in its own code, where a rerun found 0 hidden in 6 of 6 runs. Chapter 18’s own code is unchanged.

The studio recommends the same scroll padding in chapter 18’s menu, so the guide’s component matches the website.

C16-CD-01, held from chat 10

Chapter 01’s photographs in color

Rules 01.6 and 01.8 show their two federal photographs in color. Gate 1 set black and white by default, with color where color is evidence (S10), and chapter 16 follows S10, so the guide shows the same photographs two ways.

The studio recommends the creative director’s grayscale on the three plates, a style change at no byte cost.
