1. The idea: red marks where a person steps in

Each icon carries one red element, the node. It is Direction A's shared node, a 4 px disc in a 1 px seat, and it sits where a person takes part. On the lockmark it is the lever. In the atlas it marks the step where a reform acts. The eight test icons follow the same logic, shown here in concept C's flat cut.

ExplainWhere the corner lifts away, so you can see inside.

TrackThe stage a decision has reached.

ConnectThe common ground between two halves.

ActThe handle, where you take hold.

BallotThe mark you make on your ballot.

Campaign moneyThe coin that changes hands.

Feedback loopThe step where a reform acts on the loop.

SearchThe one thing the lens is looking for.

The lockmark's lever is the precedent: one red node in a grid of Charcoal ones. The icons borrow its logic and its disc.

Rule 05.3, word for word (D2-01)

The wordmark's ampersand and the lockmark's lever are signature red and do not count, and neither do the ampersands inside the hinge diagram and the hinge-circle badge, which are identity marks. The hinge diagram never shares a view with the wordmark and never stands in for the hinge divider. An icon's red accent counts as signature red when the icon stands alone, as an app tile, an avatar or a single feature icon. In a row or a menu, icons go one color and only the current one keeps its red. Each view (one screen, card, slide or printed page) then carries one working red, in this order of precedence: an error the reader must fix; the step a reader can take now; the one point the headline names, which in a systems figure is where a reform acts. One job may take several marks, such as each target of one reform. A hinge headline's ampersand uses the view's working red. The hinge divider's ampersand takes its surface's ink: Charcoal on Newsprint and White, Newsprint on Charcoal, White on Prussian. In the guide itself, specimens, labeled failure cases and palette swatches do not count. Red never sits on Prussian.

How the rule applies to these icons

  • Alone. An app tile, an avatar or a single feature icon keeps its red. That red counts as signature red, as the wordmark's ampersand does.
  • In a row or a menu. Icons go one color, and the current one keeps its red. On Newsprint that red takes a 1 px Charcoal keyline, because it marks a state, and the current label sets in bold, so the state never rests on color alone.
  • On Prussian. One color, White, with no red.

3. Three concepts at a glance

Each card shows the eight icons at 48 px, then the true 24 and 16 px masters. All three share the grid, the node and the idea in section 1. They differ in how the drawing carries the node.

Option

A. Line and node

Premium line icons in the manner of the blue and orange set you sent, with the node as the one red shape.

  • One 2 px line at 24 and 16 px, flat ends, mitered corners.
  • Lines stop at the node's 1 px seat, so the red sits just off the line.
  • At 16 px the line stays 2 px. A counter narrower than 2 px closes into a solid.
  • Red share 7% to 12% of the ink at 24 px.

24 and 16 px masters

Refined: the red shape is the node in every icon, so all eight carry one red form.

Option

B. Flat kit

Built from a few geometric pieces, in the manner of the purple and the pink and gold sets.

  • Solid pieces from a small kit: square, bar, disc, half disc, quarter ring, triangle.
  • Square corners. Pieces sit apart by 1 px cuts, as the ballot sits in its slot.
  • The node is one solid piece in a 1 px seat.
  • Red share 6% to 8% of the ink at 24 px.
48 px
24 and 16 px masters

Refined: the red piece is the node, so the kit's red is always the same disc.

Option Recommended

C. One drawing, two cuts

The Line and Flat pairing from the collage you sent, built on the skeletons you approved.

  • Direction A's geometry drawn once, as closed outlines with square corners.
  • Line cut: each outline as a 2 px wall. Flat cut: each outline filled.
  • Both cuts share one silhouette, so a product can swap them by size or by state.
  • Red share 6% to 11% of the ink at 24 px.

Two refinements to the approved skeletons

  • Act gains a lintel. The frame now runs across the top of the door. At 16 px the Phase 1 frame read close to the chapter number 01: against Inter Tight 800 "01" the flat Act scored 0.60, and with the lintel it scores 0.42. In A it falls from 0.51 to 0.38. The node stays on the center line.
  • Track rises. Y2 asked for a raised stair. The base moves up from 22 to 20, which brings the flat stair's center of mass from 17 percent of the field below center to 12.5 percent.

8. Bare and in three holders

The holders are a square, a rounded square and a circle. The rounded square is a true superellipse, |x|5 + |y|5 = 1, so its curvature changes without a step all the way around. It comes close to the corner of Apple's app icons; Apple does not publish its own mask. The SVG master draws it with 96 cubic segments that stay within 0.16 px of the exact curve at 1024 px.

Safe area

  • The icon's 20-unit live area is half the holder's side, and its 24-unit field is 60 percent. A 24 px master sits in a 40 px holder and a 16 px master in a 28 px holder, both on whole pixels. Larger tiles scale the display master.
  • In the circle, ink also stays within 80 percent of the diameter.
  • Measured on 576 rendered tiles, every icon in every holder and color at 40 and 80 px: 576 keep their ink inside the live area, the smallest margin is 25% of the side, and in the circle the farthest ink sits at 34.5% of the diameter from the center.
  • Tiles and avatars use concept C's flat cut, since a tile is a standalone mark.

What the failures say

  • Balance in A, line. Act +33% over the median. A door carries a frame and a leaf, and the leaf needs a 6-unit counter for its handle. The full-set pass can narrow the door toward the portrait keyline.
  • Balance in C, line cut. Track −21%, Feedback loop −23%, Search −21% under it. Open drawings carry less ink than outlined solids at the same stroke. Each sits 1 to 3 points past the line; a small enlargement in the full-set pass would bring them in.
  • Standalone red on Newsprint measures 2.96:1. Rule 05.3 treats it as signature red, like the wordmark's ampersand. Decision 3 below asks whether to keep that or add the keyline everywhere on Newsprint.
  • Center of mass. Track sits low in every concept, the stair's nature: 14.1 percent of the field in A and 12.5 percent in B and C's flat cut, against Phase 1's 12 percent. It was 17 to 18 percent in Phase 1. The brief's balance check is ink area, so this row carries no pass mark.

12. Rubric and recommendation

Scores on the Master Brief section 6 rubric, 1 to 5. The ship threshold is 4 on every criterion.

B, flat kit444454Most even weight of the three and strong at 16 px. It drops the line companion Gate 1 approved (Y4), and its kit cuts move a step away from Direction A.

Recommendation: concept C

C keeps the filled geometry you approved at Gate 1 and gives it the line companion Y4 already set to start at 24 px. Because both cuts share one silhouette, a menu can show the current item in the flat cut and the rest in line, which meets the state rule in section 10 of the Master Brief with a change of shape. At 16 px, in tiles and in avatars it is the flat cut, which passes every 16 px test here.

What it costs. Every icon is drawn twice at 24 px and once at 16 px, so the full set of about 60 icons (20 topics, the atlas symbols, status marks and utility icons) means about 180 masters, plus a display master per cut. A script has to check on every rebuild that the two cuts share their outer edges. Track, Feedback loop and Search in the line cut need a small enlargement to sit inside the balance band. If you prefer one cut only, B costs about a third less and loses the line companion.

13. Decisions for Dustin

  1. 1. Which concept goes forward to the full set?Options: A, line and node; B, flat kit; or C, one drawing in two cuts.
    Studio recommends: C. It extends what you approved at Gate 1 and gives products a state change by shape.
  2. 2. Red's job across the familyOptions: Red marks where a person steps in, on the node of every icon; or red only on Act, as Y3 had it before G1-07.
    Studio recommends: Red marks where a person steps in. It gives every icon one reason to carry red, the way the lockmark's lever does.
  3. 3. A standalone icon's red on NewsprintOptions: Keep it bare, as signature red at 2.96:1 like the wordmark's ampersand; or give it the 1 px Charcoal keyline everywhere on Newsprint.
    Studio recommends: Keep it bare when the icon stands alone, and keep the keyline in rows, where red marks a state.
  4. 4. Does the current icon's red in a menu count as the view's working red?Options: Count it as working red, so a menu never shares a view with an error or an ask in red; or treat it as signature red, as Rule 05.3 does for a single feature icon.
    Studio recommends: Treat it as signature red. A menu then never competes with an error or an ask for the view's one working red. This adds a clause to Rule 05.3, so it needs your named approval.
  5. 5. Act's frame becomes a full lintelOptions: Approve the lintel; or keep the Phase 1 frame over the opening only.
    Studio recommends: Approve. At 16 px it takes the flat Act from 0.60 against "01" to 0.42, and the node stays on the center line.
  6. 6. MastersOptions: Hinted 16 and 24 px masters with a separate display master from 48 px; or one unhinted master at every size.
    Studio recommends: Hinted masters. Unhinted, the ring icons fall to a crisp pixel share of 0.42, under the 0.60 line; hinted, every master reaches 0.89 or better.
  7. 7. The rounded-square holderOptions: A true superellipse with exponent 5; or Figma's published corner smoothing at 60 percent, which steps in curvature where its curve meets the arc.
    Studio recommends: The superellipse. Its curvature never steps, and its geometry is one line to state.
  8. 8. Default tile color for app icons and avatarsOptions: Charcoal tile with a Newsprint icon; or Newsprint tile with a Charcoal icon (both stay in the system, G1-09).
    Studio recommends: Charcoal for app icons and avatars, since red on Charcoal measures 4.49:1. Newsprint tiles for light pages and print.
