Overprint

A fine Charcoal line printed over one solid plane of Pillar Red, set off register toward the lower left, the way a second ink lands on a newspaper page. The red sits under the part of each subject the story is about. The line drawing names the subject without it, so the red can come and go: it shows on an icon that stands alone and on the current item in a row. From 24 px up the family is this line; at 16 px it changes to a solid cut in one color.

Construction

Rule 05.3, word for word (D2-01)

The wordmark's ampersand and the lockmark's lever are signature red and do not count, and neither do the ampersands inside the hinge diagram and the hinge-circle badge, which are identity marks. The hinge diagram never shares a view with the wordmark and never stands in for the hinge divider. An icon's red accent counts as signature red when the icon stands alone, as an app tile, an avatar or a single feature icon. In a row or a menu, icons go one color and only the current one keeps its red. Each view (one screen, card, slide or printed page) then carries one working red, in this order of precedence: an error the reader must fix; the step a reader can take now; the one point the headline names, which in a systems figure is where a reform acts. One job may take several marks, such as each target of one reform. A hinge headline's ampersand uses the view's working red. The hinge divider's ampersand takes its surface's ink: Charcoal on Newsprint and White, Newsprint on Charcoal, White on Prussian. In the guide itself, specimens, labeled failure cases and palette swatches do not count. Red never sits on Prussian.

Here: alone, as an app tile, an avatar or a single feature icon, the plane counts as signature red. In a row or menu the icons go line only and the current one keeps its plane. On Prussian the plane goes and the White line stands alone. At 16 px every icon is one color.

15. Decisions

  1. The pillar marks by sizeDirection A's skeletons drawn in line with a red plane from 24 px up, and as Direction A's filled pieces in one color at 16 px. Y1's solid treatment and Y4's line companion trade roles. Needs Dustin's named approval if Overprint is chosen. The skeletons stay.
  2. No red at 16 pxSixteen-pixel icons are always one color, and the current item shows by its label. Recommended: it narrows how Rule 05.3's icon sentences apply and leaves its words unchanged.
  3. Act's lintel at 16 px onlyThe solid cut keeps the full lintel; the line cut keeps the floor line and no lintel. Needs a named approval for the 16 px cut: “01” scores 0.23 with it.
  4. The plane ruleThe plane fills one drawn shape and prints off register at one fixed offset. Recommended: every red then belongs to a drawn part, and the offset carries the concept.
  5. Connect's exceptionConnect's plane centers on the gap and prints straight down. Recommended: Rule 01.1 bars red that leans toward a side.
  6. Standalone red on NewsprintA plane alone on Newsprint measures 2.96:1 and stands as signature red. Recommended, as round one's decision 3 proposed: the plane never carries an edge a reader needs.
  7. Topic symbols carry a node only where it is part of the subjectOutside the pillar marks only Feedback loop's three stations are nodes. For Dustin: this narrows Y5's “Direction A's grammar” for topics.
