  13. A13

    A13. How does chapter 01 say what nonpartisan means, and which finding shows it?

    Your November 6 direction says nonpartisan means one standard of evidence for everyone, with findings reported as the record shows them. Chapter 01 teaches even treatment in Rules 01.2, 01.11 and 01.16, and it never says what a writer does when the record falls on one side.

    Proposed wording. Chapter 01’s opening gains: “Nonpartisan, for us, means one standard of evidence and one way of describing sources, actors and parties, applied to everyone. We report each finding as the record shows it. When the record shows one party or official doing more of something, we say so, name who acted and size the claim to its evidence grade. No piece adds, softens or drops a finding to make the sides look even.”

    Rule 01.11 gains: “When the record shows one party using a mechanism more, report it that way, with the counts, as Rule 01.13 asks, and add no case to even the count.” Its reason becomes: “Where both parties use a mechanism, a case from each, told the same way, puts the mechanism in front of the reader. Where the record shows one side doing more, the finding stands as the record shows it.” Rule 01.20’s reason gains “in a hinge headline”, and Rule 01.12’s reason says “the spread of examples” where it now says “balance”.

    The example. A party-line vote shows each side voting as a bloc, the same act on both sides, so it shows this rule weakly. Our systems atlas holds a better candidate, graded documented (issue I155): after the 2020 election, claims of fraud large enough to change the result failed in court. Lost, Not Stolen, a 2022 review by conservative lawyers and former officials, found no evidence of outcome-changing fraud in any of 64 court cases. In one, the Trump campaign asked a federal appeals court to stop Pennsylvania’s certified result, and the court refused, noting that the campaign “never alleges that any ballot was fraudulent.” Before it prints, the research lead reads the court’s opinion and the review in full and writes its source line.

    Options

    1. (a)Adopt the wording now, and add the example before Gate 2 once the research lead has checked it, starting from the 2020 court record in atlas issue I155 Recommended
    2. (b)Adopt the wording now, with the House vote on H.R. 7008 as the example
    3. (c)Leave chapter 01 as it stands, with your direction kept in the decision log

    We recommend (a) Adopt the wording now, and add the example before Gate 2 once the research lead has checked it, starting from the 2020 court record in atlas issue I155

    Why Chapter 01 is where a newcomer learns what nonpartisan means, so your direction belongs there in plain words. The 2020 court record shows one side doing more of something your direction names, a threat to the peaceful transfer of power, and a writer applying the rule names who acted, uses record language and adds no counterweight. Its key finding comes from a review written by conservatives, which shows one standard of evidence at work. The House vote, option (b), repeats the framing a fact check flagged, “Democrats voted against a stock trading ban”, even with the bill described whole.

    Rules it touches

    • Chapter 01 Purpose
