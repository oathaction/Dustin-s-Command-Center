Item 2 Defect fix Recommended: 2b (design judgment)

Equal hinge gaps around the ampersand

V5 states a hinge gap of 0.10x on each side of the ampersand, measured ink to ink. Here x is one cap height of the wordmark, 100 units. The right gap measures 0.083x, and it still reads wider than the left, because the wedge of white above the A's diagonal adds to it. So the ampersand leans toward OATH.

Two fixes are drawn. Option 2a applies the stated rule: ACTION moves 1.63 units right so both nearest gaps measure 0.10x. The white on the right then grows further, to 1.24 times the left. Option 2b sets the gaps by area (a design judgment: I chose the area model and its 0.15x depth by eye). It measures the white between the ampersand and each neighbor, row by row across the cap band, each row counted up to 0.15x deeper than the nearest point. The ampersand moves 1.42 units toward ACTION, the wordmark keeps its width to within 0.1 unit, and both sides measure 0.205x.

I recommend 2b, labeled as a design judgment, with the V6 rule written as: equal white on both sides of the ampersand, measured as area across the cap band to a depth of 0.15x, with no ink closer than 0.07x.

Item 3 Option Recommended

Wordmark masters for display, 24 px and 16 px

V5 draws one wordmark and scales it, so the 16 px logo carries display spacing: T to H and T to I clog, I to O to N runs dark, and no stem lands on a whole pixel. This proposal draws three masters from the same letters.

The display master is option 2b plus two spacing changes: I to O and O to N open by 2.4 units (their white area rises from 0.140x to 0.164x), and the T joins open by 1.4 units. It is 978.41 units wide against V5's 970.71.

The 24 px master (222 by 24 px) has a 22 px cap height, 5 px stems and 4 px bars, all on whole pixels; each letter pair gets the same white area, the T arms are 1.5 units shorter and nothing comes closer than 2 px. The 16 px master (153 by 16 px) has a 14 px cap height, 3 px stems and bars, T arms 4 units shorter, and the 16 px master of the recommended ampersand, B. The same masters with ampersand A are on item 4, as the fallback. At 16 and 24 px the ampersand fills the full box height, 1.14 and 1.09 times the cap height, so the red keeps its weight beside Charcoal capitals. That is an optical-size change to V5's rule that the glyph matches the cap height, and it needs Dustin's approval with the rest.

Solid share of ink pixels, one color on Newsprint: original 0.56 at 16 px and 0.74 at 24 px; proposal 0.67 and 0.78.

Item 4 Option Fallback if B is declined

Ampersand option A: an optical tune and a true small master

Option A keeps the Lora-derived glyph Dustin knows and tunes it.

The display tune grows the outline with an elliptical pen, 7.5 font units up and down and 3 across, so the thin horizontals gain more than the stems: hairlines go from 70 to 85 font units and the contrast between thick and thin falls from 1.9 to about 1.7. A vertical warp then sets the overshoot to the wordmark's own, 1.34 units above the cap line and below the baseline, and keeps the tail flat on the baseline. V5 measured 0.24 units above and 2.24 below.

The 16 px master is a separate drawing, 15 by 16 px, made over the tuned outline on the pixel grid. Hairlines are 2 px (V5: 1.45 px), the bowl stem 3 px, the diagonal 2.6 px measured square to its direction, and the upper counter 3 px wide. Every horizontal and vertical edge sits on a whole pixel, so the red pixels come out solid.

Item 5 Option Recommended direction

Ampersand option B: a drawn ampersand of Oath & Action's own

Option B is a drawn ampersand. It keeps the silhouette a reader knows (a loop over a bowl, a diagonal that crosses to a tail, an arm with a serif) and makes four decisions of its own.

Contrast: one broad-nib axis, the bowl stem at 0.85 of the wordmark's stem (Lora's thick strokes reach 0.67 to 0.78) and hairlines of 13.7 to 14.9 units (Lora's: 9.8 to 10.2), so thick to thin is about 1.5 to 1 against Lora's 1.9. The red now carries weight beside the Charcoal capitals. Terminals: the arm ends in a flat cut with a beak serif that reaches only toward the loop, and the tail sweeps out flat and stops with a vertical cut, its foot on the baseline like the letters' feet. Crossing: the diagonal meets the arm close to square, and both strokes narrow where they cross, so the corners of the counters stay open. Counters: a taller loop counter and a fuller bowl than Lora's.

I drew it first as one pen path that crosses itself twice, the way an ampersand is written, then redrew the outline by hand as three contours of cubic Beziers with nodes on extrema, added weight with an elliptical pen and refitted it (41 nodes). A 16 px master, 14 by 16 px, is drawn on the pixel grid with the same rules as option A's.

Item 6 Option For Dustin's named decision

Wordmark width: a condensed exploration

Dustin's references set the wordmark in condensed heavy capitals with the serif red ampersand. The creative director measured them at 5.7 to 6.3 times as wide as their ink is tall; V5's wordmark is 9.5 times.

I drew the condensed letters from Inter Tight 800's own capitals, the outlines V5 already uses. Each letter is narrowed (to 0.74 of its width, the A to 0.81 so its counter stays open), its stems get back the weight the narrowing took, its bars thin by 2.2 units so stems and bars keep Inter Tight's 1.2 to 1, and the cap band is restored. The A's flat apex, the C's horizontal cuts and the N's joins stay Inter Tight's. Spacing uses the same area model as item 2. The ampersand is option B narrowed to 0.85, since the Lora glyph is 0.89 as wide as it is tall and sits wide among condensed capitals.

Two widths were tested. At 7.5 to 1 (by ink height; 7.77 cap heights wide) the A's counter stays 0.77 open at 16 px. At 6 to 1 (6.27 cap heights) it closes to 0.23. The 16 px test chooses 7.5 to 1, and only that width gets 24 and 16 px masters. In the same width as the current wordmark, the 7.5 to 1 version has 1.26 times the cap height; the 6 to 1 version would give 1.56 times.

Effect on the type system: Inter Tight stays the headline face, set in sentence case, and Inter and Lora Italic keep their roles. Condensed capitals live in the wordmark only; no condensed headline style is added. Because the letters come from Inter Tight, the wordmark and the headlines share terminals and apertures. Tokens change only for masthead sizes.

Item 7 Option Recommended: V2

The hinge diagram

V5's hinge diagram sets the wordmark under a dotted arc that ends in an open arrowhead, with UNDERSTAND and IMPACT beneath. Its arc keeps a fixed 1.25 px weight at every size (a non-scaling stroke), so it is heavy when small and faint when large, and its labels fall under 13 px below 516 px wide.

The proposal is drawn from Dustin's mockups: the ampersand sits in a circle on a line that joins OATH and ACTION, with a node at each end of each line, and two solid hairline arcs loop around it, clockwise. The upper arc runs from OATH to ACTION, the promise then the act, and the lower arc returns, so the figure reads as a cycle. Each arc ends in one open arrowhead. It keeps V5's rules: the original lettering anchors it, the labels sit under the optical centers of their words, and the ampersand is the master glyph in Pillar Red, here at 1.25 times the cap height in a circle 2.2 times the cap height.

Every stroke is drawn in units, so line weight scales with the mark. The display master (lines 3.5 units, arcs 2.8 units, labels with a 26-unit cap height) holds 13 px labels from 526 px wide, where its lines measure 1.27 px and its arcs 1.02 px. Below that, the compact master drops the labels and draws lines and arcs at 6 units, which stay at 1 px or more down to 241 px wide. Labels are Charcoal on light surfaces: Stone measures 4.29:1 on Newsprint, under the 4.5:1 that 13 px text needs.

This is V2. My first draft drew the arcs dotted. Atlas V7 draws hypotheses dotted and plausible links dashed, so a dotted or dashed brand line would read as an evidence grade once a reader learns the key. V2 draws them as solid hairlines, and no brand graphic on this board uses a dotted or dashed line. The systems board's five separation rules keep the diagram apart from data figures: words only, never inside a keyed figure, solid hairline arcs, red on the ampersand alone, and “hinge” kept as a brand word. Under the red rule the diagram's ampersand takes the view's working red, as a hinge headline's does.

Item 9 Defect fix Recommended

One set of minimum sizes

Item 10 Option A decision for Dustin

The red ampersand on Prussian

V5 sets White letters and a red ampersand on Prussian, and its own text says red there “stays at display size or goes mono”, while its color matrix shows small red ampersands. Pillar Red measures 3.19:1 on Prussian, and antialiasing pulls the small sizes under 3:1.

The red rule, as the creative director wrote it for every board: The wordmark's ampersand and the lockmark's lever are signature red and do not count. Each view (one screen, card, slide or printed page) then carries one working red, in this order of precedence: an error the reader must fix; the step a reader can take now; the one point the headline names, which in a systems figure is where a reform acts. A hinge headline's ampersand uses the view's working red. The hinge divider's ampersand is Charcoal. The Act handle is red only when the Act mark is itself the call to act, as on a social tile. Red never sits on Prussian.

Under that rule treatments A and C put red on Prussian and fall away. B and D stay. I recommend D, White, for identity marks on Prussian, the color V5 already gives the letters. Data graphics keep the token table's Newsprint.
