## 4. Three choices

### Choice 1. The systems maps

The studio's pick joins two directions. Loop Lines draws the whole system as a transit map on Prussian: each numbered loop is a line, each variable a station, and a variable shared by several loops is an interchange. It is the atlas's signature image and its 24 by 36 inch print. The Field Guide draws every loop as a smaller version of the R8 card you pointed to, on catalog pages of specimen plates. A small copy of the transit map, with the loop lit, sits on every card and catalog band, so a reader finds each loop on the poster.

Options: the pick (recommended); Loop Lines throughout; the Field Guide throughout; the current system, which stays the fallback (G1-11).

The cost: a reader meets two drawings, boxes on the cards and lines on the map. Shared names, shared line textures and the locator hold them together. The poster draws the 20 numbered loops, 25 variables and 46 connections; a note in its frame says so and points to the register for all 51 variables and 118 connections.

## 7. A question for you

**Q-13, the reader check.** Can five people who have never seen the brand look at the R8 card, the R10 card, the poster and the chosen icons, ten seconds each, and answer four questions: does the loop come back to the start, does it feed itself or push back, which step is least certain, and where does a reform act? The studio recommends running it before Gate 2. Every test so far used one agent rater and no people.

## 8. Risks

- The poster leaves out the 26 variables and 72 connections that sit on no numbered loop. The note in its frame keeps that honest, and it must never leave the print.
- Two drawing styles carry the maps. Readers may expect the cards to look like the poster; the reader check will show it.
- The catalog scores 3 on beauty. A further pass on the catalog alone can follow your choice.
- The station table and the district table need review whenever V7.1 changes a loop. The verifiers fail on any mismatch, so the risk is time.
